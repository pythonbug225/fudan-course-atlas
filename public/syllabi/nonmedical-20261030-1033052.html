课程简介（中文）

本课程介绍用于政策评估与面板数据分析的核心计量方法。上半部分涵盖处理效应（包括边际处理效应、异质部分效应与分位数处理效应），并讨论在高维数据环境下的政策评估与政策学习。下半部分聚焦面板数据计量经济学，内容包括线性面板模型、多项式/动态面板模型、面板门限回归、时变参数面板模型，并讨论横截面相关性、空间面板模型、具有交互固定效应的面板模型及高维面板数据的收缩估计方法。

课程简介（英文）

This course provides an advanced overview of econometric methods for policy evaluation and panel data analysis. It begins with fundamental concepts in treatment effect estimation, including average and marginal treatment effects, heterogeneous partial effects, and quantile treatment effects. Students will learn how these frameworks inform causal inference and policy evaluation, particularly in high-dimensional settings. The course then explores modern approaches to policy learning, emphasizing data-driven decision-making for optimal policy design. The second part of the course focuses on panel data econometrics, covering a range of models that capture individual heterogeneity and dynamic behavior. Topics include linear and heterogeneous panel data models, multinomial and dynamic panel models, panel threshold regression, and time-varying parameter models. Special attention is given to issues of cross-sectional dependence, spatial interactions, and models with interactive fixed effects. The course also introduces shrinkage estimation techniques for high-dimensional panel data, which are increasingly relevant in empirical research.

教学目标

This course aims to equip students with a comprehensive understanding of modern econometric methods for causal inference and panel data analysis. Students will learn how to estimate and interpret various forms of treatment effects—including marginal, heterogeneous, and quantile treatment effects—to evaluate policy interventions under different assumptions and data environments. The course emphasizes both theoretical foundations and practical implementation, enabling students to apply these tools to real-world economic and social policy questions. In addition, the course introduces advanced panel data techniques for analyzing individual heterogeneity, dynamics, and complex dependence structures. Topics include linear, heterogeneous, dynamic, multinomial, threshold, time-varying, and spatial panel data models, as well as interactive fixed effects and shrinkage estimation for high-dimensional data. By integrating classical econometric theory with modern machine learning–based policy learning approaches, the course provides a unified framework for data-driven policy analysis. Upon completion, students will be able to formulate research questions, conduct rigorous empirical analyses, critically evaluate quantitative models, and apply advanced analytical methods to interdisciplinary research problems. The course aims to develop students’ analytical thinking, computational skills, and capacity for evidence-based reasoning in a wide range of scientific and societal contexts.
