课程简介（中文）

本课程介绍形而上学发展史上一些最重要的趋势和争论，尤其关注其与基督教哲学的关系。课程将直接分析该领域几部最具影响力和最广为流传的文本。本课程将为学生未来在该领域的研究奠定坚实的基础，并为更广泛的基督教哲学研究提供重要的资源。 课程首先探讨理解现实的两种主要方法：“作为存在的现实”（柏拉图和古典基督教传统所表达）和“作为生成的现实”（黑格尔所暗示，并发展为过程神学）。接下来，课程将讨论基督教形而上学与科学的关系：基督教对现实的理解框架应该由科学结论（Kant、Pannenberg）还是启示的叙事结构（Jenson、von Balthasar）来构建。

课程简介（英文）

This course introduces some of the most important trends and debates in the field of metaphysics throughout its history, with a particular focus on its relation to Christian philosophy. It does this by direct analysis of several of the most influential and universally read texts in the field. The course will give students a strong foundation for future research in this subject area specifically, as well as important resources for research in Christian philosophy more generally. The course begins by discussing two major approaches to the understanding of reality: "reality as being" (expressed by the Platonic and classical Christian traditions) and "reality as becoming" (intimated by Hegel and developed into process theology). Next, the course discusses the relation of Christian metaphysics to science: whether the framework for the Christian understanding of reality should be shaped by scientific conclusions (Kant, Pannenberg) or the narrative structure by which revelation is disclosed (Jenson, von Balthasar).

教学目标

Familiarize students with important trends and areas of study in the field of metaphysics relating to Christian philosophy. Give students confidence using key English-language technical terms in the field of metaphysics. Demonstrate the high degree of debate within metaphysics regarding basic questions about reality and explain how these different conclusions are influenced by broader philosophical convictions. Give students awareness of how the field of metaphysics influences wider discussions within philosophy of religion. Show students the range of opinions regarding the legitimacy of metaphysics as an academic discipline and modern shifts in its methodology following recent scientific discoveries. Provide a supportive environment for students to experience reading, writing and presenting on primary sources in English language. Explain and encourage students to employ the methodologies and standards expected for international publication in the field of philosophy. Develop students’ abilities to analyze primary sources and to articulate why they do or do not find arguments convincing. Teach students to identify promising topics for discussion in article format.

教学方式

The course will be taught by weekly seminars describing and analyzing key primary texts on metaphysics throughout the history of philosophy, progressively defining four major themes within the subject: reality as being, reality as becoming, reality shaped by science, reality as narrative. Each seminar will begin with an introduction to the author discussed, presented by one of the registered students. Each seminar will end with a discussion about the text(s) covered, giving students the opportunity to raise any questions and develop critical thinking skills in a controlled environment.

教学内容与进度安排

Introduction: Explain the range of definitions of the term “metaphysics”. Discuss what it means to engage in philosophy with “integrity”. Reality as “being” 1: Outline and analyze Plato’s concept of “forms” Reality as “being” 2: Outline and analyze Aristotle’s concepts of energeia and dunamis Reality as “being” 3: Outline and analyze Thomas Aquinas’ concept of “absolute simplicity” and how it results in God’s infinity and immutability. Reality as “becoming” 1: Outline and analyze Hegel’s move from metaphysics commencing with “being” to metaphysics commencing with “becoming”. Reality as “becoming” 2: Outline and analyze Whitehead’s more thoroughgoing approach to reality defined by process in his “philosophy of organism”. Reality as “becoming” 3: Outline and analyze Cobb’s formulation of Whitehead’s process metaphysics into an explicitly Christian framework. Reality as “becoming” 4: Outline and analyze Keller’s feminist approach to reality as becoming via the concept of tehom. “Scientific” metaphysics 1: Outline and analyze Kant’s attempt to limit the scope of metaphysics using the principle of scientific empiricism. “Scientific” metaphysics 2: Outline and analyze Kuhn’s discussion of science’s role shaping cultural understandings of reality. “Scientific” metaphysics 3: Outline and analyze Pannenberg’s attempt to reformulate the concept of Spirit in modern scientific terms of a “field of force”. Reality as “narrative” 1: Outline and analyze Milbank’s argument that religion should not be obliged to agree with scientific conclusions about metaphysics. Reality as “narrative” 2: Outline and analyze Robert Jenson’s reformulation of reality in terms of narrative in order to ensure to integrity of christological revelation. Reality as “narrative” 3: Outline and analyze von Balthasar’s attempt to combine a narrative-centred theology with classical commitments to God as being. Discussion 1: Briefly summarize the key content of the course and guide classroom debates regarding the most convincing approaches to metaphysics. Discussion 2: Registered students introduce their essays and answer questions from the class in a conference format in preparation for final submission.
