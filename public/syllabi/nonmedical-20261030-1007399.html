来源：复旦教务系统教学大纲；正文整理。

课程简介（中文）

《人工智能设计与应用》课程面向研究生，系统介绍人工智能在设计与软件开发中的原理与实践。内容涵盖 Transformer、Diffusion 等核心模型，多模态与心理学导向的 AI-Agent，以及 AI 推理扩展、BitNet 加速器和无存储计算等前沿议题。课程采取讲授与实践结合的方式，学生需亲手实现简化模型，学习调用主流 API，并完成综合项目。

课程简介（英文）

Artificial Intelligence Design and Application is a graduate-level course that systematically introduces the principles and practices of AI in design and software development. The content covers core models such as Transformers and Diffusion, multimodal and psychology-oriented AI agents, as well as advanced topics including AI inference scaling, BitNet accelerators, and no-memory computing. The course combines lectures with hands-on practice, requiring students to implement simplified models, learn to call mainstream APIs, and complete a comprehensive project.

教学目标

Understand the concepts and development trends of AI design, master the fundamental principles of core models such as Transformers and Diffusion, and gain knowledge of frontier topics including AI inference scaling, BitNet accelerators, and no-memory computing. Acquire the ability to implement simplified models and utilize mainstream AI APIs, and be capable of designing and applying AI agents in image, video, and multimodal scenarios. Cultivate interdisciplinary innovative thinking, enhance problem-solving and teamwork skills, and complete a comprehensive project that bridges theory and practice.

教学方式

This course adopts a combined approach of lectures, hands-on practice, and discussion. Lectures are used to systematically introduce the core principles and frontier developments of artificial intelligence. Laboratory and practice sessions require students to implement simplified models, call APIs, and design small-scale applications. Case studies and group discussions help students understand the value of AI in multimodal and psychology-oriented scenarios. Finally, a comprehensive project trains students in interdisciplinary collaboration and innovation, completing a full learning cycle from theory to practice.

教学内容与进度安排

Week 1 (2h) – Course Introduction & Foundations Content: Overview of artificial intelligence in design and software development; historical development of AI and its integration with creative industries; introduction to course requirements, evaluation methods, and expected outcomes. Learning Objectives: Familiarize with the scope of AI in design and understand course expectations. Assignment: Short reflection (1 page) on “The role of AI in design and creativity.” Week 2 (2h) – Deep Learning Refresher Content: Key concepts in neural networks, backpropagation, loss functions, and optimization; review of convolutional and recurrent networks; limitations that motivated the shift toward new architectures. Learning Objectives: Refresh essential knowledge to prepare for advanced AI topics. Week 3 (2h) – Transformer Architecture I Content: From sequence-to-sequence models to attention-based architectures; motivation for Transformers; introduction to attention mechanism and positional encoding. Learning Objectives: Explain why attention mechanisms replaced RNNs and how Transformers revolutionized NLP. Week 4 (2h) – Transformer Architecture II Content: Encoder-decoder structures; multi-head self-attention; residual connections and normalization layers; training challenges and solutions. Learning Objectives: Understand the inner workings of a Transformer and its scalability. Assignment: Implement a simplified self-attention module in Python. Week 5 (2h) – Applications of Transformers Content: Case studies of Transformers in natural language processing, image classification, and design-related applications; introduction to BERT, GPT, and Vision Transformers. Learning Objectives: Analyze how Transformer-based systems are used across disciplines. Assignment: Group report analyzing one real-world Transformer application. Week 6 (2h) – Diffusion Models I Content: Basic intuition of probabilistic generative models; introduction to denoising diffusion process; forward and reverse diffusion; training objectives. Learning Objectives: Grasp the theoretical foundation of diffusion-based generative models. Week 7 (2h) – Diffusion Models II Content: Applications of diffusion in image, video, and audio generation; comparison with GANs and VAEs; evaluation of generative quality. Learning Objectives: Understand the strengths of diffusion models and their creative potential. Assignment: Experiment with a diffusion model API to generate images. Week 8 (2h) – AI Agents: Multimodal Systems Content: Introduction to agent-based frameworks; integration of text, vision, and audio; examples of image- and video-driven agents in design workflows. Learning Objectives: Identify design opportunities where multimodal agents can enhance creative practice. Week 9 (2h) – Psychology-Oriented AI Agents Content: Principles of human-computer interaction; affective computing; psychological models of perception and behavior; examples of conversational and adaptive agents. Learning Objectives: Recognize how psychological insights improve AI-agent design for user engagement. Assignment: Reading summary on affective AI and human-AI interaction. Week 10 (2h) – AI Inference Scaling Content: Scaling laws in AI; methods for improving inference speed; trade-offs between model size, latency, and energy efficiency; distributed inference strategies. Learning Objectives: Understand practical challenges in deploying large-scale AI models. Week 11 (2h) – BitNet Accelerator Architectures Content: Overview of hardware accelerators for AI; design of BitNet architecture; examples of efficient computation for large models; implications for AI in design contexts. Learning Objectives: Gain insight into how hardware influences the usability of AI applications. Assignment: Technical note (2 pages) summarizing one AI accelerator technology. Week 12 (2h) – No-Memory Computing Discussion Content: Exploration of “memoryless” computing concepts; emerging paradigms in neuromorphic hardware and alternative computing systems; critical perspectives on feasibility. Learning Objectives: Engage with debates on the future of AI system design. Week 13 (2h) – Case Study Seminar Content: Analysis of real-world projects applying AI to design, media, and human interaction; discussion of successes and limitations. Learning Objectives: Evaluate interdisciplinary applications and reflect on practical impact. Assignment: TBD Week 14 (2h) – Project Proposal Presentations Content: Student groups present project ideas, methodology, and expected outcomes; peer and instructor feedback. Learning Objectives: Develop clear project scope and practice academic presentation skills. Assessment: Proposal presentation Week 15 (4h) – Project Development & Mentoring Content: In-class project workshop; group mentoring; integration of technical and design knowledge; emphasis on interdisciplinary problem-solving. Learning Objectives: Apply course concepts in a practical setting with guidance. Assignment: TBD Week 16 (4h) – Final Project Presentations & Course Wrap-up Content: Group presentations and demonstrations; peer review and discussion; course reflection and synthesis. Learning Objectives: Demonstrate interdisciplinary innovation and summarize key learning outcomes. Assessment: Final project report and defense
