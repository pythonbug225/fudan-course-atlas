课程简介（中文）

Topics in Advanced Macroeconomics I Fall 2025 Course Website: https://xuzhiwei09.wixsite.com/econ/macrotopics Time: Week 1-16, 15:25-17:05, Wednesday Venue: H5409 Instructor: Zhiwei Xu Email: [联系方式已隐去] Office Hours: Wednesday, 13:00-14:00, Room 719, School of Economics Course Description: This is a PHD-level topic course in Macroeconomics. The course is designed for those Ph.D. students who start to prepare their doctoral thesis. The main content of the course focuses on the aggregate implications of individual decisions with Chinese characteristics. The course aims to help the students understand the underlying mechanism behind the important phenomena in China by using rigorous analytical tools. The topics in this course will cover macroeconomics of digital economy, banking, and government behaviors. In each topic, I will first introduce a baseline toy model that illustrates the essential mechanism. Then, I will extend the toy model to a full-fledged dynamic general equilibrium model and conduct relevant quantitative analysis. I will also talk about some applications to the Chinese economy. The course is self-contained regarding the macroeconomics. Students need to have basic knowledge about advanced macroeconomics and econometrics. After taking this course, the students are expected to be able to apply modern macroeconomic tools to analyze the phenomenon in reality. Prerequisites: Advanced Marco I and Advanced Micro I from the Ph.D. fundamental course sequence Requirements: (Subject to Changes) The course has 3 take-home assignments and a research proposal. Textbooks There is no required textbook, all of below are optional: Veldkamp, Laura. Information Choice in Macroeconomics and Finance. Princeton University Press. Jianjun Miao, Economic Dynamics in Discrete Time. DYNARE User Guide. Class handouts and other reading materials. 教学目标

This is a PHD-level topic course in Macroeconomics. The course is designed for those Ph.D. students who start to prepare their doctoral thesis. The main content of the course focuses on the aggregate implications of individual decisions with Chinese characteristics. The course aims to help the students understand the underlying mechanism behind the important phenomena in China by using rigorous analytical tools. The topics in this course will cover macroeconomics of digital economy, banking, and government behaviors. In each topic, I will first introduce a baseline toy model that illustrates the essential mechanism. Then, I will extend the toy model to a full-fledged dynamic general equilibrium model and conduct relevant quantitative analysis. I will also talk about some applications to the Chinese economy. The course is self-contained regarding the macroeconomics. Students need to have basic knowledge about advanced macroeconomics and econometrics. After taking this course, the students are expected to be able to apply modern macroeconomic tools to analyze the phenomenon in reality.
