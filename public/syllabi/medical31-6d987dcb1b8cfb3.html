课程简介（英文） Course Description ( in English ) | Internal medicine(II) is the medical specialty dealing with the diagnosis, treatment and prevention of diseases in adult. It is a fundamental curriculum of clinical disciplines and the education of internists and about research. The course is composed of： Module 1 Disorders of the Kidney and Urinary Tract Module 2 Disorders of hemotologic System Module 3 Endocrinology and Metabolism Module 4 Disorders of the Immune System, Connective Tissue, and Joints Module 5 Poisoning and Diseases of Critical Care Medicine

4.教学内容及进度安排 /Course Content & Schedule （ 按照教学周编排，应包含每节课教学内容及教学目标，如有作业及考核要求，请一并列明）

Course Requirements: Finish the theoretical lectures and practical. Finish the presentation homework. Attendance requirements: Unexcused absences for more than 1/3 of the class hours are not allowed to participate in the course examination. Disorders of the Kidney and Urinary Tract (Week 1-4) Adaptation to Renal Injury To master the kidney adaption to nephron loss. Glomerulonephropathies To master Approach to patient with suspected glomerular diseases. Clinical and pathological clssification of primary glomerular diseases. Pathogenesis, pathological and clinical presentation, diagnosis and treatment of acute poststreptococcal glomerulonephritis, IgA nephropathy and diabetic nephropathy. To be familiar with Common secondary glomerular diseases: diabetic nephropathy, small vessel vasculitis, lupus nephritis Renal pathology Urinary Tract Infections To master the typical clinical manifestations, diagnosis, differential diagnosis and therapeutic principles of acute and chronic pyelonephritis. To grasp the etiology, contagious ways and infection-relative factors of pyelonephritis. To grasp the main laboratory diagnostic methods of pyelonephritis. Acute kidney injury To master the RIFLE and KDIGO criteria of AKI classification and AKI staging. To be familiar with 1) common causes of AKI; 2) clinical approach to the diagnosis of AKI; 3) Clinical indications for emergent dialysis. To know the basic concepts and indications of continuous renal replacement therapy. Chronic kidney disease To master the definition and stage of CKD, clinical manifestations and treatment of CKD. To be familiar with epidemiology of CKD, causes and pathophysiology of CKD. To be familiar with CKD assessment and management（KDIGO guideline） Fluid and Electrolyte Disturbances & Acidosis and Alkalosis To master the main etiology, pathogenesis, clinical manifestations, diagnosis and principle of therapy of these clinical cases: inadequate volume, hypernatremia, hyponatremia, hyperkalemia, hypokalemia, metabolic acidosis and metabolic alkalosis. To master the methods to calculate the dosage of intravenous pharmaceutical applied in correcting the disorder of water and electrolytes. To grasp the main cause, pathogenesis, clinical manifestations, diagnostic and therapeutic principles of these clinical cases: water excess, water intoxication, respiratory acidosis and respiratory alkalosis. To understand the pathogenesis and diagnosis of mixed acid-base disorder. Disorders of hemotologic System (Week 4-7) Anemias To master basic information of anemia, including iron deficiency anemia, aplastic anemia, and hemolytic anemia. To be familiar with etiology, clinical features, laboratory findings, diagnosis and treatment of every kind of anemia described above. Acute and Chronic Myeloid Leukemia To master the clinical presentation, the diagnosis and the general rules of treatment of leukemia To be familiar with the classification of leukemia and chemotherapy To understand the etiology of leukemia Malignancies of Lymphoid Cells To master the definition, diagnosis and Ann Arbor Staging System of lymphoma. To be familiar with International Prognostic Index and treatment strategy for lymphoma. Multiple Myeloma To master the clinical manifestations, diagnosis of multiple myeloma. To be familiar with the classification, staging and treatment principles of multiple myeloma. To understand the etiology, pathogenesis and prognosis of multiple myeloma. Disorders of Coagulation and Thrombosis To master: The coagulation cascade has two pathways which lead to fibrin formation; Factors VIII and IX deficiency; Disseminated intravascular coagulation (DIC); Platelet disorders ,Immune Thrombocytopenic (ITP); Clinical Tests of coagulation and thrombosis. To be familiar with: Venous and arterial thrombosis and embolism; Thrombotic thrombocytopenia purpura (TTP); Procoagulants and Anticoagulants. Endocrinology and Metabolism (Week 7-11) Principles of Endocrinology To master the definition and classification of hormone; the principle of hypothalamus - pituitary - hormone axis; the principle of the diagnosis of endocrine diseases. To be familiar with the mechanisms of hormone action; the classification of endocrine diseases; the principle of the treatment of endocrine diseases. Diseases of Thyroid Gland To master the etiology classification, clinical manifestation, laboratory evaluation, diagnosis and differential diagnosis and treatment of hyperthyroidism and hypothyroidism To be familiar with Basic knowledge about the thyroid gland The overview of the thyroid disorders Other thyroid disease such as subacute thyroiditis, thyroid nodular Disorders of the Adrenal Cortex To master the etiological classification and clinical manifestation of Cushing’s syndrome and primary aldosteronism, and the main functional and localization diagnostic tests of both diseases. To be familiar with the differential diagnosis and treatment principles of Cushing’s syndrome and primary aldosteronism. Diabetes Mellitus To master the diagnosis of diabetes; the different types of diabetes and the treatment of diabetes. To be familiar with the diagnosis and treatment of DKA and the pathophysiology and mechanism of diabetes and related complications. Disorders of the Immune System, Connective Tissue, and Joints (Week 12-14) Principles of Rheumatology To master the definition and core contents of rheumatic diseases. To be familiar with common symptoms and signs of rheumatic diseases; classification and clinical significance of autoantibodies related to rheumatic diseases; commonly used imaging modalities (including X-ray, CT, MRI, ultrasound) and their application value in the diagnosis of rheumatic diseases; main treatment alternatives (pharmacological therapy, physical therapy, surgical intervention, etc.) and their clinical indications. Systemic Lupus Erythematosus (SLE) To master: Systemic clinical manifestations (involving skin and mucous membranes, joints, kidneys, cardiovascular system, respiratory system, nervous system, hematological system, etc.). Key laboratory examinations, with special emphasis on immune disorders (autoantibodies such as ANA, anti-dsDNA, anti-Sm, anti-SSA/Ro, anti-SSB/La; complement C3, C4 levels; immune complex detection, etc.). International diagnostic criteria (e.g., ACR/EULAR classification criteria) and clinical diagnostic principles. Disease activity indices (e.g., SLEDAI-2K) and evaluation methods. Indices suggesting chronic organ damage (e.g., renal scarring, pulmonary fibrosis, cardiovascular complications, etc.). Overall therapeutic principles (including patient education, lifestyle management, treatment goals) and individualized pharmaceutical therapy (glucocorticoids, antimalarials, conventional synthetic DMARDs, biologic DMARDs, targeted synthetic DMARDs, etc.), as well as dosage adjustment, adverse reaction monitoring, and long-term management. To understand: Etiological factors (genetic factors, environmental triggers such as infections, ultraviolet radiation, drugs; hormonal factors, etc.) and pathological mechanisms (immune complex deposition, abnormal activation of B cells and T cells, defective immune regulation, etc.) Rheumatoid Arthritis To master: 1.Typical clinical features: Symmetrical polyarthritis (involving small joints such as hands, wrists, feet; joint swelling, pain, tenderness, stiffness, functional impairment); extra-articular manifestations (rheumatoid nodules, interstitial lung disease, cardiovascular involvement, hematological abnormalities, etc.). 2.Pathologic characteristics: Synovitis (synovial hyperplasia, inflammatory cell infiltration), pannus formation, cartilage and bone destruction, joint deformity. 3.Key autoantibodies: Rheumatoid factor (RF), anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide (anti-CCP) antibodies, and their clinical significance (diagnostic value, prognostic implication). To be familiar with: 1.Updated international diagnosis criteria (e.g., 2010 ACR/EULAR classification criteria) and differential diagnosis (distinguishing from osteoarthritis, psoriatic arthritis, gout, SLE, etc.). 2.Disease activity evaluation tools (e.g., DAS28, CDAI, SDAI) and assessment of functional status (e.g., HAQ-DI). 3.Comprehensive treatment alternatives: Non-pharmacological therapy (patient education, physical therapy, occupational therapy, exercise training); pharmacological therapy (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, glucocorticoids, conventional synthetic DMARDs, biologic DMARDs, targeted synthetic DMARDs); surgical treatment (joint replacement, synovectomy, etc.) for advanced cases Spondyloarthritides To master: 1.Classification of seronegative spondyloarthropathies: Including ankylosing spondylitis (AS), psoriatic arthritis (PsA), reactive arthritis (ReA), enteropathic arthritis (EA), undifferentiated spondyloarthropathy, etc. 2.Common characteristics of the group: Seronegativity for RF and anti-CCP antibodies; association with HLA-B27; predilection for axial skeleton (sacroiliac joints, spine) and peripheral joints; extra-articular manifestations (uveitis, psoriasis, inflammatory bowel disease, enthesitis, etc.). 3.Comparison of clinical characteristics among different subtypes: Differences in age of onset, joint involvement patterns (axial vs. peripheral), extra-articular manifestations, and prognosis. For Ankylosing Spondylitis (AS) specifically: To master: 1.Clinical manifestations: Inflammatory low back pain (insidious onset, morning stiffness >1 hour, improvement with activity), sacroiliac joint pain, spinal stiffness and fusion, peripheral arthritis, enthesitis (e.g., Achilles tendinitis, plantar fasciitis), uveitis, etc. 2.Laboratory examinations: HLA-B27 typing, inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP), and exclusion of other diseases. 3.Pathological features: Sacroiliitis (synovitis, cartilage destruction, bone erosion, bony fusion), spondylitis, enthesitis. 4.Therapeutic strategies: Non-pharmacological therapy (patient education, regular exercise, posture management); pharmacological therapy (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs as first-line, DMARDs for peripheral arthritis, biologic DMARDs targeting TNF, IL-17, etc.); surgical intervention for severe spinal deformity or joint damage. Hyperuricemia and Gout To master: 1.Definition: Hyperuricemia (normal uric acid levels, classification of hyperuricemia: primary vs. secondary); gout (definition as a disease caused by monosodium urate crystal deposition, classification: primary vs. secondary). 2.Clinical manifestations of gout: Acute gouty arthritis; intercritical gou; chronic tophaceous gout; gouty nephropathy. To be familiar with: 1.Imaging modalities: X-ray findings; ultrasound (double contour sign, tophi, synovitis, tenosynovitis); dual-energy CT (detection of urate crystals) and their diagnostic value. 2.Diagnosis criteria and key points of differential diagnosis. 3.Treatment principles and methods: Management of hyperuricemia: Lifestyle modification (dietary control, limiting alcohol and high-fructose beverages, weight loss, adequate hydration); urate-lowering therapy (xanthine oxidase inhibitors, uricosuric agents, uricase inhibitors) and target uric acid levels. Treatment of acute gouty arthritis: Anti-inflammatory drugs (non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs, colchicine, glucocorticoids) and their usage, dosage, and contraindications. Management of chronic gout: Tophus treatment, prevention of acute attacks, protection of renal function. Poisoning and Diseases of Critical Care Medicine (Week 14-16) Poisoning And Drug Overdosage To master the pathogenesis of common poisons, including the molecular mechanisms of toxic effects on target organs, metabolic disorders induced by poisoning, and pathological changes. To master the clinical manifestations and diagnostic principles of acute and chronic poisoning: recognize typical symptoms and signs of different poison exposures, master the steps of clinical diagnosis (history collection, physical examination, laboratory tests), and differentiate poisoning from other similar diseases. To master the treatment principles of poisoning: proficiently apply general treatment measures (decontamination, toxin elimination, supportive care) and specific antidote therapies and formulate individualized treatment plans for common poisoning cases. To understand the specific properties, exposure situations, absorption, and metabolism of common poisons: including chemical and physical properties of poisons (e.g., volatility, solubility), common exposure routes (oral, inhalation, dermal, injection), processes of absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion in the human body, and factors affecting toxic effects. Principles of Critical Care Medicine To master the major tasks of critical care medicine To be familiar with the difference between ICU and other wards Shock To master the definitions and categorization of shock. To be familiar with the pathogenesis and pathophysiology of shock. To be familiar with the initial support in shock.
