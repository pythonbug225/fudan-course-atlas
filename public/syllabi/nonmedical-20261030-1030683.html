课程简介（中文）

***本课程全程采用英语授课与考核。*** 在第一学期所介绍的学术写作基本原则基础上，本课程旨在培养学生于数字时代进行学术研究所必需的技能。课程重点在于指导学生撰写适用于英语文学研究与英语语言学领域的、强有力的、基于证据的学术论证。教学内容涵盖学术研究的基本原则与伦理、选定合适的研究主题、形成研究问题、开展有效的文献综述，以及对二手文献进行批判性借鉴等核心议题。学术与分析性词汇训练将贯穿课程始终。作为研究过程的一部分，学生将通过小组项目展示来积累实践经验。

课程简介（英文）

Building on the basic principles of academic writing introduced in Semester 1, this course teaches students the skills necessary for conducting academic research in the digital age. It focuses on the writing of strong, evidence-based arguments appropriate for the fields of English Literary Studies and English Linguistics. The course covers fundamental topics including the principles and ethics of academic research, identifying an appropriate subject, formulating a research question, conducting an effective literature review, and engaging critically with secondary sources. Academic and analytical vocabulary training is integrated throughout the course. Students gain experience in presenting small group projects as part of the research process.

教学目标

This course focuses on the core academic skills necessary for university-level study and research, in addition to continuing the development of writing skills. An important feature of the course is training on how find reliable information and evaluate the differences between popular and scholarly sources. Students will become familiar with accessing the digital resources through Fudan University Library. In addition, students will learn how to quote, paraphrase, summarize, and reference appropriately. Key features of the course include “Digital Research” exercises in which the responsible use of AI as a research collaborator (not as a text generator) is taught, the integration of “Writing Unplugged” moments that encourage students hone their writing and cognitive skills by composing short pieces without the aid of electronic devices, and reflective practices through which students develop their metacognition and, hence, their ability to manage their own learning.

教学方式

The course is designed to develop critical skills, with an emphasis on the organization and structure the research and writing processes. Each fundamental skill is practiced multiple times through in-class and homework assignments. The classes are dynamic, continually involving students in the of process of their own learning and utilizing both traditional and digital modes of teaching, learning and assessment. The course comprises lectures, discussions, in-class exercises, homework assignments, group work, and AI-driven exercises. The course scaffolds the processes of writing and researching by starting with core ideas regarding appropriate sources and referencing, before moving on to using sources to defend and critique existing arguments, and build new ones. The final stage involves the addition of the student’s own analysis and research into the argument (research-based writing).

教学内容与进度安排

Unit 1: Principles and Procedures in Academic Research Week 1: Understanding Academic Research and Writing This initial class acquaints students with the aims and contents of the course, as well as the methods of assessment, and introduces research-based writing as a process. Learning outcomes: Students will understand the requirements of the course and what they need to do in order to complete it successfully. Students will understand research-based writing as a process containing multiple steps. Writing Unplugged: Diagnosis 101 Week 2: Conducting a Systemic Literature Review I This first class on conducting a literature review discusses different kinds of scholarly and popular sources. Fudan University Library resources are introduced through a practical tutorial. Learning outcomes: Students will be able to distinguish between academic/scholarly and popular sources of information, and locate sources through Fudan University Library resources. Week 3: Managing References and Citations (MLA and APA) This class introduces students to academic referencing, focusing on MLA & APA styles. The class will consider the different parts of a reference and where to find the relevant information. It will explain why referencing is important and give practical guidance on formatting in-text citations and bibliographical entries. Learning outcomes: Students will understand why referencing in academic work is important and have learned the basic principles of referencing in MLA and APA styles. They will also have learned where to look online for reliable help. Writing Unplugged: A Citation Puzzle Week 4: Conducting a Systemic Literature Review II This class explains what an annotated bibliography is, why we use them in research, and how to compile one. Learning outcomes: Students will be able to distinguish between a regular bibliography (reference list) and an annotated bibliography. They will begin to compile their own annotated bibliography for Assignment 1. Digital Research: AI Annotation Assistant Unit 2: Source-Based Writing Week 5: Integrating Sources: Summarizing Summarizing is a core task for the academic writer. In this class, students will learn what summarizing is, learn what makes a good summary, and practice writing their own summaries using specific vocabulary. This class will also discuss summarizing and referencing as a primary method of avoiding plagiarism. Learning outcomes: Students will be able to summarize simple passages and understand what academic plagiarism is. Writing Unplugged: The Summary Week 6: Integrating Sources: Paraphrasing and Quoting This class teaches students about paraphrasing and quoting, enabling them to understand what makes a good paraphrase or quotation. Students will complete exercises to practice both and learn vocabulary that helps the process. Learning outcomes: Students learn how to paraphrase and quote, and the necessity of including in-text citations. Digital Research: Paraphrase Pitfalls Week 7: Finding Your Own Voice in Academic Arguments (in-class writing tasks) This class will teach students to distinguish between what they say and what others have said, in addition to providing them with the vocabulary to interpret evidence and data. Students will learn to express their own scholarly opinions in an academic manner. Learning outcomes: Students will learn new vocabulary that helps them express agreement, disagreement, or both. Students will be able to write simple argumentative sentences using an appropriate academic register. Writing Unplugged: The Argument Salon Digital Research: On the other hand … (AI as an opposing voice) Unit 3: Research-Based Writing Week 8: Different Types of Research This class introduces students to some of the different kinds of research that take place in the humanities and social sciences, looking at different methods of data collection and analysis. The class is supported by the introduction of appropriate vocabulary. Learning outcomes: Students will have thought about what “data” is and can be in the disciplines traditionally represented by English Studies and have learned that there are many research methodologies. Week 9: The Literary Detective: Project Introduction and Workshop This class will introduce students to basic literary analysis specifically to prepare them for writing a group presentation. Learning outcomes: Students will move beyond simply summarizing to analyze how narrative structure and character foils create meaning and tension. They will practice integrating brief, purposeful quotations from a literary text (Sherlock Holmes short stories) to support a claim and generate an analytical thesis statement for a literary argument. Week 10: Storyboarding and Scripting Presentations This session introduces students to the language of writing speeches and presentations, focusing on practical considerations and basic rhetoric. Learning outcomes: Students will understand that using rhetorical devices can enhance the persuasion of their argument and that writing is an integral part of good presentations. Writing Unplugged: Storyboard and Script Week 11: The Literary Detective: Project Presentation & Writing Conference I During this class, students will present in small groups the short presentations that they have prepared. The presentations must include references and PowerPoint slides, and demonstrate appropriate vocabulary. Presentations must be scripted. There will be time at the end of the class for students to reflect on and discuss their own performances. Learning outcomes: Students will have learned to work together to prepare and present a small-scale piece of research, a process on which they can build on for the next assignment. Week 12: The Literary Detective: Project Presentation & Writing Conference II During this class, students will present in small groups the short presentations that they have prepared. The presentations must include references and PowerPoint slides, and demonstrate appropriate vocabulary. Presentations must be scripted. There will be time at the end of the class for students to reflect on and discuss their own performances. Learning outcomes: Students will have learned to work together to prepare and present a small-scale piece of research, a process on which they can build on for the next assignment. Week 13: The Linguistic Detective: Project Introduction and Workshop This session will teach students how to analyze a text for linguistic features that reveal historical and cultural context. It will give students the opportunity to prepare a small-scale research project based on their own textual analysis and draw on previous published research. Assignment 2 Due. Learning outcomes: Students will transfer their knowledge of planning essays to writing academic presentations, integrating multiple viewpoints from published (and referenced) works. They will also develop the text-critical skill through the analysis of a type of English with which they are less familiar. Week 14: The Linguistic Detective: Project Presentation & Writing Conference I During this class, students will present in small groups the short presentations that they have prepared. The presentations must include references and PowerPoint slides, and demonstrate appropriate vocabulary. Presentations must be scripted. There will be time at the end of the class for students to reflect on and discuss their own performances. Learning outcomes: Students will have learned to work together to prepare and present a small-scale piece of research that builds on the strengths and acknowledges the weakness of their previous presentation. Specific requirements of the presentation format force students to focus on structure and the logical organization of their content and argument. Week 16: Course Review and Revision This reflective session seeks to engage students with their own learning trajectory by considering what they have achieved throughout the course, but also identify areas that need further development. This class will also dedicate time to explaining the final exam format and revision of some key topics. Learning outcomes: Students will learn about the exam format, meaning they will go into the exam confidently. They will also learn out their own leaning by reflecting on the semester’s progress and what they found difficult.
