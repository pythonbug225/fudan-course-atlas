课程简介（英文）

This class is a pure mathematics class surveying a topic in model theory: NIP fields. Introductory model theory is a prerequisite. Some algebra and topology will be needed. Historically, the class of stable structures played a central role in model theory. Stability arises from classification theory--the problem of classifying the models of a first-order theory. Stability has applications to differential algebra and arithmetic geometry. In recent years, model theory has considered generalizations of stability, such as NIP. The class of NIP structures contains both the stable structures and o-minimal structures, as well as many "real-world" structures from pure mathematics (e.g. the field of p-adic numbers). NIP is related to VC dimension in statistics. It's natural to ask which fields (in the sense of algebra) are stable or NIP. The stable fields conjecture gives a conjectural classification of stable fields, and the Shelah conjecture does the same for NIP fields.

教学目标

In this course, students will learn the following: What stability and NIP are Why model theorists care about stability and NIP What some of the applications of stability and NIP are Which common structures are stable/NIP Which fields are known to be stable/NIP Why we conjecture that no other stable/NIP fields exist Which cases of these conjectures are known The class will be a survey of research results, and many proofs will be omitted. Priority will go to proofs related to the classification of dp-finite fields. Students who take this class will be exposed to a wide range of advanced topics in model theory, including some cutting-edge research. This class can serve as a basis to prepare students for research in model theory, especially the model theory of fields. Through the final project, students will gain practice in reading the mathematical literature and learning new mathematical ideas.

教学方式

The class will consist of a series of lectures. The lectures will use slides (i.e., PPTs) to state the results, accompanied with proofs on a blackboard. There will be no textbook or class notes, but the slides will be made available, as well as "Further Reading" documents with references to the literature. Students will practice the skills they have learned in weekly homework assignments. If time permits, students will occasionally present their solutions to homework. There may also be review sessions and ungraded quizzes to assess student understanding and give students practice with digesting the new concepts. Class attendance is expected, and will impact grades. At the end of the semester, each student will write a written report (15-25 pages) on a certain topic, and give a short oral presentation on the topic. A list of possible topics will be provided at the start of the semester, and students will choose topics from this list on a first-come-first-served basis. These topics will be adjacent to the material covered in class. There will be no exams in the class. The final assessment will be the written report.

教学内容与进度安排

(schedule is subject to change) Week 1: Stable theories Stability is a natural concept in model theory, some important structures are stable, and stability has strong consequences. Week 2: Stable fields Finite fields and separably closed fields are stable. We conjecture there are no others. This is known in certain cases. Week 3: NIP theories NIP is a natural concept in model theory, some important structures are NIP, and NIP has strong consequences. Weeks 4-5: Valued fields Valued fields are an important concept in number theory and topological fields. They give new examples of NIP fields. Week 6: The exchange property and t-minimality We can define the "dimension" of any definable set, and prove generic continuity of definable functions, under some technical assumptions. Week 7: Forking, superstability, and Lascar rank In stable structures, there's a natural notion of "independence" which can be used to define another notion of "dimension" for definable sets. This is tied to the important class of superstable structures. Week 8: Dp-rank and strong dependence Dp-rank is yet another notion of "dimension" in NIP structures. Many important NIP structures are dp-minimal, meaning they have dp-rank 1. Week 9: The conjectures on NIP fields Two central conjectures link NIP fields to henselian valued fields. Week 10: NIP fields of positive characteristic NIP fields of positive characteristic are Galois-theoretically constrained. Using this, the henselianity conjecture holds in positive characteristic. Week 11: The conjectural classification The Shelah conjecture implies a full classification of NIP fields. Week 12: Dp-minimal fields Dp-minimal fields are t-minimal. From this, one can prove the conjectures and classify dp-minimal fields. Week 13: W-topologies A mysterious new class of topological fields arises when studying dp-finite fields. Week 14: Dp-finite fields The classification of dp-minimal fields extends to dp-finite fields. Weeks 15-16: Student presentations
