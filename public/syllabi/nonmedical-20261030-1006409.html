课程简介（中文）

本课程为法学院法学硕士开设英语诗歌专题。精选雪莱《西风颂》、济慈《希腊古瓮颂》等短篇名篇，兼顾英美诗歌史与世界精品，文本具思想性与音乐性。以文本细读解析语言节奏、意象哲学，在审美教育中融入价值引领。针对法学背景，诗歌鉴赏培养的文本分析能力可迁移至法律条文解读，实现人文素养与专业技能双向提升。

课程简介（英文）

Welcome to "Selected Readings in English Poetry," a journey through some of the most powerful and enduring poems in the English language. Have you ever wondered what makes a simple line of text a world-famous poem? How can a few words about a quiet wood, a red rose, or a Grecian urn resonate across centuries? In this course, we will explore these questions together. We will travel from the witty sonnets of Shakespeare and the passionate odes of the Romantics to the revolutionary free verse of Walt Whitman and the complex modernism of T.S. Eliot. We will analyze the tools of the poet—from rhythm and rhyme to metaphor and irony—to unlock the secrets behind their craft. Along the way, you will meet passionate shepherds, hear America singing, and feel the defiant power of voices like Maya Angelou’s. This is not just a study of history; it is an exploration of the human heart. Prepare to be challenged, moved, and inspired.

教学目标

本课程立足双一流高校办学定位，面向法学硕士群体，以英语诗歌为教学载体，紧扣法律专业人才复合培养需求。通过系统讲授英美诗歌发展史与经典文本艺术手法，培养学员诗歌文本细读能力，并迁移至法律条文语义解构与案例逻辑分析；在诗歌审美教育中渗透人文价值引领，借助诗歌中的伦理哲思强化学员法律职业伦理意识，助力其形成兼具文学审美素养与法学逻辑思维的复合能力，实现知识传授、专业技能培养与价值塑造的协同提升。

教学方式

本课程采用“精讲—互动—实践”三位一体的教学方式。教师依托双一流高校学术积淀，精讲英美诗歌发展史与雪莱、济慈等经典文本，解析韵律节奏、意象隐喻等艺术手法；课堂通过启发式问答引导学员参与诗歌意象解读与哲学思辨，如探讨《西风颂》中自然力量的象征意义；课后设置“背诵+分析”双轨任务，学员需完成经典诗篇记忆，并结合法学思维撰写文本分析报告，实现从语言审美到法律文本解构的能力迁移，形成“输入—内化—输出”的学习闭环。

教学内容与进度安排

Unit 1: Foundations – The Art of Persuasion and Celebration (Weeks 1-3) Week 1: The Poetic Invitation – Idealism vs. Realism Poems: Christopher Marlowe, "The Passionate Shepherd to His Love" Sir Walter Raleigh, "The Nymph's Reply to the Shepherd" Key Concepts: The Pastoral tradition, poetic dialogue, theme, idealism vs. realism, meter and rhyme. Discussion Focus: What idealized vision of love and life does the shepherd offer? How does the nymph use logic and a realistic worldview to reject his offer? Week 2: The Renaissance Lyric – Love, Wit, and Song Poems: Ben Jonson, "Song: To Celia" William Shakespeare, "Sonnet 18: Shall I compare thee to a summer’s day?" William Shakespeare, "Sonnet 29: When, in disgrace with fortune and men's eyes" Key Concepts: The Lyric poem, the Sonnet form (Shakespearean), conceit, hyperbole, tone. Discussion Focus: How do these poets use language to praise a beloved? How does the strict form of the sonnet help shape its meaning and emotional arc? Week 3: Poetry as Performance and Song Poems: William Shakespeare, "All the World’s a Stage" (from As You Like It) William Shakespeare, "To be or not to be" (from Hamlet) Robert Burns, "A Red, Red Rose" & "Auld Lang Syne" Key Concepts: Dramatic monologue, blank verse, soliloquy, poetry's relationship to music, dialect. Discussion Focus: How does poetry create character and explore deep philosophical questions on stage? How does the musicality of Burns's poetry make it so memorable and enduring? Unit 2: The Romantic Revolution – Nature, Imagination, and Emotion (Weeks 4-6) Week 4: The Spirit of Nature Poems: William Wordsworth, "I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud" William Wordsworth, "The Solitary Reaper" William Wordsworth, "The World Is Too Much with Us" Key Concepts: Romanticism, the sublime, the spiritual power of nature, memory. Discussion Focus: In Wordsworth's view, what is the proper relationship between humanity and the natural world? How can nature provide comfort and spiritual insight? Week 5: Beauty, Art, and Transience Poems: John Keats, "To Autumn" John Keats, "Ode on a Grecian Urn" Lord Byron, "She Walks in Beauty" Key Concepts: The Ode, sensory imagery, apostrophe, aestheticism ("art for art's sake"). Discussion Focus: How does Keats use language that appeals to all five senses? What timeless questions about art, beauty, and truth does the Grecian urn raise? Week 6: The Power of the Elements and the Poet Poems: Percy Bysshe Shelley, "Ode to the West Wind" Percy Bysshe Shelley, "Ozymandias" Key Concepts: Symbolism, personification, political commentary, irony. Discussion Focus: How does Shelley use the wind as a symbol for poetic inspiration and revolutionary change? How does "Ozymandias" use irony to comment on the fleeting nature of power? Unit 3: New Voices in a New World – The American Experience (Weeks 7-9) Week 7: The American Romantics – Contrasting Voices Poems: Henry Wadsworth Longfellow, "A Psalm of Life" & "I Shot an Arrow into the Air" Walt Whitman, "I Hear America Singing" Key Concepts: Didactic poetry, free verse, cataloging, the American identity. Discussion Focus: How does Longfellow's moralizing and formal verse differ from Whitman's expansive and democratic vision? What does it mean to "sing" America? Week 8: The Private and Powerful Voice of Emily Dickinson Poems: "I'm Nobody! Who are you?" "Success is counted sweetest" "A narrow Fellow in the Grass" "If you were coming in the Fall" Key Concepts: Slant rhyme, unique punctuation (the dash), personification, ballad meter. Discussion Focus: How does Dickinson explore big themes (identity, success, nature, love) in short, compressed poems? What effect does her unconventional style have on the reader? Week 9: The Harlem Renaissance – Expanding the American Song Poems: Walt Whitman, "O Captain! My Captain!" (revisited as context for Lincoln) Langston Hughes, "The Negro Speaks of Rivers" Langston Hughes, "Harlem (A Dream Deferred)" Key Concepts: The Harlem Renaissance, collective identity, historical consciousness, simile. Discussion Focus: How does "The Negro Speaks of Rivers" create a sense of history and identity that stretches across continents and centuries? How does "Harlem" use a series of powerful images to explore the consequences of social injustice? Unit 4: The Shock of the New – Modernism and Its Aftermath (Weeks 10-13) Week 10: The Turn to Modernism Poems: W. B. Yeats, "The Second Coming" W. B. Yeats, "The Lake Isle of Innisfree" T. S. Eliot, "The Love Song of J. Alfred Prufrock" Key Concepts: Modernism, symbolism, fragmentation, allusion, alienation. Discussion Focus: How do these poems reflect a sense of anxiety and change in the early 20th century? We will spend significant time decoding the challenging but rewarding "Prufrock." Week 11: The American Moderns – City vs. Country Poems: Carl Sandburg, "Chicago" & "Fog" Robert Frost, "Mending Wall" & "Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening" Key Concepts: Urban poetry, apostrophe, conversational tone, ambiguity. Discussion Focus: How do Sandburg and Frost create two very different portraits of modern American life? What deeper philosophical questions lie beneath the simple surfaces of Frost's poems? Week 12: Modernist Images and Victorian Echoes Poems: Ezra Pound, "In a Station of the Metro" William Carlos Williams, "The Red Wheelbarrow" Christina Rossetti, "Remember" Key Concepts: Imagism, "no ideas but in things," the Victorian sonnet. Discussion Focus: How can a poem create a powerful effect in just a few words? We will contrast the starkness of Imagism with the formal elegance and emotional depth of Rossetti's Victorian lyric. Week 13: The Dramatic Monologue and the Confessional Voice Poems: Robert Browning, "My Last Duchess" Sylvia Plath, "Metaphors" Key Concepts: The Dramatic Monologue, unreliable narrator, the Confessional school of poetry. Discussion Focus: How does Browning reveal the chilling character of the Duke through his own words? How does Plath use a single, extended metaphor to explore the complex experience of pregnancy? Unit 5: Contemporary Voices – Power, Nature, and Self (Weeks 14-16) Week 14: The Power of the Individual Voice Poems: Maya Angelou, "Still I Rise" Gwendolyn Brooks, "We Real Cool" Key Concepts: Anaphora (repetition), enjambment, voice, social commentary. Discussion Focus: How do Angelou and Brooks use rhythm and sound to create poems of defiance, resilience, and tragedy? Week 15: Finding Peace in the Modern World Poems: Mary Oliver, "Wild Geese" & "When I am among the trees" Ella Wheeler Wilcox, "Solitude" Key Concepts: Contemporary nature poetry, didactic verse, aphorism. Discussion Focus: How does Mary Oliver offer a path to finding one's place in the natural world? We will discuss the different ways poetry can offer comfort and wisdom. Week 16: The Complex Simplicity of Robert Frost Primary Poems for Discussion: "Mending Wall" "Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening" "The Road Not Taken" "Out, Out—" Secondary Poems (to illustrate his range): "Fire and Ice" "Acquainted with the Night" "Provide, Provide" Key Concepts for the Week: The Frost Persona:Understanding the speaker of his poems is not Frost himself, but a carefully crafted persona—the folksy, wise, Yankee farmer. Ambiguity and Irony:The gap between what is said and what is meant; how Frost’s poems resist easy interpretations. Nature as a Setting for Human Drama:Unlike the Romantics, Frost’s nature is not always benevolent or spiritual. It is often a dark, indifferent, and mysterious backdrop for human choice and tragedy. Boundaries and Isolation:A recurring theme explored through walls, woods, and darkness. Traditional Form, Modern Themes:How Frost uses classical forms (blank verse, sonnets, intricate rhyme schemes) to explore modern anxieties like alienation, doubt, and meaninglessness.
