课程简介（英文）

This is an introductory course on model theory, a branch of mathematical logic with close ties to abstract algebra. Model theory uses first-order logic to analyze mathematical structures such as groups, fields, rings, and partially ordered sets. For example, in this class we will characterize which sets are definable in the field C of complex numbers, and which structures M have the same first-order properties as C. Abstract algebra and mathematical logic are not prerequisites for this class. We will build up the necessary algebra from scratch. On the other hand, we will assume familiarity with mathematical formalism (lemmas, definitions, proofs, induction, functions, sets, ordered pairs, etc.). This is a mathematics course, not a philosophy course.

教学目标

We will learn what model theory is, and how it relates to abstract algebra. We will first study the basic tools of model theory, like definable sets, elementary equivalence, quantifier elimination, categoricity, the Löwenheim-Skolem theorem, the compactness theorem, and Vaught's criterion. We will apply these tools to prove quantifier elimination for algebraically closed fields, and deduce algebraic consequences. Finally, we will prove the Ryll-Nardzewski theorem about ℵ0-categorical theories.

教学方式

The material will be taught through blackboard-style mathematics lectures, accompanied by homework assignments. The lecturer will provide extensive course notes (a draft of a textbook), which the students may choose to read as a reference.

教学内容与进度安排

We will approximately follow this schedule: Weeks 1-2: theories, models, elementary classes, definable sets Weeks 3-4: equational theories, universal algebra, groups and rings Weeks 5-6: the compactness theorem, Löwenheim-Skolem theorem, categoricity Weeks 7-8: quantifier elimination and types Week 9: MIDTERM EXAM Weeks 10-12: algebraically closed fields, existentially closed models Weeks 13-14: monster models Weeks 15-16: the omitting types theorem and the Ryll-Nardzewski theorem Week 17: FINAL EXAM
