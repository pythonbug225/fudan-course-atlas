课程简介（中文）

本课程将系统阐释区块链演进的核心理念——这一金融科技革命的基石及其潜在颠覆性影响，涵盖稳定币、DeFi、NFT、资产代币化等数字经济/数字金融前沿议题。我们将深入探讨金融科技技术矩阵的协同效应，解析其如何催生多元化的颠覆性应用场景。课程内容既包含基础理论建构，更聚焦商业模式创新成果，以及金融相关行业与数字经济领域的产品服务应用实践。教学采用高度互动的研讨模式，通过典型案例分析结合行业领袖实战分享，激发创新思维。课程鼓励学员以开放革新的视角探索科技赋能金融的无限可能，最终需完成结业专题设计，将课堂所学转化为具有现实价值的创新项目方案。

课程简介（英文）

This course will explain the core knowledge about the evolution of blockchain, a cornerstone of Fintech revolution, and its potential disruption, including stablecoin, DeFi, NFTs, tokenization, and other related digital economy/digital finance topics. We will also discuss the synergies among other Fintech technologies, enabling a spectrum of disruptive impact. The course will cover the fundamentals and focus on the resulting business model innovation and the application use cases for products and services in finance-related and digital economy industries. The course is designed to be very interactive with discussions involving various business use cases as well as incorporating practical insights from leading industry experts. The class highly encourages students to adopt an innovative and open-minded attitude to leverage technology in finance. The class will require a final project whereby the concepts and learning developed in the class will be applied to a real-world project idea.

教学目标

This course introduces the basic concepts and definitions of blockchain technology and its applications in the financial system. It also covers the history and development of digital currencies. Overall, the course aims to help undergraduate students understand how blockchain technology and digital currency have reshaped and will continue to reshape the global economic and financial system.

教学方式

We will investigate through industry examples, classroom discussion, invited industry expert talks, team projects with regular sharing and weekly check-pointed write-ups.

教学内容与进度安排

Session 1 (Week 1-4) Topic: Blockchain Foundations: Verifiable Records, Consensus, Programmable Execution and Beyond Note: Week 1: Introduction to blockchain as a shared-ledger technology. This part explains why blockchain differs from an ordinary shared database, focusing on the shared-ledger problem, independent verification, tamper-evident history, and the conditions under which blockchain is actually needed. Students are introduced to a practical decision framework for distinguishing blockchain from traditional database solutions. Week 2: Consensus as the mechanism for resolving inconsistent histories. This part explains the goals and core properties of consensus, including shared ordering, safety, liveness, and finality, and shows why blockchain environments are more difficult than traditional distributed systems because identities are cheap to create. It introduces voting rights, Sybil attacks, and anti-Sybil mechanisms, and provides a conceptual introduction to Proof of Work and Proof of Stake. Week 3: Blockchain as a ledger of state and as a programmable execution system. This part covers transaction validity, block structure, hash commitments, and verifiable state transitions, and then introduces smart contracts and decentralized applications (DApps). It explains how blockchain extends from record-keeping to programmable rule execution and automated digital coordination. Week 4: Verification beyond a single chain. This part introduces privacy-preserving techniques, zero-knowledge proofs, and cross-chain mechanisms. It highlights how blockchain systems can be extended to support privacy and interoperability, while also clarifying which trust assumptions remain outside the scope of purely on-chain verification. The two important examples of public blockchain: Bitcoin and Ethereum. Smart contract basics.
