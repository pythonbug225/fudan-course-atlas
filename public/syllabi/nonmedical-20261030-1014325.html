图情研究经典、渊源以及方法

2025秋 · MLIS60010.01 · 李彦霖、刘丽君

来源：复旦教务系统教学大纲；正文整理。

课程简介（中文）

本课程将指导学生对图情研究的经典英文文献进行深度解读和研讨，帮助学生对当下飞速发展的图情学科的有关重要课题的历史渊源和实践运用获得较为全面的理解和把握。 该课程所选择的图情经典专题文献关涉图情理论和管理实践的重要论题。授课包括导读和讨论英文经典文献文本，介绍学术期刊、专著以及数据库（期刊库和电子书库）渊源与检索策略，针对学生在外文专业书刊及数据库检索和获取中的常见问题和难点，以所择英文文献为例，从术语到检索、从文献甄别到学术论文相关性和学术价值的判断等给予指导，以帮助学生加强英文文献的阅读理解、分析以及写作能力，提高硕士论文的英文摘要部分的写作水平，进而提高学生对图情研究领域的历史脉络和国际研究成果的把握。

课程简介（英文）

This course will guide students to conduct in-depth interpretation and discussion of classic English literature in library and information science (LIS) research. It aims to help students gain a comprehensive understanding and grasp of the historical origins and practical applications of important topics in the rapidly developing field of library and information science. The selected classic thematic literature in library and information science for this course covers key topics in LIS theory and management practice. The teaching content includes guided reading and discussion of classic English literature texts, introduction to the origins and retrieval strategies of academic journals, monographs, and databases (journal databases and e-book databases). Addressing common problems and difficulties encountered by students in retrieving and accessing foreign-language professional books, periodicals, and databases, the course will use the selected English literature as examples to provide guidance—from terminology explanation to literature retrieval, from literature screening to the judgment of the relevance and academic value of academic papers. This is intended to help students enhance their abilities in reading comprehension, analysis, and writing of English literature, improve their writing skills in the English abstract section of master's theses, and further strengthen their grasp of the historical context of the library and information science research field and international research achievements.

教学目标

LEARNING OUTCOMES The students will search, identify, and analyse the research articles and important topics for their master thesis; The students will apply the theories and thoughts learned to the practice of current issues in the fields; The students will grasp the origin and development of the important theories, thoughts, and arguments in the field.

教学方式

COURSE MATERIALS Basic readings with full text will be assigned; Additional readings with citations will be added during the classes. Major reference book: Richard E. Rubin, Rachel G. Rubin. Foundations of Library and Information Science. Vol Fifth edition. ALA Neal-Schuman; 2020 McDonald, John D., Levine-Clark, Michael. Encyclopedia of Library and Information Science, Fourth Edition. This is a reading-intensive course. You are expected to share your thoughts or raise questions at least 3 times for each class session so that you prove to your instructor your preparation for the class and your respect for your peers. Failing to participate will count as one absence. If you are late (or leave class early) three times, it will also count as one absence. You are allowed to miss three sessions without any penalty, but each additional absence will result in a 5% decrease in your final grade. Major Assignment: Throughout the semester, students will be working on an essay for conference submissions (Final essay). The 1st and 2nd homework are designed to help you work towards it. You will be given an opportunity to discuss your work within your group with an instructor and improve it. You are not required to finish your homework at this point, but you do need to have a discussable draft to participate. 1st Homework: Use your tentative master thesis topic (if you have chosen) or any topic that interests you, conduct a search and locate relevant literature, including articles, books, or reference tools, from any resources from Fudan University Library in both electronic and print: It is recommended to start with the course reading list or Encyclopedia of Library and Information Science as you are required to cite at least 3 of them. Observe the language of these articles and locate keywords that can lead you to other literature. List no less than 20 relevant titles (including at least 3 from the reading list of this course) in English by following the national standard citation manual; List no less than 5 relevant titles in Chinese by following the national standard citation manual, which cited the English titles you selected; Write an annotated bibliography that illustrates the background of your research: What is the topic (the problem to be solved/ the question to be answered)? What are the relevant concepts? From what aspects of the problem/question will you discuss this problem/question? What are the arguments along with the counter-argument(s)? For the concepts or aspects that you intend to cite resources across languages, compile two annotated bibliography keywords: How are they related? Is one of them a direct translation? etc... What information/opinion do you intend to cite? Does it define a concept or support/contradict an argument? Please read the rubric for more detailed requirements. 2nd homework: Based on your first homework, write a first draft of your final esssay: Cite no less than 10 English literature, and follow the national standard citation manual for the citation format; Clearly describs the topic and important concepts in paragraphes and complete sentences. Address the arguments from the literature and your proposed assumption, in paragraphes or bullet points. If needed, an examination design. Final essay: The improved and complete version of your 2nd homework with conference submission information: Title of the proposed presentation; An abstract of the proposed presentation (150-200 words); Names of presenter(s), position(s) and/or title(s); Employer or affiliated institution; Contact information including email address and telephone number; Brief biographical statement (no more than 100 words) Late Assignments : Assignments are due at midnight on the due date. After that time, they are considered late, and your score will drop 10%, with an additional 10% grade reduction for each day past the due date that your instructor receives the assignment. In cases of illness or family emergency, students must contact faculty members by email before the assignment is due. In-Class Activities: Some class sessions may involve in-class activities, and these activities may result in a grade. One typical in-class activity would be sharing an assigned reading. The grade will be calculated as an average score for all your in-class activities grade. You cannot make up an in-class activity if you miss the class, but the lowest two scores will be dropped off. Open-book Pop Quizzes: The purpose of having pop quizzes is to enforce reading. The quizzes will be given according to the reading materials for that session. “Open-book” means you can still read during the quiz, including your notes. However, any usage of electronic devices will be considered cheating. Therefore, the range of "open-book" is limited to non-digital material. CLASSROOM DECORUM You are expected to conduct yourself in a professional manner. Turn off your cell phone; even if it does not ring, it is distracting. Texting, sleeping, working on non-related material or being disrespectful is unacceptable. If you behave in a disruptive manner during a common session, small group, or lab session, you will be marked absent for the day, and you may be asked to leave the session.
