课程简介（中文）

This course examines the latest research on investment strategies and asset pricing. The course primarily focuses on quantitative strategies in stocks and bonds including international assets. The purpose of the course is to expose future investment professionals both to fundamental concepts in portfolio management and to cutting edge research. Starting with the building blocks of risk and return, the course discusses building quantitative strategies, assessing and controlling investment risk, how to implement strategies, how to evaluate the performance of managers, and minimizing trading costs. It also discusses new research in behavioral finance on predictable patterns in asset returns perhaps due to systematic mistakes made by investors, including evidence on value and momentum strategies. Financial crises and their impact on investments decisions will be covered as well. The course includes multiple real-world practical examples to illustrate the theoretical concepts.

教学目标

This course is part of the Quantitative Finance Studies Program at FDSM, taught by Yacine Aït Sahalia, Jianqing Fan and Ronnie K. Sircar, Professors at the Bendheim Center for Finance, Princeton University. The course is designed to equip individuals interested in careers as portfolio managers and financial analysts at mutual funds, pension funds, and investment professionals at other providers of investment services such as financial planners, insurance companies, investment banks, and commercial banks with the major tools and methodologies employed in these positions. The course will also provide general managers, senior functional managers, and other non-financial managers who may have no formal background in investment management with sufficient familiarity of investment theory and practice to interpret and comfortably use investment information in their daily decisions. Outside these roles, the course will equip students to communicate more effectively Advanced Portfolio Management Course Syllabus 2 with investment specialists, including portfolio managers and investment product providers who will interact with them in a sell side capacity. After completing this course, students will be able to: . Master the main tools from econometrics and statistics that are employed in asset pricing applications . Be able to construct optimal portfolios . Understand the variables affecting equity asset returns . Be prepared to conducted basic empirical research on the properties of asset returns including the identification of common factors and their separation from idiosyncratic components . Conduct portfolio evaluation at both the individual and institutional levels . Understand the factor affecting bond returns and the management of fixed income portfolio . Understand the dynamics behind financial crises, the circumstances that make them possible and the reforms that make them less likely. . Understand the new developments in financial technologies (FinTech). Student performance will be assessed on the basis of an exam (in class, closed notes) given at the end of the semester. A sample exam is provided in the course packet. A graduate Teaching Assistant is available for the duration of the course to help students as needed.

教学方式

Lecture notes You will receive a printed copy of the lecture notes. Lecture notes are made available to help your own learning only. You are not allowed to share lecture notes or any other course materials such as the practice questions and answers, post or redistribute them to anyone or anywhere, including online, etc. Please respect these rules. Pre-read chapters Chapter 1 (Review of Econometrics and Statistics) and Chapter 2 (Risk, Return and their Measurement) of the lecture notes cover material that you have already learned but is necessary to review before we begin the course. These two chapters are for you to study on your own before the course begins. They will not be covered during the lectures. Their content will be assumed known.
