课程简介（中文）

  本课程邀请国际知名学者为复旦大学化学或相关学科本科生及研究生授课，主题覆盖当前化学研究发展的前沿领域。2026学年邀请荷兰莱顿大学医学院Oleg A. Mayboroda教授讲授《化学视角的人体生理学：组学观点》，从定量与测量视角阐述人体代谢调节的原理。本课程基于一个核心理念：代谢是一个可调节的化学网络，只有通过测量才能揭示其运作逻辑。不同于传统生理学对器官和疾病的罗列式描述，也不同于对复杂难辨的酶促反应网络的简单堆砌，本课程重点探讨分析化学如何通过实现结构鉴定、浓度测量和控制机制的实验可操作性，揭示代谢调节的内在逻辑。

课程简介（英文）

  This course invites internationally renowned scholars to teach undergraduate and graduate students at Fudan University in chemistry or related disciplines, covering cutting-edge topics in current chemical research. In 2026, Professor Oleg A. Mayboroda from Leiden University Medical Center in the Netherlands will deliver the course "Human Physiology for Chemists: An Omics Perspective." The course introduces the principles of human metabolic regulation from a quantitative and measurement-centered perspective. It is built on the premise that metabolism is a regulated chemical network that becomes intelligible only through measurement. Rather than presenting physiology as a catalogue of organs and diseases or complex, confusing network of the enzymes the course examines how analytical chemistry reveals the logic of metabolic regulation by making structural identification, concentration measurements, and control mechanisms experimentally accessible. The human body is therefore approached as a dynamic, multicomponent chemical system organized into interacting pools whose stability and adaptability depend on coordinated regulatory processes.

教学目标

  This course aims to introduce chemistry and analytical chemistry students to the principles of human physiology and metabolic regulation from a measurement-centered perspective. The objective is to develop the ability to view the human body as a dynamic chemical system whose stability and adaptability arise from coordinated regulatory processes that operate across multiple molecular and physiological scales.

  Students will learn how physicochemical constraints, metabolic regulatory networks interact to maintain homeostasis under varying physiological conditions. Particular emphasis is placed on understanding how concentrations of metabolites reflect underlying regulatory mechanisms, and why some variables are maintained under tight control while others show greater biological variability.

  The course further aims to develop critical awareness of how analytical measurements shape biomedical knowledge. Students will examine both the power and the limitations of modern analytical technologies, recognizing that physiological processes are typically inferred from a limited set of accessible biological samples.

  By integrating concepts from physiology, biochemistry, and analytical science, the course seeks to prepare students to interpret biochemical measurements in a physiological context and to navigate the data-rich landscape of contemporary biomedical research. In doing so, it emphasizes the balance between empirical measurement and conceptual understanding that underlies meaningful scientific inquiry.

教学方式

   The course combines lectures, guided discussions, and written assignments. Lectures introduce key physiological and biochemical concepts, while written exercises develop interpretative reasoning skills. Round-table discussions and Q&A seminars encourage critical analysis of experimental data and train the integration of analytical chemistry approaches with physiological reasoning.
