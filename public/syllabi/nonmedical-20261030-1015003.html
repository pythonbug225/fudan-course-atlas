基督教哲学专题

来源：复旦教务系统教学大纲；正文整理。

课程简介（中文）

本课程概述了基督教哲学的独特之处，为哲学系学生和其他院系的学生提供了引人入胜的基督教思想入门。 不了解基督教哲学就无法理解西方文化。基督教影响了西方社会的各个领域，包括文学、电影、音乐和艺术。即使是当代世俗社会也无法摆脱基督教的影响，仍然生活在一个主要由基督教创立的思想体系中。 通过探讨基督教哲学的关键主题，本课程将向学生介绍基督教以及西方文化中一些最重要的概念和美学。

课程简介（英文）

This course provides a broad overview of the key themes that make Christian philosophy distinctive. It offers an engaging introduction to Christian thought for both philosophy students and students from other departments. It is impossible to understand Western culture without understanding Christian philosophy. Christianity has influenced every area of Western society, including its literature, film, music and art. Even contemporary secular society cannot escape the influence of Christianity but continues to live in a system of thought largely created by this religion. By examining key themes in Christian philosophy, the course will introduce students to some of the most important concepts and aesthetics in the religion, and by extension Western culture.

教学目标

The course will introduce students to some of the most important concepts and aesthetics in the religion, and by extension Western culture. It will be taught through the use of primary texts, written by some of the most prominent Christian philosophers in history, familiarizing students with these key scholars. The mode of interpretation will be analytical, giving students the opportunity to develop their own critical thinking skills to decide which approach to each theme they find most convincing. It will also introduce them to the research methodologies expected for international publication, so the course will be of particular benefit to students who wish to progress to graduate-level study or continue their studies abroad. The language of teaching for this course is English, giving students a supportive environment to practice reading philosophical texts in English, become familiar with key philosophical and aesthetic themes in English, and express their own academic ideas in oral and written English.

教学方式

The content of the course is structured by seven key themes in Christian philosophy. Each theme will be studied over two classes that will examine two different approaches to the theme. In this way, students will gain first-hand experience of the diversity of thinking within Christian philosophy even regarding its core concepts. Love Beauty Evil Suffering Language Faith Holiness This course will be taught through weekly lectures that describe and analyze key texts in the field of Christian philosophy. Each lecture will include multiple opportunities to raise questions and discuss their own ideas in relation to the text, allowing students to develop their critical thinking skills in a controlled environment.

教学内容与进度安排

Introduction to Christian themes: Outline the structure of the course. Provide examples of the way Christian values have reshaped philosophy and aesthetics within both academic and popular perspectives. Love 1: Discuss Richard of St. Victor’s concept of love leading to union with God and service to others. Love 2: Examine Thomas Aquinas’ understanding of God as love itself. Beauty 1: Explain David Bentley Hart’s argument for “beauty” as a theological category. Beauty 2: Discuss the way Christian philosophy leads to an aesthetic of “broken beauty” in Western art. Evil 1: Examine Augustine’s explanation of evil as a corruption of goodness. Evil 2: Explore Karl Barth’s explanation of evil as the reality rejected by God in the act of creation. Suffering 1: Outline Jürgen Moltmann’s concept of God suffering alongside humanity. Suffering 2: Discuss Karen Kilby’s conclusion that no convincing explanation of human suffering is possible within Christian philosophy. Language 1: Explain Pseudo-Dionysius’ attempt to speak about God by balancing positive and negative language. Language 2: Discuss Thomas Aquinas’ argument for the possibility of direct language about God through the principle of analogical signification. Faith 1: Outline Martin Luther’s concept of salvation by faith alone. Faith 2: Explain Schleiermacher’s concept of faith as a feeling of absolute dependence. Holiness 1: Explore the concept of theosis, first proposed by Athanasius. Holiness 2: Discuss the role of the Holy Spirit in Christian experience. Recap and discussion of course syllabus: Brief summary of the content of the course. Classroom debates regarding the continued significance of Christian themes in Western culture.
