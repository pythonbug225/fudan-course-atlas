中国古文字比较研究

来源：复旦教务系统教学大纲；正文整理。

课程简介（中文）

本课程目标是介绍中国现存最早的文字材料，即商代晚期甲骨刻辞和青铜器铭文，并将这些文字材料放在古代世界其他早期文明文字的大背景中比较。本课程特别注意文字产生之前的表现系统。通过这些文字材料的研读，熟悉早期文字的载体、文字记载的内容、使用文字的场合等，提高比较研究能力。听完本课程后，能够对中国文字产生发展过程得到更广泛、更深入的理解。

课程简介（英文）

This course introduces recent studies on early Chinese writing. The aim of the course is to present script invention in China in a comparative perspective. The course covers the time period from Neolithic cultures (ca. 6500 BC) until the beginning of the Western Zhou period (ca. 1046—771 BC), but the main emphasis is on the late Shang Dynasty (ca. 1250—1046 BC). For China, this was a period of great social change which included the development of writing. It is in the material and written culture of this period that we first find codified expressions of ideology as well as the origins of values and concepts that characterize classical Chinese thought for the next three millennia. Although the emphasis is on Western works, the course is also based on recent interpretations of oracle-bone and bronze inscriptions in Chinese paleography. The course is taught in English, but some materials for reading are available in Chinese translation.

教学目标

The objective of the course is to introduce modern trends of research on early Chinese writing. The contents of the course contain examples of oracle-bone and bronze inscriptions and incorporate relevant works of Chinese paleographers, but the main emphasis is on introducing the current state of Western scholarship in this field. This course is designed to give students understanding that though oracle-bone and bronze inscriptions are valuable sources of early Chinese ideology and history in their own right, certain themes, ideas, and functions that we find in early Chinese writing parallel other regions of antiquity and as such they are worth situating in a comparative framework.

教学方式

面授。

教学内容与进度安排

Week Content 1 Introduction: The contents and requirements of the course. Defining “early civilization”. Defining “early writing”. 2 Visual representation before writing: Upper Palaeolithic visual art. 3-5 Predynastic Egypt. Emergence of writing in Egypt. Visual and written culture in Early Dynastic Egypt. 6-7 Mesopotamia and the Ancient Near East. The origin of the cuneiform writing. 8-9 Neolithic China. 10-13 Oracle-bone and bronze inscriptions of the late Shang Dynasty. 14-15 Emergence of writing in Preclassic Mesoamerica. Reading the Maya glyphs and art. 16 Students work on final papers.
