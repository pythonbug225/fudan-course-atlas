课程简介（中文）

Ever since Descartes ushered in modern philosophical investigations of the necessary conditions for knowledge, the relationship between thinking or reasoning and knowing or having knowledge has been fraught with difficulties.  A demand for complete proof by reason has led to versions of skepticism.  Gettier’s discovery of a fatal flaw in the traditional conception of knowledge as justified true beliefs has given the movement against the overly intellectual theory of knowledge new momentum.  Externalism could be seen as a rebellion against “thinking too much” for the sake of having knowledge.  However, contemporary philosophical theories of knowledge are still too distant from the scientific understanding of our cognitive faculty.  In the second half or third of the course, we devote our time to discuss the relevant scientific literature on cognition and knowledge. The leading text will be Danial Kahneman’s “Thinking Fast and Slow.”  We know a lot of things without having to think too much, but such knowing often leads us into lazy generalizations that breeds stereotypes and prejudices.  An important role of thinking carefully is to correct prejudices.

教学目标

To teach philosophy major undergraduate students (and perhaps MA level philosophy students) the difficult relationship between Thinking and Knowing in the history as well as contemporary research about knowledge and cognition.

教学方式

Classroom lectures and in-class discussion.

教学内容与进度安排

Week 1: Introduction：general issues of theory of knowledge

Readings: No assigned readings for the 1st class.

Week 2: Cartesian general skepticism: dreams and demons

Readings: 【TK】Selection from Descartes’s Meditations

Week 3: Humean special skepticism, on e.g. induction and causation

Readings: 【TK】Selections from Hume’s Enquiry

Week 4: Perceptual Knowledge: causal and receptive theory of perception

Readings: 【TK】Selections from Locke’s Treatise

Week 5: National Day holiday

Week 6: What counts knowledge and its problems: Gettier Problem

Readings: 【TK】Gettier paper;

Week 8: Internalist vs. externalist theory of knowledge

Readings: 【TK】1. Goldman.  2. Lehrer & Paxon

Week 9: Thinking, reason, and perceiving: the realm of reason for knowledge

Readings: 【SA】1. 刘闯.  2. Sellars; McDowell

Week 10: Know-how vs know-that: Ryle on know-how knowledge

Readings: 【SA】Ryle

Week 11: Psychology of judging and knowing: fast and slow thinking

Readings: 【TFS】Kahneman

Week 12: Models of perception: Predictive Processing (PP) and Bayesian Brain (I)

Readings: 【SU】Part I

Week 13: Models of perception: Predictive Processing (PP) and Bayesian Brain (II)

Readings: 【SU】Part II (perhaps also Part III)

Week 14: Neuro- and psycho- logical grounding of fast/slow thinking

Reading: 【SA】1. 刘闯；2. Selections of 【FST】 and 【SU】

Week 15: Virtual epistemology revisited: Rethinking Sosa’s virtual epistemology

Readings: 【SA】1. Sosa; 2. 刘闯

Week 16: Intelligence & knowledge in nature: their independence and relations

Readings：【SA】刘闯

Final paper
