课程简介（中文）

课程简介（Course Description） 本课程旨在系统提升金融学博士生的学术写作、学术展示与分析能力，重点训练学生阅读与撰写高质量英文金融学术论文的能力。课程将写作策略、学术汇报技巧与优秀金融学论文的精读与分析相结合，帮助学生全面提升以学术英语进行研究交流与成果表达的能力。

课程简介（英文）

Course Description This course is designed to strengthen the writing, presentation, and analytical skills of Ph.D. students in finance, with an emphasis on reading and producing high-quality academic English manuscripts. It combines writing strategies, presentation techniques, and the analysis of exemplary finance papers.

教学目标

Course Objectives Improve academic writing in finance. Develop effective oral presentation skills. Analyze well-written finance research papers. Engage in mini-projects and presentations for hands-on practice.

教学方式

教学方式 本课程采用互动式与实践导向相结合的教学方式，包括课堂讨论、学生论文展示以及研究 proposal 的撰写与讲评。通过围绕经典与前沿金融文献开展结构化讨论，训练学生的批判性阅读与学术表达能力；通过论文与研究想法的展示，提升学生进行学术汇报与回应反馈的能力；通过分阶段的 proposal 撰写与修改，帮助学生系统掌握规范的研究设计与学术写作方法。

教学内容与进度安排

Course Schedule (Tentative, subject to change) Week 1: Academic Writing Skills Topics: Common issues in academic writing. Writing clearly, concisely, and logically. Structuring finance papers: Abstract, Introduction, Results. Readings: Cochrane, J. H. (2005). Writing tips for PhD students. Unpublished Manuscript, University of Chicago, Booth School of Business. Berk, J. B., Harvey, C. R., & Hirshleifer, D. (2017). Preparing a referee report. Journal of Economic Perspectives, 31(1), 231–244. Harvey, Campbell R. (2015). Reflections on Editing the Journal of Finance, 2006-2012, https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2191787 Week 2: Market Efficiency & Social Benefits of Finance Topics: Market Efficiency and Its Empirical Challenges Finance’s Role in Society: Benefits and Externalities Readings: Fama, E. F. (1991). Efficient Capital Markets: II. The Journal of Finance, 46(5), 1575–1617. Zingales, Luigi, 2015, Presidential Address: Does Finance Benefit Society? Volume70, Issue4, Pages 1327-1363. (optional) Journal of Finance Presidential Address: https://afajof.org/presidential-address-videos/ Week 3: Venture Capital Topics: Reading complex survey and empirical papers. Analyzing structure, data presentation, and logical flow. Readings: Hochberg, Yael V., Alexander Ljungqvist, and Yang Lu, 2010, Networking as a barrier to entry and the competitive supply of venture capital, Journal of Finance 65, 829–859. Lerner, J., & Nanda, R. (2020). Venture capital's role in financing innovation: What we know and how much we still need to learn. Journal of Economic Perspectives, 34(3), 237–261. Gompers, P., Gornall, W., Kaplan, S. N., & Strebulaev, I. A. (2020). How do venture capitalists make decisions? Journal of Financial Economics, 135(1), 169–190. Week 4: Innovation & Product Market Topics: Innovation, patents, and product market Highlighting hypothesis development and contribution. Readings: Hoberg, G., & Phillips, G. M. (2025). Scope, Scale, and Concentration: The 21st-Century Firm, Volume 80, Issue1, Pages 415-466 Hirshleifer, D., Hsu, P.-H., & Li, D. (2013). Innovative efficiency and stock returns. Journal of Financial Economics, 107(3), 632–654. Chemmanur, Thomas J., Loutskina, Elena and Tian, Xuan, 2014, Corporate Venture Capital, Value Creation, and Innovation, The Review of Financial Studies, Vol. 27, No. 8, pp. 2434-2473. Week 5: Institutional Trading, Low Latency Trading Topics:​ Institutional trading skills Effects of Low-Latency Trading on Market Dynamics​ Readings: Brogaard, Jonathan, Hendershott, Terrence and Riordan, Ryan, 2014, High-Frequency Trading and Price Discovery, The Review of Financial Studies, Vol. 27, No. 8, pp. 2267-2306 Puckett, Andy, and Xuemin Sterling Yan, 2011, The interim trading skills of institutional investors, Journal of Finance 66, 601-633. Week 6: Presentations Students are expected to present a short project. Acceptable formats include a focused literature review, a novel research idea, or a brief empirical analysis. Peer feedback on writing and presentation. Instructor feedback on language, clarity, and logic.
