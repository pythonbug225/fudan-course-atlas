教学内容与进度安排

The measured signal from the sensor is usually weak. Therefore, one of the key issue for designing a sensor and a corresponding instrument for the parameter measurement is how to accurately measure and effectively deal with the weak signals. In this way, the focus of this course is how to use advanced digital signal processing technology (such as denoise and correlation detecting technology) to process weak signals from a sensor. It is possible for one to combine the fusion technology from many different kinds of sensors and the advanced digital signal processing technology for solving the detection problem of measured coupling parameters. Thus, it is expected that some measured parameters which could not be measured and/or be detected very well in the past can be handled by sensor signal processing. Therefore, the theory and method of digital signal processing combined with sensor data fusion will also be the content that must be mastered in this course. It is known that the conventional sensors only report the measurement values. Their actual accuracy and/or the accuracy in an application are generally unknown, usually replaced by nominal values. If an installed sensor is newly calibrated, one may believe that the measurements given by the sensor are within the range of the accuracy given. Are the measurements reported by the sensors still within the range of accuracy given on the calibrated time after a period of use? Therefore, it is necessary for students to understand the theory and method of self-validating sensors. This is because the theory of the self-validating sensor can help to improve the accuracy and reliability of the measurements via the sensor self-diagnosis. Furthermore, when the sensor has been confirmed to be faulty, the measurement value can still be estimated by sensor-validating technology.
