俄罗斯史

2025秋 · HIST30073.01 · Rostislav Berezkin

来源：复旦教务系统教学大纲；正文整理。

课程简介（中文）

本课程介绍俄罗斯史主要的事件与人物，从九世纪（基辅罗斯的建立）至1917年（十月革命，即俄罗斯大帝国的灭亡），同时要介绍该时期俄罗斯的文化，美术与思想方面的主要成就。教师也关注俄罗斯与周围国家关系史（包括文化往来），特别注意俄罗斯与中国关系史。

课程简介（英文）

This course presents a broad picture of political and cultural situation, including the Russo-Chinese contacts in the 17th-19th centuries. I will also introduce basic historical sources (in Chinese and English translations) and relevant research works that students can use for the further study. The course is taught in English. The contents is divided into sixteen lectures, including the Kievan Rus, the period of division, culture of the period of Mongol dependency, culture of independent Moscow state; the beginning of secular culture in the 17th century; reforms of Peter I, Westernization of Russian culture in the 18th century; the golden age of aristocratic culture at the end of the 18th century; new trends and schools in Russian culture at the beginning of the 20th century.

教学目标

The aim of this course is to introduce major figures and events in the history of Russia from the 9th century till 1917. It also presents major achievements of the Russians in the culture, arts, and thought of that period. This is a new course, as the history of Russia was never taught at Fudan University before. I try to provide the students with the basic knowledge of the history of Russia and its place in the world history.

教学方式

Lectures and discussion of historical materials (in English translation)

教学内容与进度安排

Week 1: Origins of Russia Introduction: Russia as a neighbor state of China. The importance of Russian history for the Chinese students of history. Geographical position of Russia and cultural exchange between Western and Oriental countries. Russia before the Russians: multi-national diversity before the establishment of the Russian state. East Slavonic peoples: origins, migrations and development. Special features of indigenous Russian culture and religion. Week 2: Kievan Russia (9th -11th century) Introduction: Starting with the legend of the foreign Prince’s invitation. Development of trade on the lands of modern Russia. The influence of trade on the formation of Russian state. Exchange between different nations and growth of power of the Kievan Prince. The establishment of feudal system in Russia. Was Kievan Russia an agricultural or urban state? The biggest cities of Russia and their development. Introduction of Orthodox Christianity in the 10th century and its influence on the culture and art. Reasons for the decay of Kiev and weakening of its state. Week 3: Russia during the period of “feudal division”. Introduction: The prince succession law in Kievan Russia and the problems it brought. How many states were there in ancient Russia? The development of Russian feudal system. The reasons and consequences of the split. Was there democracy in ancient Russia? The city-state of Novgorod and development of north-western Russia. Attacks of the neighbor nations: fighting with Germans and Swedish knights in the north-west and Turkic peoples in the south-east in the 12-13th centuries. Achievements in the culture: architecture, painting and decorative arts developed extremely quickly. Mongols’ invasion and the destruction of economy, cities and culture. Week 4: The period of Mongolian rule and growth of Moscow. Introduction: When China and Russia were parts of one Mongolian state. The rule of Tartars (Golden Horde State) and continuation of Russia’s split into autonomous states. The reasons of the Moscow’s growth and the fight for power between princes. How Moscow princes “collected” their lands: towards unification of Russia. The origins of peasants’ servitude in Russia. How Moscow princes fought with the Tartar rulers. Lithuanian-Russian State and the reasons for the division between “Great Russia”, Belorussia and Ukraine. Week 5: Great Prince of Moscow (15th-early 16th century). Introduction: Ivan the Third became the ruler of the independent state. The defeat of the Tartars (the end of the Golden Horde) and building of the independent state. Establishment of servitude system. The independence of state brought the independence of church. Development of Russian Orthodox Church and its relations with the political authority. Development of trade, cities, culture and art in the 14th-15th century. Week 6: Ivan the Fourth – the first Russian tsar and his time (1533-1584). Introduction: “Terrible monarch”: a blessing or disaster for the country? The inner politics of Ivan the Terrible during the beginning of his reign (1533-1584) and consolidation of the tsar’s power. Ivan the Terrible conquers small Tartar states. Russian state expands to the east. Ivan the Terrible fights with “opposition” in his state. The policy of “divided state”. Wars with Poland and Sweden: Russia is defeated on the Western borders. Week 7: Time of chaos (1598-1613). Introduction: Ivan the Terrible’s son Fedor Ivanovich (1584-1598) and the end of the first Russian dynasty. Boris Godunov (1598-1603) and his attempt to establish a new dynasty. The time of chaos and foreign invasion: “The fake prince” and Polish occupation of Moscow. Patriotic movement, war with Poland and establishment of Romanov dynasty (1613-1917) . Week 8: New tsar dynasty and the development of Russian state in 1613-1682. Midterm examination Introduction: Was the 17th century the time of success of Russia? The inner and foreign politics of first tsars of Romanov dynasty: Mikhail (1613-1645), Alexey (1645-1676) and Fedor (1676-1682). Does upheaval continue? Folk uprisings and endless wars. Eastern Ukraine returns to the Russian state. Development of religion and culture in the 17th century: the great Church schism and the beginning of Westernization process. The first contacts between Russia and China. Week 9: the time of Peter the Great (1682-1725) and the establishment of Russian empire. Introduction: the new dynastic crisis and the attempt of woman’s reign (Tsarevna Sofia). Peter the Great and Westernization, secularization of Russia. Successful wars against Turkey and Sweden. Peter the Great built new army and fleet. Peter the Great founded Saint-Petersburg (1703) and moved his capital there (1712). The reasons and consequences for the capital’s change. Peter the Great and Oriental countries, his interest for China. People who disobeyed Peter the Great’s reforms. Russia faces new political crisis. Week 10: Descendants of Peter the Great: the epoch of palace revolts (1725-1762). Introduction: The epoch of women’s reign: upheaval starts from the palace. Everybody can be an emperor: the reign of Katherine the First (1725-1727), Peter the Second (1727-1730), Anna (1730-1740) and Ivan the Sixth (1740-1741). Reign of Peter the Great’s daughter Elisabeth (1741-1762) and the return of Peter the Great’s policies. Development of culture and arts: Westernization gains power. Reign of Peter the Third in 1762 and the new revolt. Week 11: Catherine the Great (1762-1796) and the time of enlightened monarchy. Introduction: how the German princess became the Empress of Russia. Advantages and disasters of “enlightened absolutism”: the wars and uprisings during Catherine’s reign. Russia conquers Crimea, “three divisions of Poland”, Russia controls Ukraine and Belorussia. New developments in Russian culture. Catherine the Great and the interest for China. Week 12: Russia at the end of the 18th-beginning of the 19th century. Introduction: Unfilial tsar Paul (1796-1801) and his denial of Catherine’s policy. The beginning of Alexander’s reign (1801-1825): Catherine’s policy revisited. The first Great Patriotic War (1812): Russia defeats Napoleon. Terror of servitude system and the process of liberation of serves: the defeat of “aristocratic revolution” in 1825. Week 13: Russia in the middle 19th century Introduction: epoch of Tolstoy and Dostoevsky Conservative emperor: Nikolai the First (1825-1855). Crimean war and defeat of Russia. Alexander the Second (1855-1881) and liberation of serves in 1861. Development of revolutionary movement and the end of liberal emperor in 1881. Russian conquest of Poland and Central Asian states: the epoch of “unequal treaties”. Week 14: Russian culture in the period great reforms (1860-1880) The spread of revolutionary movements: “going to the people” movement and revolutionary terror. The golden age of Russian novel: novels by Turgenev (1818-1883), Lev N. Tolstoi (1828-1910) and Dostoevsky (1821-1881) and their influence in the world. The golden age of Russian classical music: Tchaikovsky (1840-1893), development of Russian ballet. “Moving exhibitions” and Russian national style in painting. Week 15: The last emperors of Russia Introduction: Russian empire faces revolution. Two conservative emperors Alexander the Third (1881-1894) and Nikolai the Second (1894-1917): Russia develops in the traditional system. Russo-Japanese war and the first revolution in 1905. Can Russia develop and a constitutional monarchy? Two revolutions in 1917 and the reasons for the empire’s collapse. Week 16: Russian culture at the end of the 19th century (1880-1900) Introduction: Cultural policies during the reign of Alexander III (1881-1894) and the first period of Nicholas II reign (1894-1905). Russian education before Revolution of 1917; major research institutions. Novels and drama by Chekhov (1840-1904): development of literary satire. Major achievements of Russian science in the second half of the 19th century. Folk arts and crafts in the second half of the 19th century. The second wave of “Chinese taste” in the second half of the 19th century and images of China in Russian imagination. Week 17: Oral presentation on final paper in class Week 18: Writing final paper
