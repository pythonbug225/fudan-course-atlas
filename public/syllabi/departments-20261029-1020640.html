课程简介（中文）

这门课程介绍审计的基本原理，重点关注财务报表审计。课程涵盖审计的性质、各种审计类型以及注册会计师（CPA）职业的角色。学生将学习审计报告、审计目标，以及审计证据的收集与评价。课程还探讨审计风险模型、不同的审计测试方法和完成审计所需要的程序。

课程简介（英文）

This course introduces students to the fundamentals of auditing, focusing on the financial statements audit. It covers key topics such as the nature of auditing, various types of audits, and the role of the CPA profession. Students will learn about audit reports, audit objectives, and the accumulation and evaluation of audit evidence. The course also explores the audit risk model, different audit tests, and the procedures for completing an audit.

教学目标

On successfully completing this course, students are expected: 1）To understand the auditing profession including CPA profession, ethics, and legal liability. 2）To know concepts and theories regarding audit objectives, responsibilities, audit evidence, audit report, audit risk, and audit planning. 3)   To  develop a comprehensive understanding of auditing processes.

教学内容与进度安排

Week 1: Audit and assurance service Nature of auditing Distinction between accounting and auditing Economic demand for auditing Assurance services Types of audits Types of auditors Certified public accountant (CPA) Learning Objectives: 1  Describe auditing. 2  Distinguish between auditing and accounting. 3  Explain the importance of auditing in reducing information risk. 4  List the causes of information risk, and explain how this risk can be reduced. 5  Describe assurance services and distinguish audit services from other nonassurance services. 6  Differentiate the three main types of audits. 7  Identify the primary types of auditors. 8  Describe the requirements for becoming a CPA. Week 2: CPA profession CPA firms (activities, structures) Regulations and regulatory bodies (SOX, PCAOB, SEC and AICPA) Auditing standards (International v.s. US) Organization of U.S. auditing standards Quality control Learning Objectives: 1  Describe the nature of CPA firms and what they do. 2  Describe the structure of CPA firms. 3  Understand the role of the PCAOB and the effects of the Sarbanes-Oxley Act on the CPA profession. 4  Summarize the role of the Securities Exchange Commission in accounting and auditing. 5  Describe the key functions performed by the AICPA. 6  Understand the role of international auditing standards and their relation to U.S. auditing standards. 7  Use U.S. auditing standards as a basis for further study. 8  Identify quality control standards and practices within the accounting profession. Week 3&4: Audit reports Standard unmodified opinion audit report Critical Audit Matters Emphasis-of-Matter Explanatory Paragraph /Nonstandard Report Wording Modification to the Opinion in the Audit Report Materiality Auditor’s decision process Learning Objectives: 1  Describe the parts of the standard unmodified opinion audit report for nonpublic entities under AICPA auditing standards. 2  Specify the conditions required to issue the standard unmodified opinion audit report. 3  Understand the Critical Audit Matters 4  Describe the circumstances when Emphasis-of-Matter Explanatory Paragraph /Nonstandard Report Wording can be used in the audit report 5  Identify the types of audit reports that can be issued when an unmodified opinion is not justified. 6  Explain how materiality affects audit reporting decisions. 7  Draft appropriately modified opinion audit reports under a variety of circumstances. 8  Determine the appropriate audit report for a given audit situation. Week 5: Professional ethics Professional ethics AICPA Code of Professional Conduct Independence Rule – Rule 200 SEC and SOX on independence Learning Objectives: 1  Distinguish ethical from unethical behavior in personal and professional contexts. 2  Explain the importance of ethical conduct for the accounting profession. 3  Describe the purpose and content of the AICPA Code of Professional Conduct. 4  Understand Sarbanes-Oxley Act and other SEC and PCAOB independence requirements and additional factors that influence auditor independence. Week 6&7: Audit responsibilities and objectives Objective of Conducting an Audit of Financial Statement Management’s responsibilities Auditor’s responsibilities Financial statement cycles Management assertions General and specific audit objectives How the objectives are met Learning Objectives: 1  Explain the objective of conducting an audit of financial statements and an audit of internal controls. 2  Distinguish management’s responsibility for the financial statements from the auditor’s responsibility for verifying those statements. 3  Explain the auditor’s responsibility for discovering material misstatements due to fraud or error. 4  Identify the benefits of a cycle approach to segmenting the audit. 5  Describe why the auditor obtains assurance by auditing transactions and ending balances, including presentation and disclosure. 6  Distinguish among the management assertions about financial information. 7  Link transaction-related audit objectives to management assertions for classes of transactions. 8  Link balance-related audit objectives to management assertions. 9  Explain the relationship between audit objectives and the accumulation of audit evidence. Week 8: Audit evidence Nature of audit evidence Four audit evidence decisions Persuasiveness of audit evidence Types of audit evidence Audit documentation Learning Objectives: 1  Contrast audit evidence with evidence used by other professions. 2  Identify the four audit evidence decisions that are needed to create an audit program. 3  Specify the characteristics that determine the persuasiveness of evidence. 4  Identify and apply the eight types of evidence used in auditing. 5  Understand the purposes of audit documentation. Week 9&10: Audit planning and materiality Analytical procedures Common ratios Types of analytical procedures Audit planning Accept client and perform initial audit planning Understand client’s business and industry Conduct preliminary analytical procedures Materiality Materiality for financial statements as a whole Determine performance materiality Estimate misstatement and compare with preliminary judgement Learning Objectives: 1  Know the types of analytical procedures and their purposes. 2  Compute common financial ratios. 3  Discuss why adequate audit planning is essential. 4  Make client acceptance decisions and perform initial audit planning. 5  Gain an understanding of the client’s business and industry. 6  Perform preliminary analytical procedures. 7  Apply the concept of materiality to the audit. 8  Make a preliminary judgment about what amounts to consider material. 9  Determine performance materiality during audit planning. 10  Use materiality to evaluate audit findings. Week 11: Assessing the risk of material misstatement Overview of audit risk Audit risk Risk assessment procedures Considering fraud risk Identification of significant risks Audit risk model Assessing acceptable audit risk Assessing inherent risk Relationship of risks to evidence and factors influencing risk Learning Objectives: 1  Define risk in auditing. 2  Distinguish the different types of risk assessment procedures. 3  Understand important auditor considerations related to the risk of material misstatement due to fraud. 4  Describe the auditor’s responsibility to identify significant risks. 5  Describe the audit risk model and its components. 6  Assess acceptable audit risk. 7  Consider the impact of several factors on the assessment of inherent risk. 8  Discuss the relationship of risk to audit evidence. 9  Discuss how materiality and risk are related and integrated into the audit process. Week 12&13: Audit of Internal control and control risk Internal control Overview Management’s responsibility Auditor’s responsibility COSO framework Obtain and document understanding Test of controls Report on controls Learning Objectives: 1  Describe the three primary objectives of effective internal control. 2  Contrast management’s responsibilities for maintaining internal control with the auditor’s responsibilities for evaluating and reporting on internal control. 3  Explain the five components of the COSO internal control   framework. 4  Obtain and document an understanding of internal control. 5  Assess control risk by linking key controls and control deficiencies to transaction-related audit objectives. 6  Describe the process of designing and performing tests of controls. 7  Understand how control risk impacts detection risk and the design of substantive tests. 8  Understand requirements for auditor reporting on internal control. 9  Describe the differences in evaluating, reporting, and testing internal control for nonpublic and smaller companies. Week 14: Fraud auditing Types of fraud Conditions for fraud Examples of fraud risk factors Assessing the risk of fraud Corporate governance oversights to reduce fraud risk Responding to the risk of fraud Specific fraud risk areas Learning Objectives: 1  Define fraud and distinguish between fraudulent financial reporting and misappropriation of assets. 2  Describe the fraud triangle and identify conditions for fraud. 3  Understand the auditor’s responsibility for assessing the risk of fraud and detecting material misstatements due to fraud. 4  Identify corporate governance and other control environment factors that reduce fraud risks. 5  Develop responses to identified fraud risk. 6  Recognize specific fraud risk areas and develop procedures to detect fraud. Week 15: Overall audit strategy and audit program Types of audit tests How to select types of audit tests Cost of the tests Types of evidence to select Relationship between different tests Evidence mix Design of an audit program Introduction TOC and substantive tests TDB Summary of the audit process Learning Objectives: 1     Use the five types of audit tests to determine whether the financial statements    are fairly stated. 2     Select the appropriate types of audit tests. 3     Understand the concept of evidence mix and how it should be varied in different circumstances. 4     Design an audit program. 5     Compare and contrast transaction-related audit objectives with balance-related audit objectives 6     Understand key evidence-related terms. 7     Integrate the four phases of the audit process. Week 16: Completing the audit Perform additional tests for presentation and disclosure Review for contingent liabilities and commitments Review for subsequent events Accumulate final evidence Evaluate results Issue audit report Communicate with audit committee and management Auditor’s responsibility for subsequent discovery of facts Learning Objectives: 1  Design and perform audit tests related to presentation and disclosure. 2  Conduct a review for contingent liabilities and commitments. 3  Conduct a post-balance-sheet review for subsequent events. 4  Design and perform the final steps in the evidence-accumulation segment of the audit. 5  Integrate the audit evidence gathered and evaluate the overall audit results. 6  Communicate effectively with the audit committee and management. 7  Identify the auditor’s responsibilities when facts affecting the audit report are discovered after its issuance. Week 17: Final exam Week 18: Grading
