课程简介（中文）

本课程旨在向外国留学生介绍中国传统思想，帮助学生理解中国文化传统。课程将介绍先秦道家与墨家思想。二者对中国人精神世界的形成产生了深远而持久的影响，并将继续塑造未来中国人的生命观念。本课程的大部分内容将聚焦于先秦道家学派，选取并介绍道家学派的代表性思想家（即老子与庄子），提供并讨论相关原始文献与二手研究资料。课程将以基于文本阐释的哲学分析为核心，也就是说，教学将围绕文本展开并聚焦具体问题。本课程高度鼓励采用比较研究方法并开展课堂讨论。 本课程的官方语言为英语。

课程简介（英文）

This course aims at giving a general account of Chinese traditional thoughts and helping students to understand Chinese tradition. It offers an introduction to the Pre-Qin Daoism and Mohism. They have had tremendous and lasting impacts on the formation of the Chinese mind and will continue to shape the Chinese outlook on life in the future. The most part of this course will concentrate on pre-Qin Daoist school. Representative thinkers (namely, Laozi and Zhuangzi) from the Daoist School will be selected and introduced. Original texts and secondary researches will be provided and discussed. This course will be focusing on philosophical analysis based on textual interpretations. That is to say, it will be textual focused and issues centered. The comparative approach and class discussions are highly encouraged in this class. English is the official language in this class.

教学目标

The teaching objective of this course is to enable students without a background in Chinese philosophy to understand the thoughts of Pre-Qin Daoism and Mohism, teach them methods for reading ancient Chinese classics, and familiarize them with key issues involved in discussing ancient Chinese philosophy.

教学方式

Lectures and class discussions

教学内容与进度安排

Course Schedule (Tentative) Week 1-2: Introduction to the course; Introduction to the Pre-Qin period & the Pre-Qin Daoist school Introduction to Laozi Issues: How to do Chinese Philosophy? (the methodology of Chinese philosophy); The identity of Chinese Philosophy (two sides of one coin) Readings: The Western Zhou History in The Cambridge History of China. Chapter 1-3 in Fung Yu-lan’s A Short History of Chinese Philosophy. Chapter 1-2 in The World of Thought in Ancient China. Bo Mou, “On some methodological issues concerning Chinese Philosophy: an introduction”. Antonio S. Cua, “The emergence of the history of Chinese Philosophy”, Routledge History of World Philosophies. Week 3-7: The Laozi: the Classics and its interpretation Issues: Dao (the Way) and its images (the infant, water, mother etc.), wuwei (nonaction), ziran (self-so), political philosophy (the good ruler), philosophy of language (linguistic engagement, the problem of utterance, the linguistic strategies), on wars, on human nature and desires, on knowing, the reversal is the movement of Dao Readings: “Part I The Laozi and the Bamboo Texts” in the Dao Companion to Daoist Philosophy. “Laozi”,Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Week 8-13: The Zhuangzi: the Classics and its Interpretation (mainly focusing on the first and the second inner chapters) Issues: the metaphors in the Zhuangzi, the relationship between heaven and human being, on life and death, on human nature, on fate, on uselessness, on knowledge, on political philosophy, on philosophy of language, on how Zhuangzi remarks on other schools of thought, on Skepticism, on Mysticism, on the way of self-cultivation. Readings: “Part II The Zhuangzi” in the Dao Companion to Daoist Philosophy. “Zhuangzi”, Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy. Dao Companion to Zhuangzi. Week 14-15: Introduction to Mohism; will read some selected text, e.g. on universal love (兼爱)
