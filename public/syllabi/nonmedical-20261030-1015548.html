课程简介（中文）

本课程专为国际本科生设计，旨在建立一个理解中国的“文化坐标系”。课程将围绕“‘中国’从何而来？”等一系列核心问题展开探索，融合历史、哲学、艺术等多元视角，聚焦于塑造中华文明的核心主题与“文化DNA”。本课程为你提供一套整体性的认知框架与分析工具，助你理解中国的历史逻辑，为深入解读“今日中国”打下坚实基础。

课程简介（英文）

This course is specially designed for international undergraduates to establish a "cultural coordinate system" for understanding China. We will explore a series of core questions—such as "Where does 'China' come from?", "How was its social order constructed?", and "What defines the spiritual world of its people?"—by integrating diverse perspectives from history, philosophy, art, and sociology. Together, we will focus on the core themes and "cultural DNA" that have shaped Chinese civilization. This course aims to provide you with a holistic cognitive framework and foundational analytical tools, enabling you to grasp China's unique historical trajectory and cultural logic, and thus find the essential "decoder key" for interpreting the complex phenomena of today's China.

教学目标

This course aims to enable students, through issue-based teaching and interactive experiences, to: Cultivate Values: Develop an open, inclusive, and critical cross-cultural perspective, enabling them to objectively view the complexities of Chinese history and culture. Impart Knowledge: Master core analytical frameworks for interpreting Chinese social and cultural phenomena, including but not limited to: the world order under the "Tianxia" (天下) concept, state capacity under the "grand unification" ideology, and elite selection under the culture of the "imperial examination system" . Develop Abilities: Foster a "cultural traceability" mindset, enabling them to proactively identify and trace the deep historical and cultural roots within contemporary Chinese social phenomena and cultural products. Through Project-Based Learning (PBL), students will enhance their interdisciplinary skills in information integration, teamwork, and creative application.

教学方式

Inquiry-based Modular Teaching: Guides learning through core questions, integrating multi-dimensional content from history, culture, and society. Interactive Lectures: Incorporates multimedia materials (maps, artifact photos, videos) to deliver engaging and illuminating instruction. Group Discussions & Debates: Focused on weekly topics, this method encourages students to share perspectives from their diverse cultural backgrounds. On-site & Localized Learning: Makes full use of resources on the Fudan campus and across Shanghai—such as museums and historical sites—for on-site instruction and field trips. Student-led Project Presentations: Students will work in groups to complete two thematic research projects and present their findings.

教学内容与进度安排

Module 1: Defining "China": The Foundation and Framework of a Civilization W1: How Do We Begin a Genuine Dialogue about China? (Introduction) W2: One Continent, Two Great Rivers: How They Shaped China's Earliest Worldview. W3: Are All "Chinese People" the Same? – The Diversity and Unity of the "Chinese Nation" (Zhonghua Minzu). Module 2: Constructing "Order": Ideological Blueprints and Institutional Pillars W4: The Hundred Schools of Thought: How Confucianism, Daoism, and Legalism Provided Ideological Frameworks for Governance. W5: From "Imperial Zhuangyuan" to "Gaokao Zhuangyuan": How has One Exam Shaped the Destiny of Millions in China? W6: From the First Emperor of Qin to China's High-Speed Rail: How "Grand Unification" Has Shaped China's Power and Speed. Module 3: Decoding the "Chinese Heart": The Emotional Code in Art and Life W7: From the Book of Songs to Jay Chou: How Chinese Emotions Are Written into Songs. W8: Why Can a Single Line on a Piece of Paper Be Worth a Fortune? – The Philosophy and Wealth within Chinese Calligraphy and Painting. W9: From Sun Wukong (the Monkey King) to The Wandering Earth: The Chinese Imagination of Heroes and Collectivism. W10: "Who Is in Charge of My Life?" – An Analysis of Love and Restraint in the Chinese Family. W11: Project Presentations (Midterm) Module 4: Connecting with the "World": Ancient China's Global Role W12: How Bustling Was the Silk Road? – The Openness and Cultural Fusion of the Han and Tang Dynasties. W13: Dream of a Maritime Empire – Zheng He's Voyages in the Ming Dynasty and the Global Flow of Silver. W14: The Collapse of the Celestial Empire: When Ancient China Encountered the Modern World. Conclusion and Assessment W15: Why China? – What Is Our Answer? W16: Final Examination
