美国文学选读

2025秋 · GEEF20014.02 · 叶如兰

来源：复旦教务系统教学大纲；正文整理。

课程简介（中文）

课程以19世纪至今的美国文学经典为核心，通过文本细读与文化阐释，系统剖析美国社会的发展脉络及其文化特性。在聚焦美国文学独特性的同时，注重与别国文学作品的比较研究，在跨文化视野中探讨美国文学与文化。通过理论与案例的结合，培养学生文学鉴赏能力、批判性思维和跨文化理解力，形成对文学、历史与社会的多维认知。

课程简介（英文）

This course is a survey of American culture through the examination of its literature and the major contributors to American literature from the 19th century to the present day United States. The intent is to study American society and the beauty, richness, diversity and complexity of its culture through the readings of literary works written by famous American authors. In making selections of the reading, attention has been given to theme, style and language. By taking this course, students will be exposed to what has been generally agreed on as the best literary works meanwhile acquire a functional literacy in American literature.

教学目标

本课程旨在引导学生： 理解美国文学发展脉络：系统掌握19世纪至今美国文学的主要流派、代表作家及其时代背景，认识文学作品与社会文化的互动关系； 培养文本分析能力：通过细读经典文本，提升对文学主题、叙事技巧和语言风格的鉴赏力，学会运用文学批评方法解读作品； 拓展跨文化视野：通过中美文学比较及多元文化视角，探究美国文学的独特性与世界文学的共通性，增强文化敏感性与包容力； 提升思辨与表达：在课堂研讨与写作训练中，发展批判性思维，能够清晰表达文学观点，形成独立见解。

教学方式

本课程采用多元化教学模式，注重互动与实践： 课堂讲授：梳理文学史脉络，解析核心文本的思想内涵与艺术特色； 小组研讨：围绕重点作品开展主题讨论，培养学生批判性思维与协作能力； 比较阅读：精选中美等跨国别文本进行对比分析，强化跨文化理解； 写作训练：通过文学评论、反思日志等写作任务，提升学术表达能力； 多媒体辅助：结合影视作品、历史影像、互文艺术作品等资源，拓展文学解读维度。

教学内容与进度安排

Week Module Learning Objective 1 From Colonial Literature to Literary Nationalism An introduction Anne Bradstreet: Contemplation Benjamin Franklin: The Autobiography Hector St. John de Crevecoeur: What is an American? To introduce students to the early stages of American literature, focusing on colonial works and the development of a distinct American literary voice. 2-3 From Literary Nationalism to Early American Romanticism Washington Irving: The Legend of Sleepy Hollow; Rip Van Winkle To explore the transition from a nationalistic literature to the early romanticism in America, highlighting key works that reflect this shift. 4 Transcendentalism Ralph Waldo Emerson: Self-Reliance Henry David Thoreau: Civil Disobedience; Walden To examine the works of major Transcendentalist writers, emphasizing their ideas on individualism, nature, and spirituality. 5 American Dark Romanticism Edgar Allan Poe: The Fall of the House of Usher; Black Cat; The Tell-Tale Heart Nathaniel Hawthorne: The Minister’s Black Veil To analyze the darker aspects of American romanticism through the works of Edgar Allan Poe and Nathaniel Hawthorne, focusing on themes of horror and the supernatural. 6 American Romantic Poetry William Cullen Bryant: Thanatopsis Henry Wadsworth Longfellow: A Psalm of Life Edgar Allan Poe: The Raven Walt Whitman: O Me! O Life!; Song of Myself Emily Dickinson: I Died for Beauty, but was Scarce; Because I Could Not Stop for Death To study the poetry of the American romantic period, understanding the themes of death, life, and the human experience. 7 American Realism Mark Twain: The Celebrated Jumping Frog of Calaveras County Henry James: The Real Thing Kate Chopin: The Story of an Hour Charlotte Perkins Gilman: The Yellow Wallpaper To delve into the works of American realist writers, examining their portrayal of everyday life and social issues. 8 American Naturalism Stephen Crane: The Open Boat Jack London: To Build A Fire To explore the naturalist movement in American literature, focusing on the themes of survival, human nature, and the environment. 9 Modernism I (Poetry) Carl Sandburg: Chicago Ezra Pound: In a Station of the Metro e.e.cummings: Pity this busy monster, manunkind Langston Hughes: Theme for English B To introduce students to modernist poetry, highlighting the innovative techniques and themes that define this period. 10-11 Modernism II (Fiction) Ernest Hemingway: Hills Like White Elephants Scott Fitzgerald: Winter Dreams William Faulkner: A Rose for Emily To examine modernist fiction, focusing on the narrative techniques and the exploration of complex human relationships. 12 Modern Drama Eugene O’Neil: Long Day’s Journey into Night Edward Albee: Who’s afraid of Virginia Woolf Tennessee Williams: A Streetcar Named Desire To study the works of modern dramatists, analyzing their portrayal of human psychology and social issues. 13 Literature after WWII Flannery O’Connor: A Good Man is Hard to Find Kurt Vonnegut: Harrison Bergeron Donald Barthelme: Engineer-Private Paul Klee Misplaces an Aircraft between Milbertshofen and Cambrai, March 1916 Sylvia Plath: Daddy Allen Ginsberg: Howl; America To explore post-war American literature, focusing on themes of disillusionment, social critique, and the human condition. 14 Multi-ethnic Literature Saul Bellow: A Silver Dish Isaac Singer: Gimpel the Fool Vladimir Nabokov: Symbols and Signs Sherman Alexie: This is What it Means to Say Phoenix Arizona To introduce students to the diverse voices in American literature, emphasizing the contributions of writers from various ethnic backgrounds. 15 Q&A 16 Final Examination 17 Grading Process 18 Submission of Final Grading
