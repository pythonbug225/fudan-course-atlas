课程简介（中文）

《艾滋病基础与临床（英）》是复旦大学临床医学院开设的专业选修课，系统讲解HIV感染的流行病学、发病机制、临床诊疗及防控策略。结合全球与中国流行病学数据、循证证据及最新指南，通过多媒体教学、案例研讨、翻转课堂等多元教学法，培养学生对艾滋病综合防治的临床思维与国际视野，为应对新发挑战提供理论与实践基础。

课程简介（英文）

In recent years, acquired immune deficiency diseases（AIDS） remains a major global , clinical treatment and research is facing new challenges. Improving medical students' understanding of these diseases, and enhancing the way of thinking and the ability to diagnosis and treatment of immune deficiency diseases is necessary for medical education.

教学目标

1. To provide students with information and knowledge about virology, immunology and epidemiology of HIV infection

2. To help students master the principles of diagnosing and treating HIV infection and its related opportunistic infections and tumors.

教学方式

Face-to-face teaching with multi-media system.

In-class assessment, ask and answer questions through the wechat group and e-learning system, turn-over class room and one-minute thinking.

教学内容与进度安排

Week1, Clinical presentations and diagnosis of HIV infection/AIDS

Content: ①Introducing the natural history of HIV infection: the acute infection stage, asymptomatic stage, and AIDS stage. The progression from initial HIV infection to its terminal stage is a protracted and intricate process, with a diverse array of HIV-related clinical manifestations occurring at different stages of the disease course. ②According to post-infection clinical presentations, the entire course of HIV infection can be divided into three stages: the acute infection stage, asymptomatic stage, and AIDS stage. This overview will detail the clinical manifestations of each period, focusing particularly on the AIDS stage—the ultimate stage of HIV-induced illness. In this phase, patients typically have CD4+ T lymphocyte counts below 200 cells/µL. The predominant clinical features include HIV-associated symptoms and signs, as well as a multitude of opportunistic infections and malignancies. Several illustrative clinical cases, accompanied by images, will be presented. ③Regarding the diagnosis of HIV infection/AIDS, the discussion begins with an overview of diagnostic techniques employed for HIV detection: antibody/antigen tests, nucleic acid amplification tests (NATs), viral isolation and culture, immune function assessments, and HIV drug resistance testing. Following this, diagnostic principles and criteria for each of the three stages (acute infection, asymptomatic infection, and AIDS) will be elucidated, with an emphasis on the diagnostic standards for the AIDS stage.

 

Week2, Virus and immunity

Content: ①Introduction to the human immune system; ②Overview of common viruses; ③ Mechanisms of antiviral immunity and viral counteraction; ④Introduction to the pathogenesis of HIV: molecular structure, genetic structure and Vaccine development for HIV.; ⑤Mechanisms by which HIV induces immunodeficiency in the host.

 

Week3, Rational use of antimicrobial agents in immunocompromised populations

Content: ①Antimicrobial agents for fungal, bacterial, viral and other infections. ②General principles of rational use of antimicrobial agents. ③Special Considerations for antimicrobial treatment in immunocompromised populations. ④Case discussion of antimicrobial treatment in immunocompromised hosts.

 

Week4, Antiretroviral therapy for HIV infection/AIDS

Content: ①Strategies for treating HIV infection; ②Categories, mechanism of action, and classification of antiretroviral drugs: there are over 40 drugs classified into seven main categories, including Nucleoside/Nucleotide Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NRTIs/NtRTIs), Non-Nucleoside Reverse Transcriptase Inhibitors (NNRTIs), Protease Inhibitors (PIs), Integrase Strand Transfer Inhibitors (INSTIs), Fusion Inhibitors (FIs), CCR5 Antagonists, and Capsid Inhibitors. These drugs work by interfering with different stages of the viral life cycle to prevent HIV replication. ③Timing of antiretroviral therapy (ART) initiation；④Antiretroviral treatment regimens, encompassing special considerations for specific patient populations such as those with comorbidities or drug resistance. ⑤Follow-up and monitoring: strategies for managing treatment failure, addressing side effects, and regular assessments to evaluate therapy effectiveness and patient health. ⑥ Recent advances and future trends of antiretroviral therapy.

 

Week5, Clinical managment for HIV drug resistance

Content: ①Introduction to the concept of HIV drug resistance; ②Methods for detecting HIV drug resistance: Genotypic testing, phenotypic testing; ③Determining HIV drug resistance; ④Management after Detection of HIV Drug Resistance: switching medications, salvage therapy, adherence counseling; ⑤Understanding low viral load and its management; ⑥Clinical case study.

 

Week6, Clinical mangement of HIV-associated invasive fungal infections

Content: ①Introduction to the diagnosis and antifungal treatment of common invasive fungal diseases (cryptococcal meningitis, Pneumocystis pneumonia, Talaromyces marneffei infection, aspergillosis, and oropharyngeal candidiasis); ②Antiviral therapy for patients co-infected with fungal diseases [timing of ART initiation, ART regimens, and immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS)]; ③Case discussion.

 

Week7, Diagnosis and treatment of HIV-associated viral infections

Content: ①Introduction to the diagnosis and antiviral treatment of common viral infections (cytomegalovirus infection, herpes zoster, Epstein-Barr virus infection, JC virus infection, HBV, HCV, and human papillomavirus infection); ②Antiretroviral therapy for HIV patients co-infected with viral infections [timing of ART initiation, ART regimens, and and immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS)]; ③ Case discussion.

 

Week8, Interactions between HIV and MTB

Content: ①Interactions between HIV and MTB: reciprocal facilitation of disease progression;

②Clinical manifestations and diagnosis of MTB infection; ③Management of co-infection: Anti-TB treatment regimens and duration, timing and strategies for antiretroviral therapy, handling of immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS), interactions between anti-TB and antiretroviral medications.

 

Week9, HIV and cancer

Content: ①AIDS defining malignancies (Non-hodgkin Lymphoma, Kaposi's sarcoma, cervical cancer); ②Screening and management of non AIDS defining tumors (lung cancer, liver cancer, anal tumors) in HIV-positive individuals; ③Treatment for patients with concurrent malignancies (chemotherapy, antiretroviral therapy); ④Prevention and management of complications; ⑤Case discussion.

 

Week10, Epidemiology of HIV infection/AIDS

Content: ①Basic concepts of Epidemiology: Definitions of HIV epidemiology data. ②HIV/AIDS Epidemic Globally and in China: changing trend of new infections, AIDS-related death and patients accessing antiretroviral treatment, key populations, WHO's Four 95% targets for HIV control, China HIV/AIDS epidemic. ③Cohort studies of HIV infection: definition of cohort studies, research interests, study design and data collection of several famous HIV cohort studies and their contributions; ④HIV and aging-related diseases: growing burden of aging-related comorbidities in HIV infection; aging of people living with HIV, HIV infection and premature aging; adverse effects of ART; other risk factors.

 

Week11, HIV control and prevention

Content: ①Three key aspects of infectious disease prevention and control: controlling the source of infection; interrupting transmission routes; protecting susceptible populations; ②Common strategies for preventing HIV infection; ③Social behavioral interventions; ④pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP) and post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP); ⑤Prevention of mother-to-child transmission (PMTCT); ⑥ Treatment as prevention strategy (TasP).

 

Week12, Broad neutralizing antibodies for HIV infection

Content: ① Structural & functional Basis of HIV bNAbs; ② Landmark bNAb discovery cases; ③ Engineering strategies for enhanced efficacy; ④ Clinical implementation and challenges; ⑤ Ongoing studies and future trends.

 

Week13, Examination

Content: ①Written examination (2 hours), open-book. Students are allowed to take with printed teaching materials, but electronic instruments are forbidden.
