课程简介（中文）

本课程为公共政策定量研究方法提供实践性导论，培养学生设计、执行和诠释实证分析的能力。课程涵盖研究基础、回归分析和因果推断三大模块，学生将学习如何构建政策相关的研究问题、运用STATA进行统计分析，并评估研究设计的有效性。通过作业、讨论和实际应用，课程强调动手实践，为学生开展严谨的独立研究及高效呈现研究成果奠定坚实基础。

课程简介（英文）

This course offers a practical introduction to quantitative research methods for public policy, equipping students with the skills to design, execute, and interpret empirical analyses. Through three modules—research foundations, regression analysis, and causal inference—students will learn to formulate policy-relevant questions, apply statistical techniques using STATA, and critically evaluate research designs. The curriculum emphasizes hands-on learning through assignments, discussions, and real-world applications, preparing students to conduct rigorous, independent research and effectively communicate findings.

教学目标

This course aims to provide a practical foundation in quantitative research methods for public policy. Students will learn to formulate research questions, design studies, and conduct empirical analyses using statistical software (STATA). The curriculum covers essential topics from research design and regression analysis to advanced causal inference techniques, equipping students with the skills to execute and critically evaluate policy-relevant research.

教学方式

Course Learning Activities This course employs a combination of instructional methods to achieve its learning outcomes: Lectures: Instructors will present core concepts of public policy research methods and provide live demonstrations of empirical analysis techniques using STATA. Readings & Discussion: Weekly required readings will be accompanied by reflection questions to guide your analysis. Class weeks will dedicate time to facilitated discussions on key themes, driven by 3-4 core questions posed by the instructor. Feedback: The instructor and teaching assistant (TA) will provide timely and constructive feedback on all submitted assignments to support your learning progress.

教学内容与进度安排

Course Schedule Module 1: Foundations of Research Methods Week 1: Course Introduction & The Logic of Inquiry Overview of course structure, objectives, and requirements. Introduction to the role and importance of empirical research in public policy and the social sciences. Reading: Neuman, L. W. (2007). Why do research. In Social research methods: Qualitative and quantitative approaches. Pearson Education India. Babbie, E. R. (2020). Chapter 1: An Introduction to Inquiry. In The practice of social research. Cengage Learning. Week 2: Research Design & Question Formulation This week covers the foundational steps of the research process, including how to develop a feasible and significant research question and select an appropriate methodological approach. Reading: Neuman, L. W. (2007). What Are the Major Types of Social Research. In Social research methods: Qualitative and quantitative approaches. Pearson Education India. Week 3: Measurement, Sampling, and Units of Analysis This week delves into core methodological concepts: constructing valid and reliable measures, understanding scales and indexes, designing sampling strategies, and correctly defining the unit of analysis. Reading: Babbie, E. R. (2020). Chapter 5: Conceptualization, Operationalization, and Measurement. In The practice of social research. Cengage Learning. Sheppard, V. (2020). Chapter 4: Measurement and Units of Analysis. In Research Methods for the Social Sciences: An Introduction. BCCampus. Neuman, L. W. (2007). Qualitative and Quantitative Sampling. In Social research methods: Qualitative and quantitative approaches. Pearson Education India. Week 4: Hypothesis Testing & Data Collection This week connects theory to data, covering the formulation and testing of hypotheses, interpretation of statistical significance (p-values), and linking these concepts to practical data collection techniques. Reading: Babbie, E. R. (2020). Chapter 16: Statistical Analyses. In The practice of social research. Cengage Learning. Sheppard, V. (2020). Chapter 6: Data Collection Strategies. In Research Methods for the Social Sciences: An Introduction. BCCampus. Module 2: Regression Analysis Week 5: Regression Fundamentals: Bivariate and Multiple Regression Introduction to regression analysis as a tool for modeling relationships. This week covers bivariate (simple) regression and extends to multiple regression, emphasizing interpretation. Reading: Wooldridge, J. M. (2000). Chapter 2: The Simple Regression Model. Introductory Econometrics: A Modern Approach. South Western College Publishing, USA, 529-569. Wooldridge, J. M. (2000). Chapter 3: Multiple Regression Analysis: Estimation. Introductory Econometrics: A Modern Approach. South Western College Publishing, USA, 529-569. Week 6: Modeling Conditional Relationships: Interaction Effects This week builds on multiple regression by teaching how to model, estimate, and interpret interaction effects to test whether a relationship depends on the value of another variable. Reading: Stock, J. H., & Watson, M. W. (2015). Chapter 8: Nonlinear Regression Functions. In Introduction to econometrics (3rd ed.). Pearson Education. Week 7: Introduction to Panel Data Regression Methods This week introduces panel data and its advantages. The focus is on the Random Effects (RE) model, its application, and the critical assumption that entity-specific effects are uncorrelated with predictors. Reading: Stock, J. H., & Watson, M. W. (2015). Chapter 10: Regression with Panel Data. In Introduction to econometrics (3rd ed.). Pearson Education. Fahrmeir, L., & Tutz, G. (2001). Random Effects Models. In Multivariate Statistical Modelling Based on Generalized Linear Models. Springer. Wooldridge, J. M. (2000). Chapter 3: Advanced Panel Data Methods. Introductory Econometrics: A Modern Approach. South Western College Publishing, USA, 529-569. Week 8: Advanced Regression: Models for Limited Dependent Variables Introduction to specialized models for non-continuous outcomes: Logistic regression for binary data, and Poisson/Negative Binomial regression for count data. Focuses on application and interpretation. Reading: Wooldridge, J. M. (2000). Chapter 17: Limited dependent variable models and sample selection corrections. Introductory Econometrics: A Modern Approach. South Western College Publishing, USA, 529-569. Module 3: Causal Inference Week 9: The Experimental Ideal and Endogeneity Introduction to randomized controlled trials (RCTs) as the gold standard for causal inference, covering key concepts like random assignment and validity. Contrasts this with the problem of endogeneity (omitted variable bias, reverse causality) in observational studies. Reading: Angrist, J. D., & Pischke, J. S. (2009). Chapter 3: Making Regression Make Sense. In Mostly harmless econometrics: an empiricist’s companion. Hill, A. D., Johnson, S. G., Greco, L. M., O’Boyle, E. H., & Walter, S. L. (2021). Endogeneity: A review and agenda for the methodology-practice divide affecting micro and macro research. Journal of Management, 47(1), 105-143. Week 10: Fixed Effects Models This week covers the Fixed Effects (FE) model, which controls for all time-invariant confounders in panel data, providing a powerful tool for causal inference in observational settings. Reading: Stock, J. H., & Watson, M. W. (2015). Chapter 10: Regression with Panel Data. In Introduction to econometrics (3rd ed.). Pearson Education. Angrist, J. D., & Pischke, J.-S. (2008). Chapter 5: Parallel Worlds: Fixed Effects, Differences-in-differences, and Panel Data. In Mostly Harmless Econometrics: An Empiricist's Companion. Princeton University Press. Week 11: Difference-in-Differences This week focuses on the DID design for evaluating policy impacts. Key topics include the core methodology, its application, and the critical parallel trends assumption—including how to test it. Reading: Angrist, J. D., & Pischke, J.-S. (2008). Chapter 5: Parallel Worlds: Fixed Effects, Differences-in-differences, and Panel Data. In Mostly Harmless Econometrics: An Empiricist's Companion. Princeton University Press. Week 12: Instrumental Variables Introduction to IV as a method to address endogeneity. Covers the concept of a valid instrument, the mechanics of Two-Stage Least Squares (2SLS) estimation, and interpretation of results. Reading: Angrist, J. D., & Pischke, J. S. (2009). Chapter 4: Instrumental variables in action: sometimes you get what you need. In Mostly harmless econometrics: an empiricist’s companion. Week 13: Matching Methods This week presents matching techniques (e.g., Propensity Score Matching, Coarsened Exact Matching) to construct counterfactual groups from observational data, aiming to approximate the conditions of an experiment. Reading: Austin, P. C. (2011). An introduction to propensity score methods for reducing the effects of confounding in observational studies. Multivariate Behavioral Research, 46(3), 399-424. Iacus, S. M., King, G., & Porro, G. (2012). Causal inference without balance checking: Coarsened exact matching. Political Analysis, 20(1), 1-24. Week 14: Review and Synthesis Q&A week to prepare for the final assessment. Week 15: Final Assessment Final examination.

  • 课堂表现Participation 10.0% This grade rewards active and meaningful contribution to class discussions. Evaluation will be based on the quality and frequency of your engagement.
  • 作业/实验/实践Assignment 40.0% Students will complete four research assignments. These are designed to develop the practical skills necessary to independently apply research methods and tools to answer policy-related questions.
