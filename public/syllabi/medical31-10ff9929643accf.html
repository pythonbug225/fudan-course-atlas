1. 课程简介 /Course Description

课程简介（中文） Course Description ( in Chinese ) | 《艾滋病基础与临床（英）》是复旦大学医学专业选修课，系统讲解HIV感染的流行病学、发病机制、临床诊疗及防控策略。结合全球与中国流行病学数据、循证证据及最新指南，通过多媒体教学、案例研讨、翻转课堂等多元教学法，培养学生对艾滋病综合防治的临床思维与国际视野，传递“以患者为中心”和国际合作应对流行病的理念，培养科学探索精神和人文关怀精神。

课程简介（英文） Course Description ( in English ) | "Acquired Immunodeficiency Syndrome：Basic and Clinical Science" is an elective medical course offered at Fudan University. It provides a systematic overview of the epidemiology, pathogenesis, clinical diagnosis and treatment, and prevention and control strategies of HIV infection. Integrating global and Chinese epidemiological data, evidence-based findings, and the latest clinical guidelines, the course employs diverse teaching methods such as multimedia instruction, case-based discussions, and flipped classrooms. It aims to cultivate students' clinical reasoning and global perspective in comprehensive HIV/AIDS prevention and care, convey the principles of "patient-centered" practice and international cooperation in addressing pandemics, and foster a spirit of scientific inquiry and humanistic care.

2. 教学目标 /Course Objectives （结合本校办学定位、学生情况、专业人才培养要求，应体现价值引领、知识传授、能力培养等方面的内容。）

Value Guidance Establish correct disease awareness and anti-discrimination consciousness, cultivate patient-centered medical humanistic spirit, and enhance sense of global health responsibility and international collaboration awareness. Knowledge Transfer Systematically master the epidemiology, virology, and immunological mechanisms of HIV infection, as well as clinical diagnosis and treatment, antiretroviral therapy, prevention and treatment of opportunistic infections and associated malignancies. Competency Development Enhance clinical reasoning and evidence-based practice capabilities through case analysis, flipped classrooms, scenario simulations, and other teaching activities, cultivating diagnostic and differential diagnostic skills, physician-patient communication techniques, and interdisciplinary team collaboration abilities.

3 . 教学方式 /Teaching Methods （应体现实现教学目标所采用的具体策略、方法和组织形式）

This course adopts a blended teaching model combining online and offline approaches, employing diverse pedagogical strategies to achieve the three overarching objectives of value guidance, knowledge transfer, and competency development. Classroom Instruction: We utilize multimedia presentations with animations and videos to systematically deliver knowledge on HIV epidemiology, virology, immunology, and clinical management. Each session incorporates one-minute reflection exercises to encourage students to actively review and consolidate their learning. Interactive Learning: Through flipped classrooms, students engage in self-directed pre-class preparation via videos and literature, followed by case-based discussions and group activities during class. Scenario simulations on the Superstar platform enable students to assume the role of physicians, developing clinical reasoning and physician-patient communication skills through virtual patient encounters. Collaborative Practice: Image library analysis and specimen classification tasks cultivate diagnostic and differential diagnostic abilities. Team-based flipped classroom activities foster collaboration and peer learning, with mutual feedback promoting self-reflection and continuous improvement. Assessment and Evaluation: A combination of formative assessment strategies—including pre-class questionnaires, in-class quizzes, and flipped classroom performance evaluation—ensures continuous monitoring of student progress. These assessments are designed to align with the course objectives, providing timely feedback to guide both teaching and learning. Ideological and Political Education Integration: Anti-discrimination education and medical ethics are seamlessly integrated throughout the course to cultivate a patient-centered medical humanistic spirit. Students are encouraged to develop a sense of global health responsibility and cross-cultural collaboration awareness, preparing them to address health disparities and promote equitable care for all patients regardless of their background or health status.

4.教学内容及进度安排 /Course Content & Schedule （ 按照教学周编排，应包含每节课教学内容及教学目标，如有作业及考核要求，请一并列明）

Week 1: History and Global Health Perspectives on HIV/AIDS Content: Introduction to the Course: Overview of course design, learning objectives, teaching schedule, and assessment methods. Pre-class questionnaire to understand students’ knowledge foundation and expectations. History of HIV/AIDS Discovery: Chronological account of the early AIDS cases in the 1980s and identification of HIV as the causative agent. Key milestones in diagnostic testing development and combined antiretroviral therapy. Global Epidemiology and Perspective: Current global statistics on HIV prevalence, incidence, and mortality. Discussion of global targets and the concept of “95-95-95.” Ideological and P olitical E ducation: Addresses fear, discrimination, and stigma surrounding HIV/AIDS, emphasizes the importance of caring for affected patients, and examines typical domestic and international case studies. Chapter Overview : This section serves as an overview of the entire course, providing a brief introduction to the knowledge points covered in this section and their correspondence with the subsequent chapters. Learning Objectives: Recall the course framework, expectations, and assessment methods through active participation in pre-class questionnaire State the historical timeline of HIV/AIDS discovery and early response Master the significance of “95-95-95” targets in global HIV response Outline the comprehensive knowledge structure of the entire course through chapter overview Cultivate interest in global health challenges and the importance of multidisciplinary collaboration Assess students' baseline knowledge of HIV/AIDS virology, immunology, epidemiology, and clinical management, identifying specific learning gaps to guide instructional focus. Week 2: Introduction of HIV Virology Content: Basic Concepts of Virology: Introduction to virus classification, structure, and replication strategies. Brief introduction to retrovirus. HIV Structure: Detailed examination of HIV components—envelope with glycoprotein spikes (gp120 and gp41), the conical capsid containing genetic material and essential enzymes. HIV Replication Cycle: Step-by-step progression through viral life cycle—binding to CD4 receptor and co-receptors (CCR5 or CXCR4), membrane fusion and entry, uncoating, reverse transcription converting RNA to DNA, integration of viral DNA into host genome, viral protein synthesis, assembly of new viral particles, and release and maturation. Connective Question: How should we treat HIV infection based on our knowledge of HIV virology.(Chapter 3) Learning Objectives: Recall the basic characteristics of virus structure and retrovirus classification Master HIV structural components and their functions Outline the complete HIV replication cycle from entry to release Name the three key enzymes targeted by antiretroviral medications Build curiosity about viral mechanisms and appreciate the scientific spirit in HIV research Week 3: Antiretroviral Therapy for HIV Infection Content: Goals of HIV Treatment: Understanding what successful treatment looks like—achieving and maintaining undetectable viral load, preserving immune function, preventing opportunistic infections, and improving quality of life. Introduction to the concept of “U=U” (undetectable equals untransmittable). How antiretroviral agents(ARVs) Works: Recall the knowledge about HIV replication cycle, introduce corresponding ARV class and explain how each class of ARVs work. Treatment Combinations: Introduction to the concept of “regimens” and the development of combined antiretroviral therapy. Starting Treatment: Trace the paradigm shift in HIV treatment timing—from deferring therapy until advanced AIDS to adopting immediate treatment upon diagnosis. Introduce the evidence supporting early intervention, such as reduced disease progression, decreased transmission risk, and improved patient outcomes. Connective Question: Why PLWH should receive a combination of ARVs.(Chapter 4) Learning Objectives: State the primary goals of antiretroviral therapy including “U=U” concept Master major antiretroviral drug classes and their basic mechanisms Explain why combination therapy is essential for effective HIV treatment Recognize the importance of treatment adherence and develop responsibility for patient outcomes Appreciate the paradigm shift from deferred to immediate treatment Week 4: HIV Viral Mutation and Drug Resistance Content: Understanding Viral Mutations: Basic concept of how viruses mutate during replication. Why HIV mutates particularly rapidly compared to other viruses. Mechanism of Drug Resistance: Introduce how mutations can allow viruses to escape drug effects. Drug pressure and the selection of resistant viral variants. Clinical Implications: What happens when drug resistance occurs—limited treatment options, continued disease progression, and transmission risk. Importance of adherence. Prevention of Resistance: How healthcare providers and patients work together to prevent resistance. The role of regular monitoring and early identification of treatment failure. Connective Question: How should we detect HIV viral and drug resistance? Learning Objectives: Explain why HIV mutates rapidly and how this contributes to resistance development Summarize the process by which drug resistance develops during treatment Master the clinical consequences of drug resistance for treatment options Recognize the importance of adherence and shared responsibility in preventing resistance Describe strategies for preventing and managing drug resistance Week 5: Viral Assessment for HIV Content: HIV Testing Methods: Comprehensive overview of diagnostic approaches—antibody tests (ELISA, rapid tests, Western blot), antigen/antibody combination tests, and nucleic acid tests (NAT). Understanding the “window period” and its clinical significance. Understanding Viral Load: What viral load measures and why it matters. How to track treatment effectiveness through viral load testing. Drug resistance test : Introduce different methods of drug resistance tests, focusing on genetic methods. Scenario simulation : Set up a scenario simulation on the Superstar platform. Students take on the role of a physician, with virtual patients receiving HIV testing. Based on different testing results, students provide appropriate advice to the patients. This activity allows students to apply what they have learned in this session and previous sessions, enabling teachers to assess students' comprehension and practical application abilities. Connective Question: If a person detected as HIV positive does not receive ART, what might happen to the body?(Chapter 5) Learning Objectives: Compare different HIV testing methods and their appropriate applications Master interpreting viral load results and understand its clinical significance Explain the concept of the “window period” and its importance in diagnosis Apply diagnostic knowledge through scenario simulation, taking on the physician role to develop patient-centered communication skills Build basic skills for interpreting laboratory results Week 6: Introduction of HIV Immunology Content: Basic Immunology Review: Fundamental concepts of the immune system for students with limited background—innate immunity (first-line defense) versus adaptive immunity (specific targeted responses), antibodies and antigens, and immune cells (T cells, B cells, macrophages). How HIV Infects Immune Cells: Detailed explanation of how HIV targets CD4+ T helper cells—the primary immune cells infected by HIV. Brief introduction of other immune components affected by HIV. Progressive Immune Damage: Understanding how HIV gradually destroys CD4+ T cells over time. The relationship between CD4 count and immune function. Immune Activation and Chronic Inflammation: How HIV infection causes ongoing immune activation even during treatment. The consequences of chronic inflammation for long-term health outcomes. Connective Question: What might happen when CD4 count decrease continuously?(Chapters 7-12). Learning Objectives: Recall fundamental immune system concepts including innate and adaptive immunity Master how HIV specifically targets and infects CD4+ T helper cells Summarize the progressive nature of HIV-induced immune damage over time Understand the relationship between CD4 count and opportunistic infection risk Appreciate the importance of immunology in understanding HIV pathogenesis Week 7: Immunology of HIV and Mycobacterial Co-infection Content: The Global Burden: Epidemiology of HIV-TB co-infection worldwide and in China. Why this combination represents a major global health crisis. How HIV Increases TB Susceptibility: Detailed explanation of how HIV damages the immune system in ways that specifically increase vulnerability to TB infection and disease. The role of CD4+ T cells in controlling TB. How TB Affects HIV Disease: Evidence showing that TB co-infection accelerates HIV progression. Biological mechanisms behind this bidirectional relationship. Immunological Battleground: Understanding the immune response to TB and how HIV impairs this response. Emphasize that HIV infection prevents the formation of complete granulomas. Connective Question: How to Recognizing TB in HIV Patients. Learning Objectives: Explain the bidirectional relationship between HIV and TB infection Understand how HIV impairs cell-mediated immunity to increase TB susceptibility Master how HIV prevents the formation of complete granulomas in TB infection Recognize the global burden of HIV-TB co-infection and develop awareness of health equity Understand why HIV-TB represents a particularly challenging combination requiring integrated care approaches Week 8: Clinical Presentation and Management of HIV and MTB Content: Recognizing TB in HIV Patients: Common symptoms, signs and radiology of pulmonary and extrapulmonary TB in HIV-infected individuals. Diagnostic challenges and approaches. Treatment of TB in HIV: Basic principles of anti-TB therapy. The importance of completing the full treatment course. Standard treatment regimens and their duration. Immune Reconstitution Inflammatory Syndrome (IRIS): Introduction to IRIS—what it is, why it occurs, and how it is managed. Understanding the paradox of worsening symptoms despite effective treatment. Timing of Antiretroviral Therapy: When to start HIV treatment in patients with active TB. Balancing the need to treat both infections with complications such as IRIS. Connective Question: What else might occur when the immune system is severely impaired?(Chapters 9-12) Learning Objectives: Identify clinical manifestations of TB in HIV-infected patients Describe approaches to diagnosing TB in HIV patients Master the principles of TB treatment and the importance of treatment completion Recognize considerations for timing antiretroviral therapy in TB patients Understand IRIS and develop compassion for patients experiencing treatment complications Week 9: HIV-Associated Invasive Fungal Infections Content: Basic mycology: Introduces the definition and characteristics of fungi with a focus on four categories relevant to human health, including edible fungi, toxic fungi, pathogenic fungi and opportunistic fungi. Each category is illustrated with representative examples. HIV-Associated Invasive Fungal Infections : The clinical presentation diagnostic approach and treatment principles of typical fungal infections in people living with HIV, including Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia , cryptococcal meningitis, Talaromyces marneffei infection and visceral candidiasis. F lipped classroom activity ： an image library of various fungi and fungal disease specimens is made available to students prior to class Students are asked to complete two tiered tasks. 1)analyze and categorize images from the library identifying each specimen as edible fungi toxic fungi pathogenic fungi or opportunistic fungi. 2)a simplified case study is provided with patient history imaging findings and microscopy photos showing cyst structures. Students are expected to identify the causative organism, determine appropriate treatment , consider the underlying condition which is HIV infection, and propose diagnostic steps to confirm the disease. Connective Question: Are you ready for another flipped classroom activity about HIV-Associated Viral Infections? Learning Objectives: Classify fungi into four categories relevant to human health Master clinical features of major HIV-associated fungal infections Explain diagnostic approaches for common fungal opportunistic infections Apply knowledge through tiered image analysis and case study problem-solving Develop teamwork skills through flipped classroom collaboration Demonstrate understanding of previous chapters through flipped classroom performance, forming self-assessment and feedback Week 10: HIV-Associated Viral Infections Content: Viral Opportunistic Infections Overview: Understanding the range of viral infections that affect HIV patients. The role of immune surveillance in controlling viral reactivation. Typical opportunistic viral infections: Clinical presentation, diagnostic approach, and treatment principles of typical viral Infections in people living with HIV, such as herpesvirus family(CMV, EBV, VZV and HSV) infections, JC virus and human papillomavirus. Flipped Classroom Activity : An image library of various viral infections and related disease specimens is made available to students prior to class. Students are asked to complete two tiered tasks. 1) Analyze and categorize images from the library, identifying each specimen as CMV, HSV, VZV, EBV, HPV, or JC virus based on characteristic morphological and clinical features. 2) A simplified case study is provided with patient history, imaging findings, and laboratory results. Students are expected to identify the causative organism, determine appropriate treatment, consider the underlying condition which is HIV infection, and propose therapeutic steps to control the disease. Connective Question: Could viral infections lead to cancers? Learning Objectives: Recognize the spectrum of viral opportunistic infections in HIV patients Master clinical features of common viral opportunistic infections Understand PML presentation and management in immunocompromised patients Recognize the link between HPV and cancer risk in HIV patients Develop analytical skills through case study problem-solving and collaborative learning Engage in formative assessment by receiving peer and instructor feedback on flipped classroom performance, reflecting on learning gaps, and developing strategies for continuous improvement Week 11: AIDS-Defining Cancers Content: Why some cancers are enriched in PWH : Basic understanding of how immune surveillance prevents cancer development. How immunodeficiency increases cancer risk through loss of surveillance and increased oncogenic viral infections. Kaposi’s Sarcoma: Caused by human herpesvirus-8 (HHV-8). Clinical presentation—characteristic skin lesions and potentially internal involvement. Treatment approaches including antiretroviral therapy and chemotherapy. Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma: Various types affecting HIV patients. Clinical presentation, staging, and treatment principles. Role of EBV in some lymphomas. Prognosis improvement with modern treatment. Cervical Cancer: Caused by persistent HPV infection. Increased risk and more aggressive behavior in HIV-positive women. Screening recommendations and prevention strategies. Treatment approaches. Connective Question: Is there other important viral induced cancers? Learning Objectives: Master how immunodeficiency impairs immune surveillance and increases cancer risk Understand the virology basis of AIDS-defining cancers: HHV-8 causes Kaposi’s sarcoma, EBV is associated with lymphoma, HPV causes cervical cancer Explain how loss of immune surveillance allows oncogenic viruses to promote cancer development Recognize clinical features of major AIDS-defining cancers Master principles of prevention and early detection for AIDS-defining cancers Week 12: HIV-Associated Liver Disease Content: Liver Disease in HIV Patients: Overview of the spectrum of liver disease affecting HIV-positive individuals. The liver as a target of HIV-related complications and medication toxicity. Viral Hepatitis Co-infection: Prevalence, significance, prevention strategy and treatment principle of HBV and HCV co-infection, non-alcoholic fatty liver disease. Understanding the the impact of HIV on liver disease progression to fibrosis, cirrhosis and liver cancer. Small quiz : Students are asked to draw a chart to show the causal relation ship among HIV, HBV, HCV, NAFLD, fibrosis, cirrhosis and liver cancer. Connective Question: How could PWH overcome long-term complications and live a healthy life? Learning Objectives: Recognize the spectrum of liver disease affecting HIV-positive individuals Master the impact of HIV on viral hepatitis (HBV, HCV) progression Explain the relationship between NAFLD, fibrosis, cirrhosis, and liver cancer Identify modifiable risk factors and monitoring approaches for liver health Apply understanding through quiz by drawing causal relationship charts among HIV, viral hepatitis, NAFLD, fibrosis, cirrhosis, and liver cancer, demonstrating logical thinking and knowledge integration Week 13: Living a Healthy Life with HIV Content: Beyond Viral Suppression: Understanding that successful HIV treatment involves more than undetectable viral load. Overall Physical health, mental health, and social well-being as interconnected components. Key points of long term health management for PWH: Risk factors and prevention strategy for cardiovascular disease, osteoporosis and fractures, kidney failure, mental health and aging. Ideological and political education : Explores how to promote equal medical rights for people living with HIV, help them overcome long-term complications, and achieve sustained health. This includes introducing the chronic disease cohort study conducted by Fudan University School of Public Health, the one‑stop service and whole‑course management model at Shanghai Public Health Clinical Center, as well as promoting viral suppression occupational protection, and anti-discrimination efforts. Connective Question: Could PHW finally get free from the pain and stress caused by HIV/AIDS? Learning Objectives: Understand that comprehensive HIV care addresses physical, mental, and social well-being Master prevention and management strategies for common long-term complications Understand the importance of patient-centered care, multidisciplinary clinical collaboration, and the integration of public health surveillance with clinical practice Appreciate China’s achievements in HIV care including chronic disease cohort studies, one-stop service models, and whole-course management approaches Reflect on promoting equal medical rights and anti-discrimination efforts Week 14: Advances Towards a Cure for HIV Infection Content: The Cure Challenge: Why finding a cure for HIV is so Understanding the concepts of latent viral reservoirs—cells where HIV persists in a dormant state despite effective treatment. Types of Cure Approaches: Functional cure (controlled infection without medication) versus sterilization cure (elimination of all virus). The scientific rationale behind different cure strategies. Leading strategies such as shock and kill, block and lock, CAR-T,CRISPR and bNAbs. Ideological education: Showcases Fudan University’s achievements in HIV cure research while acknowledging significant setbacks such as the failure of Merck’s therapeutic vaccine, so as to help students understand the difficult and tortuous nature of scientific discovery, thereby encourages them to bravely explore scientific frontiers in their future careers. Connective Question: Beyond the individual level, how can we eliminate AIDS at the population and even global levels? Learning Objectives: Explain why HIV is difficult to cure including the concept of latent viral reservoirs Master the distinction between functional cure and sterilization cure approaches Name major cure strategies including shock and kill, block and lock, CAR-T, CRISPR, and bNAbs Appreciate the difficult and tortuous nature of scientific discovery through case studies of both achievements and setbacks Develop spirit of scientific exploration and perseverance for future careers Week 15: HIV Prevention and Control Content: Combination Prevention Approach: Understanding that effective HIV prevention requires multiple strategies working together. Biomedical, behavioral, and structural interventions as complementary approaches. Comprehensive HIV Prevention Strategies: This module covers HIV prevention through three approaches: biomedical methods (PrEP, PEP, U=U), behavioral strategies (counseling, condoms, harm reduction), and prevention of mother-to-child transmission (maternal treatment, cesarean delivery, infant prophylaxis). Ideological and political education : This course provides comprehensive ideological and political education for international students, focusing on HIV anti-discrimination and global collaboration. Through engaging discussions with CDC experts and HIV social workers, participants explore effective stigma reduction strategies and relevant legal frameworks. The program highlights China's commitment to global HIV prevention goals while fostering intercultural dialogue and mutual understanding. By addressing social and structural challenges, students develop a shared commitment to eliminating discrimination and strengthening collaborative partnerships worldwide. Learning Objectives: Explain the concept of combination prevention including biomedical, behavioral, and structural approaches Master biomedical prevention methods including PrEP, PEP, and U=U Recognize behavioral interventions and prevention of mother-to-child transmission strategies Understand the significance of eliminating discrimination and caring for patients in HIV control, and enhance awareness of equal treatment for all patients Recognize the value of global cooperation in eliminating AIDS, and cultivate the spirit of exchange and collaboration Week 16: Final Examination Content: Written examination (2 hours), open-book. Students are allowed to take with printed teaching materials, but electronic instruments are forbidden.
