课程简介（中文）

本课程围绕“智力（intelligence）”这一核心心理学构念，采用“经典理论 → 前沿交叉 → 社会应用”的递进结构，系统讲授智力的心理测量学基础与关键争议，并强化三条前沿主线：认知神经科学证据、跨文化智力比较与测评公平、以及人工智能与人类智力的关系。 课程在保留经典心理测量框架（如一般智力g、层级模型、信度与效度、测验标准化等）的同时，引导学生把这些框架迁移到真实世界的评估问题中（教育与选拔、健康老化与临床特殊人群、以及AI时代的评估与治理）。 课程采用项目式学习（PBL）与结构化同伴评审，培养学生在跨文化与AI情境下进行“可测量、可解释、可公平”评估方案设计与批判性论证的能力。

课程简介（英文）

This course examines intelligence through a progressive structure—classic foundations → frontier intersections → societal applications. While preserving core psychometric frameworks (e.g., g and hierarchical models, reliability/validity, standardisation), the course strengthens three frontier strands: cognitive neuroscience, cross-cultural comparison and measurement fairness, and AI in relation to human intelligence. Students will learn to evaluate claims about intelligence using rigourous measurement reasoning and to translate theory into practice across applied settings (education and selection, health/ageing and special populations, and assessment governance in the AI era). The course includes project-based learning (PBL) and structured peer review to develop students’ ability to design and critique assessments that are measurable, interpretable, and fair under cross-cultural and AI-relevant constraints.

教学目标

By the end of the course, students will be able to: 1. Explain major psychometric theories of intelligence (e.g., g, hierarchical models; CHC as a practical mapping framework) and articulate their empirical bases and limitations. 2. Apply measurement principles (reliability, validity evidence, standardisation, interpretation boundaries; basic IRT concepts) to evaluate and design assessment tasks. 3. Integrate evidence across levels (psychometric, information-processing/mechanistic, and cognitive neuroscience evidence) when assessing scientific claims about intelligence. 4. Conduct cross-cultural reasoning about intelligence assessment, including measurement invariance/equivalence, differential item functioning (DIF), response styles, and fairness implications. 5. Analyse AI in relation to human intelligence as a measurement and evaluation problem (construct representation, generalization, contamination, and consequential validity). 6. Design an applied assessment proposal for a real-world target population or setting, including construct definition, task design, scoring/interpretation, validation plan, fairness/ethics safeguards, and revision based on peer feedback.

教学方式

In-person lectures + structured discussion: Weekly sessions combine instructor-led explanation with guided discussion prompts focused on interpretation, methodology, and implications. Project-Based Learning (PBL) milestone sessions (Weeks 5 and 12): In-class group presentations, lottery-assigned peer review, and instructor synthesis (details below). Pre-recorded micro-lectures (e-learning platform): Core instructional content for the two PBL milestones is delivered via short videos released in advance, allowing in-class time to be used for demonstration, critique, and synthesis. Student Research Spotlight (optional bonus; Weeks 10–11, 13-16): A brief student-led research introduction at the beginning of class to keep frontier intersections current and connected to measurement reasoning (details below). Detailed Description of PBL Sessions Purpose The PBL component trains students to translate classic measurement logic into an applied assessment design under real constraints: construct clarity, task design, scoring/interpretation, validity evidence, fairness (including cross-cultural issues), and ethics. The two milestones align with the course progression: Week 5 (v1) emphasises classic measurement foundations. Week 12 (v2) requires explicit integration of cross-cultural fairness and/or AI-era evaluation concerns, plus application feasibility. Team formation Students form groups of 3-4 by Week 3 (instructor may assist if needed). Each group selects one applied scenario from an instructor-provided list (see Point 3 below; or alternatively, propose one for approval). Required scenario types Social intelligence/social cognition assessment for autistic children Cross-cultural adaptation of a reasoning/working memory task (East Asia vs Euro-American contexts) Cognitive aging screening protocol in a community health setting AI capability evaluation framed through psychometric validity and contamination control Pre-class requirements (micro-lectures on e-learning) For each PBL milestone, the instructor releases short micro-lectures a week in advance. 教学内容与进度安排

Note. Week numbering assumes a standard 18-week term. Week 9 is reserved for the midterm exam. Weeks 17–18 are office hours. Classic Foundations → Measurement & Models Week 1 — Introduction: What is intelligence and why measure it? History, definitions, and the measurement problem; what IQ tests can and cannot claim. Week 2 — Psychometric frameworks I: g, hierarchical models, and construct modeling Factor-analytic logic; interpretation boundaries; how models relate to test design. Week 3 — Psychometric frameworks II: Reliability, validity, and standardisation Validity evidence menu; norming; fairness as part of validity; overview of IRT as a toolbox. Week 4 — Mechanisms and task performance: information-processing perspectives Working memory, processing speed, reasoning; task impurity and construct representation. PBL Milestone 1 Week 5 — PBL Session I (10%): Applied Assessment Proposal (v1) In-class group presentations + lottery peer review + instructor synthesis. (See PBL section in "Teaching Methods" for exact format and deliverables.) Frontier Intersections → Genes/Environment/Brain Week 6 — Cognitive neuroscience of intelligence I: brain systems and individual differences Network perspectives; correlates vs mechanisms; interpretability. Week 7 — Genetics, development, and stability across the lifespan Heritability and what it does/does not imply; development and aging; G×E framing. Week 8 — Environmental effects and methodological challenges Causal inference issues; education and social context; measurement and selection effects. Week 9 — Midterm Exam (25%) Coverage: dominant theories & methods; biological/neuroscience basis; environmental effects. Frontier Intersections → Culture & AI Week 10 — Cross-cultural intelligence comparison Collectivism vs individualism in assessment contexts; response styles; norming; measurement invariance and DIF as the backbone of fair comparison. (Research Spotlight begins this week.) Week 11 — AI and human intelligence: measurement and evaluation in the AI era Construct validity, generalisation, benchmark limitations, contamination, and fairness governance. PBL Milestone 2 Week 12 — PBL Session II (15%): Revised Proposal (v2) + Peer Review (See PBL section in "Teaching Methods" for exact format and deliverables.) Societal Applications → Education/Work/Health/Clinical + Ethics Week 13 — Intelligence in education and selection: uses, misuses, and policy implications Prediction vs causation; opportunity structures; fairness and consequences. Week 14 — Alternative constructs: creativity, critical thinking, and evidence standards What counts as evidence for a construct? Measurement feasibility and predictive utility. Week 15 — Extremes and special populations Giftedness, intellectual impairment, neurodiversity; ethical assessment practices. Week 16 — Culture, sex/gender, and intelligence: interpretation and societal impact Measurement equivalence, bias, and responsible communication of group differences.
