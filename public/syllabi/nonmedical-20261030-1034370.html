课程简介（中文）

本课程通过阅读二十世纪至二十一世纪的重要西班牙语小说作家作品，探讨当代西班牙语文学的发展及其文化意义。课程结合细读文本与文学理论问题，分析小说与短篇故事如何回应社会与文化变迁。课程将选读从豪尔赫·路易斯·博尔赫斯、罗贝托·波拉尼奥等经典作家到当代青年作家的作品，讨论叙事形式、作者观念、拉丁美洲文化场域的形成以及虚构与现实之间的关系。同时，课程也关注文学作品在全球范围内的传播，以及西班牙语文学如何参与当代全球文化互动。通过阅读与课堂讨论，学生将掌握分析当代叙事文学的基本方法，并理解西班牙语文学在全球文化语境中的位置。

课程简介（英文）

This course examines contemporary Spanish-language fiction through the reading of key authors from the twentieth and twenty-first centuries. Combining close literary analysis with broader questions from literary theory, the course explores how novels and short stories engage with cultural and social transformations. Through the study of writers ranging from canonical figures such as Jorge Luis Borges and Roberto Bolaño to younger contemporary authors, the course addresses questions related to narrative form, authorship, the development of cultural fields in Latin America, and the relationship between fiction and reality. It also considers the global circulation of literary works and the ways in which Spanish-language fiction participates in broader global cultural dynamics. Special attention is given to the historical contexts in which these texts were produced and to how they engage with wider debates about modernity, globalization, and literary innovation. Through reading and discussion, students will develop analytical tools to interpret contemporary narrative and situate Spanish-language cultural production within global contexts.

教学目标

The objective of this course is to provide students with analytical tools to study contemporary Spanish-language fiction. Through the close reading of novels and short stories by major twentieth- and twenty-first-century authors, the course introduces key concepts and debates in literary analysis and literary theory. Students will learn to examine narrative form, authorship, and the relationship between fiction and social reality while situating literary works within their historical and cultural contexts. Particular attention will be given to the development of literary and cultural fields in Latin America and to the global circulation of Spanish-language literature. The course also aims to strengthen students’ skills in reading, discussion, and academic writing in Spanish.

教学方式

The course combines lectures, close reading of literary and critical texts, and guided discussions. Each session introduces key authors, historical contexts, and critical concepts that help students analyze contemporary Spanish-language fiction. In general, classes are divided into two parts. In the first part, the teacher will present the assigned readings, introducing the author, the historical context, and the main interpretative questions. The second part of the class is devoted to the close analysis of the literary work. Through guided reading, the class examines narrative form, historical context, and the relationship between fiction and literary theory. Students are expected to actively participate in class discussions and to engage critically with the assigned readings. The course also includes individual research work, allowing students to explore a topic related to contemporary Spanish-language fiction and present their findings in written and oral form.

考核构成

  • 出勤Attendance 10.0% Students are required to attend at least 75% of the classes. Absences must be properly justified according to university regulations.
  • 课堂表现Participation 10.0% Active and informed participation in class discussions is essential. Students are expected to complete the required readings in advance and contribute regularly to debates, demonstrating engagement with both literary texts and theoretical materials.
  • 作业/实验/实践Assignment 10.0% Short analytical responses and research-oriented exercises submitted throughout the semester. These assignments are designed to support the development of the final research paper.
  • 课题论文Research Paper 70.0% The final assignment consists of a research paper (in Spanish) addressing a cultural and social problem related to Latin America. The paper must demonstrate theoretical grounding, close textual analysis, and independent critical argumentation.
