全球公共政策

2025秋 · GEEC20011.01 · 叶欣

来源：复旦教务系统教学大纲；正文整理。

课程简介（中文）

本课程旨在构建全球政策框架基础知识，系统介绍全球公共政策的核心概念、历史演进及关键行为体（国家、国际组织、非政府组织等），结合气候变化、全球健康、数字治理等当代议题，通过蒙特利尔议定书、新冠疫苗分配等真实案例，培养学生分析跨国政策挑战与治理机制的基础能力，同时涵盖技术治理、区域一体化等前沿内容，夯实全球治理认知基础。

课程简介（英文）

This course aims to build foundational knowledge of global policy frameworks, systematically introducing core concepts, historical evolution, and key actors (states, international organizations, NGOs, etc.) of global public policy. Combining contemporary issues such as climate change, global health, and digital governance, it cultivates students' basic capabilities to analyze transnational policy challenges and governance mechanisms through real-world cases like the Montreal Protocol and COVID-19 vaccine distribution. It also covers cutting-edge topics such as technological governance and regional integration to solidify the cognitive foundation of global governance.

教学目标

本课程教学目标紧密契合“扎根中国、放眼全球”的办学定位，立足培养具有全球视野与中国情怀的公共政策人才。通过系统讲授全球政策框架核心知识，帮助学生建立跨学科知识体系；以蒙特利尔议定书等案例为载体，培养政策分析、跨国比较与批判性思维能力；融入气候变化等全球性议题，引导学生树立可持续发展理念，理解中国在全球治理中的角色，实现价值引领、知识建构与实践能力的三维培养目标。

教学方式

该课程采用案例驱动、互动探究与实践应用相结合的教学方式，通过多元策略实现三维教学目标： 知识建构：跨学科案例浸润式教学 以蒙特利尔议定书、新冠疫苗分配等真实案例为载体，通过 “概念讲解 + 政策对比 + 数据可视化”，系统拆解全球政策框架的历史逻辑与运行机制。 每周配套学术文献阅读，结合课堂讲授，帮助学生建立从理论到实践的知识迁移能力。 能力培养：互动探究与比较分析 采用 “小组讨论” 形式，引导学生运用跨国比较思维分析政策失灵与治理创新。 通过情景模拟强化对多元行为体互动机制的理解。 价值引领：中国视角与全球议题融合 专题切入 “中国与全球治理”，引导学生理解中国在全球公共产品供给中的角色，渗透 “全球视野 + 中国情怀” 的价值导向。 结合社会运动案例，穿插讨论政策公平性与社会正义，培育可持续发展理念。 实践强化：本地化项目输出 期末要求学生完成 “本地案例研究 + 可视化分析”，通过课堂展示与期末报告，将全球治理理论与本土实践结合，提升政策分析与表达能力。

教学内容与进度安排

Week 1-4: Core Concepts and Historical Context Week 1: Introduction to Global Public Policy Define globalization, GPP, and governance; analyze transboundary issues (e.g., climate change, pandemics). Contrast successful (Montreal Protocol) and failed (global carbon tax) policies; discuss institutional dynamics. Reading: Hunter et al. (2010), Ch. 1-2 (simplified excerpts); Kahnie & Biermann (n.d.), Introduction (abridged). Week 2: Historical Evolution of Global Governance Trace the rise of post-WWII institutions (UN, WTO); discuss Cold War impacts and 2008 financial crisis lessons. Compare G20 and UN roles in economic governance; highlight structural limitations (e.g., inequality). Reading: Ikenberry (2018), summary overview; Brown et al. (n.d.), WHO history highlights. Week 3: Key Actors in Global Policy Classify states, IGOs (e.g., WHO), and non-state actors (e.g., Greenpeace); analyze their roles in EU chemical policies. Discussion: NGO negotiations on climate treaties. Reading: Gutner (n.d.), Ch. 1-2 (introductory chapters); Birn (n.d.), Gates Foundation case summary. Week 4: Global Public Goods and Challenges Explain GPG characteristics (e.g., COVID-19 vaccines); visualize distribution inequality with World Bank data. Debate patent laws and COVAX access gaps; introduce GAVI’s financing models. Reading: Hardin (1968), simplified summary; Helfer (n.d.), IP policy overview. Week 5-8: Contemporary Issues and Policy Applications Week 5: Climate Change and Environmental Policy Introduce the Paris Agreement and EU Green Deal; contrast individual actions (e.g., recycling) with state policies (e.g., China’s carbon neutrality). Reading: Guttman et al. (n.d.), China-U.S. policy comparison infographics; Spector (2012), The Climate Fixers (adapted version). Week 6: Global Health Governance Trace COVID-19 response; critique vaccine nationalism and COVAX challenges; analyze WHO’s role. Reading: Farmer et al. (n.d.), Ch. 4 (simplified health equity overview); Packard (n.d.), Ch. 7-8 highlights. Week 7: Migration and Refugee Policies Categorize migration drivers (e.g., Syrian crisis); compare EU Schengen vs. Hungary’s policies; discuss UNHCR mandates. Reading: Cueto et al. (n.d.), WHO migration health brief; Guttman et al. (n.d.), China’s NGO case summary. Week 8: Economic Inequality and Global Policy Visualize wealth gaps with Credit Suisse data; introduce OECD’s minimum tax agreement; debate trade sanctions’ impacts. Reading: Orsini et al. (n.d.), regime complexes summary; Petersen (2024), inequality infographics. Week 9-12: Technology, Regions, and Case Studies Week 9: Technology and Global Governance Compare EU GDPR and China’s data laws; discuss TikTok’s content moderation challenges; introduce AI governance debates. Reading: Wagner (n.d.), Ch. 2 (tech governance overview); Bernot et al. (2024), TikTok case summary. Week 10: Social Justice and Global Movements Analyze #MeToo/BLM’s policy impacts; contrast anti-discrimination laws in the U.S., EU, and India. Reading: Gostin & Katz (n.d.), health regulations and equity; Young (n.d.), environmental regime effectiveness summary. Week 11: China and Global Governance Map BRI and AIIB’s roles; present Sri Lanka port case as a development success; avoid controversial debates. Reading: Sun & Ferris (n.d.), China’s NGO engagement summary; Zhang (n.d.), supply chain trust brief. Week 12: Regional Integration (EU, ASEAN) Compare EU supranational vs. ASEAN intergovernmental models; analyze RCEP’s trade growth data. Reading: Zhu (2023), ASEAN’s balancing act summary; Choi et al. (2024), NAFTA’s local impact infographics. Week 13-16: Conflicts, Emerging Challenges, and Presentations Week 13: Conflict and Peacebuilding Explore Congo’s mineral conflicts; discuss UN peacekeeping successes (e.g., Liberia) and challenges. Reading: Ostrom et al. (n.d.), Ch. 1 (commons governance overview); Ndikumana & Verick (2024), resource curse summary. Week 14: Emerging Challenges (Populism, AI) Link European populism to anti-globalization; discuss AI disinformation risks and Germany’s NetzDG. Reading: Alter & Meunier (n.d.), regime complexity summary; Wilson et al. (2020), populism and COVID-19 brief. Week 15-16: Student Presentations
