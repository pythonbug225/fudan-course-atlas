课程简介（中文）

本课程旨在为学生提供国际关系学科的学术发展历史以及主要理论的基础知识。课程将采用历史唯物主义的方法，将国际关系主要理论流派的诞生与发展放在其历史背景中来讨论。课程既从社会科学视角，介绍西方主流国际关系理论与方法，也从思想史角度为学生提供对理论的语境化、历史化理解。通过这种方法希望学生能获得认识国际关系理论的批判视角。

课程简介（英文）

This course aims to provide students with a foundational understanding of the academic development history of the discipline of International Relations (IR) and the fundamental knowledge of its main theories. The course will employ a historical materialist approach, discussing the emergence and development of the major theoretical schools of IR within their historical contexts. It will introduce mainstream Western IR theories and methodologies from a social science perspective, and also offer students a contextualized and historicized understanding of these theories from the viewpoint of intellectual history. Through this approach, it is hoped that students will gain a critical perspective on understanding IR theories.

教学目标

1. To acquire basic understandings of major schools of International Relations.

2. To acquire a historical materialistic understanding of the development of IR as a social science discipline.

3. To develop a basic sense of dialectic thinking in understanding international politics 

4. To acquire basic ability to critical review IR theories

教学方式

Lecture and seminar

  • Stephen Walt, “International Relations: One World, Many Theories,” Foreign Policy, No. 110 (Spring 1998), pp. 29-46. PDF
  • David Singer, “The Level-of-Analysis Problem in International Relations,” World Politics, Vol. 14, No. 1 (1961), pp. 77-92.

  • John Mearsheimer, The Tragedy of Great Power Politics (New York: W.W. Norton, 2001/2014), Chapters 1–2 (core structural arguments).
  • Burchill et al., Theories of International Relations (Palgrave Macmillan, 2013), Chapter 2.

Week 3: English School and International "Society"

  • Barry Buzan, An Introduction to the English School of International Relations: The Societal Approach (Cambridge: Polity, 2014), selected chapters.
  • Hedley Bull, The Anarchical Society: A Study of Order in World Politics, 3rd ed. (London: Palgrave Macmillan, 2002), key sections on international society.

Week 4: Liberalism and International "Institution"
