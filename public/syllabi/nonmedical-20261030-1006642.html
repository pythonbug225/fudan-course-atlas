Commercial Banking

来源：复旦教务系统教学大纲；正文整理。

课程简介（中文）

Money and banking are fundamental to the functioning of modern economies. Commercial banks play a central role in credit creation, monetary transmission, business cycles, and financial crises. The 2008 Global Financial Crisis and more recent disruptions—such as the Evergrande crisis and the collapse of Silicon Valley Bank—highlight how banking institutions can both amplify and respond to economic shocks.

课程简介（英文）

Money and banking are fundamental to the functioning of modern economies. Commercial banks play a central role in credit creation, monetary transmission, business cycles, and financial crises. The 2008 Global Financial Crisis and more recent disruptions—such as the Evergrande crisis and the collapse of Silicon Valley Bank—highlight how banking institutions can both amplify and respond to economic shocks.

教学目标

By the end of the course, students will be equipped to critically analyze the commercial banking sector, understand its systemic importance, and engage with current policy debates. The course also aims to prepare students for independent research in banking, macro-finance, or financial regulation.

教学内容与进度安排

Week 1: Introduction: commercial banks in China and Global Topics: How important are banks in the economy? • Global financial crisis (2008): Subprime lending, securitization, Lehman collapse, spillover effects through global banking channels • China's 2010s real estate boom: Shadow banking, LGFVs, Evergrande Suggested Reading: • Bernanke, Ben S. "The courage to act: A memoir of a crisis and its aftermath." (2015). • Gertler, M., and S. Gilchrist. 2018. What Happened: Financial Factors in the Great Recession. Journal of Economic Perspectives 32:3–30. • Hachem and Song (2016), “Financing Real Estate in China,” NBER WP • Fang, Min, Libin Han, Zibin Huang, Ming Lu, and Li Zhang. Place-based land policy and spatial misallocation: Theory and evidence from China. University of Toronto, Department of Economics, 2022. Week 2: Financial intermediation, credit creation & payments Topics: How do banks make and move money? • Financial intermediation: Deposits vs. loans, Net Interest Margin (NIM) • Credit creation & money multiplier • Payments systems: CNAPS vs. Alipay/WeChat; Fedwire vs. SWIFT Suggested Reading: • Gorton, Gary and George Pennacchi (1990), “Financial Intermediaries and Liquidity Creation,” Journal of Finance • Stiglitz, J. E., and A. Weiss. 1981. Credit Rationing in Markets with Imperfect Information. American Economic Review 71:393–410. • de Meza, D., and D. C. Webb. 1987. Too Much Investment: A Problem of Asymmetric Information. Quarterly Journal of Economics 102:281–292 Week 3: Bank balance sheets and risk management Topics: What bank reports reveal about risk and stability • Balance sheet structure: Assets, liabilities, capital • Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR), Loan-to-Deposit Ratio (LDR) • Maturity mismatch and liquidity provision • Basel I–III: Tier 1/2 capital, leverage ratios • China's Basel implementation: Case of CCB • Liquidity metrics: LCR, NSFR • Bank stress testing frameworks Suggested Reading: • Diamond, Douglas and Philip Dybvig (1983), “Bank Runs, Deposit Insurance and Liquidity,” Journal of Political Economy • Holmstrom, Bengt, and Jean Tirole. "Financial intermediation, loanable funds, and the real sector." the Quarterly Journal of economics 112, no. 3 (1997): 663 691. • Diamond, D. W., and R. G. Rajan. 2001. Liquidity Risk, Liquidity Creation, and Financial Fragility: A Theory of Banking. Journal of Political Economy 109:287 327 • Allen, F., and D. Gale. 2000. Financial Contagion. Journal of Political Economy 108:1–33. Week 4: Monetary policy & regulatory Impact Topics: How central banks and regulation influence bank behavior • PBOC tools: RRR, MLF, LPR, and interest rate transmission • Regulatory arbitrage: WMPs, trust loans • Macroprudential regulation: Three Red Lines policy Suggested Reading: • Drechsler, Itamar, Alexi Savov, and Philipp Schnabl. "Liquidity, risk premia, and the financial transmission of monetary policy." Annual Review of Financial Economics 10, no. 1 (2018): 309-328. • Ongena, Steven, and David C. Smith. "Bank relationships: a review." Performance of financial institutions: Efficiency, innovation, regulation 221 (2000). • 易纲. "再论中国金融资产结构及政策含义." 经济研究 3 (2020): 4-17. Week 5: Global banking Topics: how commercial banks function as global systemically important financial institutions (G-SIFIs), their role in cross-border finance, and comparative lessons between China and other major economies (U.S., EU, Japan) • Global Intermediation & Payment Systems: Eurodollar markets, syndicated loans (e.g., HSBC’s global lending); China’s offshore RMB (CNH) market vs. USD dominance. • Payment Systems: SWIFT, CIPS • Currency Risks: Dollar-denominated debt in emerging markets (e.g., Evergrande’s USD bonds) Suggested Reading: • Cetorelli, Nicola, and Linda S. Goldberg. "Banking globalization and monetary transmission." The Journal of Finance 67, no. 5 (2012): 1811-1843 • Cetorelli, Nicola, and Linda S. Goldberg. "Liquidity management of US global banks: Internal capital markets in the great recession." Journal of International Economics 88, no. 2 (2012): 299-311. • Bruno, Valentina, and Hyun Song Shin. "Cross-border banking and global liquidity." The Review of Economic Studies 82, no. 2 (2015): 535-564. • Miranda-Agrippino, Silvia, and Hélene Rey. "US monetary policy and the global financial cycle." The Review of Economic Studies 87, no. 6 (2020): 2754-2776. Week 6: The Future – Fintech & New Challenges Topics: Disruption and adaptation in modern banking • Fintech developments: Digital yuan, MYbank, online credit scoring • Open banking and API integration • Risks and benefits of digital disruption Suggested Reading: • Gambacorta, L., Huang, Y., Li, Z., Qiu, H., & Chen, S. (2020). “Data vs collateral”, Working Paper, BIS. • Gorton, G. B., Ross, C. P., & Ross, S. Y. (2022). “Making Money ”(No. w29710). National Bureau of Economic Research.
