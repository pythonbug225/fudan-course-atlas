1. 课程简介 /Course Description

课程简介（中文） Course Description ( in Chinese ) | 分子影像学作为一门交叉学科的前沿，能够揭示生物分子和细胞层面的内在机制，为药物研发和医学诊疗提供前所未有的洞察。课程采用全英文线上线下混合教学模式，通过课堂讲授、讨论和实践练习相结合，提供对分子影像及其应用的基础知识。重点强调了影像技术和探针在生物制药研发、疗效实时评估以及人工智能分析集成在加速药物发现中的关键作用。

课程简介（英文） Course Description ( in English ) | As a cutting-edge interdisciplinary field, molecular imaging can reveal the intrinsic mechanisms at the biological molecular and cellular levels, offering unprecedented insights for drug development and medical diagnostics. The course adopts a hybrid online-offline teaching model in English, combining lectures, discussions, and practical exercises to provide foundational knowledge on molecular imaging and its applications. It emphasizes the critical roles of imaging technologies and probes in biopharmaceutical R&D, real-time efficacy evaluation, and the integration of AI analysis to accelerate drug discovery.

2. 教学目标 /Course Objectives （结合本校办学定位、学生情况、专业人才培养要求，应体现价值引领、知识传授、能力培养等方面的内容。）

通过本课程，学生将全面了解分子成像及其在药物研发中的应用，并重点关注人工智能 (AI) 驱动的数据分析的变革性作用。学生将探索成像技术如何在识别和验证药物靶点、评估药物疗效以及实时监测治疗反应方面发挥重要作用。通过深入研究前沿进展，包括人工智能驱动的药物研发以及细胞疗法和放射性药物等新兴领域，学生将深入了解该行业。此外，本课程还将培养学生必备的科学沟通技能，使他们能够有效地传达研究成果，并最终为这一跨学科领域的发展做出贡献。

3 . 教学方式 /Teaching Methods （应体现实现教学目标所采用的具体策略、方法和组织形式）

本课程采用混合式学习方法，将在线预习与线下课堂互动相结合。鼓励学生在课堂上提问并进行深入讨论，同时在线平台也促进了持续的交流和知识共享。期中考试要求学生进行全面的英语演示，展示他们对课程材料的理解和运用。期末考试以一篇内容丰富的论文作为结题的起点，要求学生进行深入研究，并撰写一篇关于影像和药物开发的专业论文（3000-5000字），并运用英语进行深入分析和深入思考。

4.教学内容及进度安排 /Course Content & Schedule （ 按照教学周编排，应包含每节课教学内容及教学目标，如有作业及考核要求，请一并列明）

Chapter 1: General Information of Molecular Imaging (2 courses) Content: The definition of molecular imaging The relationship between the conventional imaging and molecular imaging The history, development and future of molecular imaging Aims: Learning the basic information of the molecular imaging, the relationship between the conventional medical imaging and molecular imaging, the advantages and potential applications of molecular imaging in drug discovery. Chapter 2: Basic principle of imaging modalities (8 courses) Content: X-ray image Computer tomography (CT) Ultrasonic imaging (UI) Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI） Positron emission tomography (PET) Single photon-emission computed tomography (SPECT) Optical imaging Aims: Learning the basic theory, imaging features and application ranges of different imaging modalities used in clinical diagnosis, comparing the advantages and disadvantages of different imaging modalities, predicting their potential development in molecular imaging. Chapter 3: Imaging contrast media used in medical diagnosis (6 courses) Content: Contrast agents used in MRI Radiotracers used in PET and SPECT Fluorescent probes used in optical imaging Contrast media used in ultrasonic imaging SERS probes and their mechanism Photoacoustic imaging probes Multimodal imaging media The applications of the multimodal imaging probes used in clinic Aims: Learning the basic theory of contrast media mediated signal enhancement, the role of contrast media played in diagnosis of major human diseases, how to increase the targeting specificity of the contrast media, how to prepare the novel contrast media with high sensitivity, low toxicity and good biocompatibility. Chapter 4: The applications of image-guided therapy (6 courses) Content: Image-guided drug delivery Image-guided surgery Image-guided diagnosis with assistance of endoscope The technologies and probes used in image-guided therapy Aims: Learning the role of imaging played in evaluating the disease development stages before the treatment, dynamically monitoring the bio-distribution of the drug in vivo during the treatment and real-timely evaluating the therapeutic efficacy after the treatment. Chapter 5: The application of molecular imaging in drug discovery (6 courses) Content: The application of imaging technology in pharmacokinetic study; The application of imaging technology in Clinical 0 phase; The application of imaging technology in monitoring therapeutic efficacy. The application of imaging technology in cell therapies, for example, iv vivo visualization of chimeric antigen receptor (CAR)-T cells . Aims: Learning the role of imaging from the initial targeting domain selection to tracking the drug dynamic distribution in animal models to the finally real-timely evaluating the therapeutic responses of the testing drug. Chapter 6: The development and clinical practice for radiomics (4 courses) Content: The definition of radiomics; Origin and development of process for radiomics; Common research methods of radiomics; The clinical application for radiomics with existing operating procedures. Radiopharmaceuticals for diagnosis and therapeutics. Aims: Combining with the current status of imaging, learning the concept of radiomics, and the relationship between them. Understand the content and role of radiomics in tumor diagnosis and efficacy evaluation, and make a prospect for its development. Chapter 7: The development of artificial intelligence (AI) and imagingomics (4 courses) Content: The application of artificial intelligence in molecular imaging; The application of AI in drug screening, for example, the use of AI and fluorescence imaging in the discovery of drugs that regulate mitochondria. Imagingomics : technology, application and prospect. Aims： Familiarize students with radiomics and artificial intelligence; Encourage the involvement of students in these evolving areas in the future. The midterm exam requires students to deliver a comprehensive English presentation demonstrating their understanding and application of course material. The final exam culminates in a substantial thesis, demanding students to conduct in-depth research and write a professional paper (3000-5000 words) on imaging and drug development, drawing upon English language materials for informed analysis and insights. The midterm presentation accounts for 30% of the final grade, and the paper accounts for 70% of the final grade. The final grade will be transformed to A/A-/B+/B/... The paper is completed in English and includes the title, abstract, keywords, main text, and references. The reference materials are mainly from recent years, with no less than 15 articles. (1) 90-100 points: The paper has a rigorous structure, fluent writing, thorough analysis, clear and creative viewpoints, and a standardized format. (2) 80-89 points: The paper has a clear structure, smooth writing, thorough analysis, clear viewpoints and a certain degree of originality, and the format basically meets the standards. (3) 70-79 points: The structure of the paper is reasonable, the text is fluent, the analysis is deep, the viewpoints are clear, and the format basically meets the standards. (4) 60-69 points: The structure of the paper is not reasonable enough, the text and analysis have a certain depth, the viewpoints are relatively clear, and the format basically meets the standards. (5) Below 60: The structure of the paper lacks hierarchy, the text is not smooth enough, the analysis lacks depth, there are no clear viewpoints, and the format does not comply with standards.
