课程简介（中文）

This course investigates key concepts in international finance. Topics include the balance of payments; saving and investment; international debt; international interest rates and exchange rates; financial crises; and China’s role in the global economy.

课程简介（英文）

This course investigates key concepts in international finance. Topics include the balance of payments; saving and investment; international debt; international interest rates and exchange rates; financial crises; and China’s role in the global economy.

教学目标

This course aims to: 1. Provide students with a fundamental understanding of key concepts and theories in international macroeconomics. 2. Introduce students to contemporary topics and emerging issues in international finance. 3. Develop students' research skills, including critically evaluating existing literature, presenting academic work effectively, collaborating in research teams, and defining and organizing a research project.

教学内容与进度安排

Week 1 Topic: Global Imbalances Contents: • How big are international transactions in goods, services, and financial assets for the United States, China and other countries? • Does the United States have a trade deficit or a trade surplus with the rest of the world? What about China, Europe, and Latin America? • Is the United States an external debtor or an external creditor? • How have the trade balance and the international asset position of the United States, China and other countries evolved over time? Reading: 1. Schmitt-Grohe, Uribe and Woodford (2021) Ch.1 2. Caballero, Farhi and Gourinchas, 2008, An equilibrium model of “Global Imbalances” and low interest rates, 98(1): 358-393. Homework: In-class assignment 1 Week 2 Topic: Current Account Contents: •Develop a framework to address whether trade and current account deficits/surplus are sustainable in the long run. •Build a model of an open economy to study the determinants of the trade balance and the current account. •Study the response of the trade balance and the current account to income shocks. • Pay special attention to how those responses depend on whether the shocks are perceived to be temporary or permanent. Case: The Global Savings Glut and the U.S. Current Account Deficit, a 2005 speech by Ben Bernanke; Guest lecture on Feb 22 for EMF24 Reading: 1. Schmitt-Grohe, Uribe and Woodford (2021) Ch.2-3 2. American Economic Review, 102(3): 1-23. Homework: In class assignments 2, Group presentations 1-3 for EMF25E Week 3 Topic: Determinants of the Real Exchange Rate Contents: • How large and persistent are deviations from purchasing power parity? • What factors determine deviations from purchasing power parity?
