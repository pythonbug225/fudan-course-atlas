课程简介（中文）

本课程侧重于理解一个国家的整体经济表现：是什么导致了经济的增长，为什么经济活动的节奏会随着时间的变化而变化？这是一个非常大的话题：经济包含着数百万人、数十万公司，一个日益复杂的金融系统，以及角色不断变化的政府。

课程简介（英文）

This course focuses on understanding the performance of a country’s economy as a whole: what causes it to grow, and why the pace of economic activity varies over time. This is a very big topic: economies encompass millions of people, hundreds of thousands of diverse firms, an increasing complex financial system, and governments with ever-changing roles in the economy.

教学目标

（Including value, knowledge and ability objectives） Students will understand the growth and cyclicality of macroeconomic activity; and the role of firms, financial system and government in driving the economy.

教学方式

Examples of Class Participation include: Attending all lectures Asking questions of me Answering questions, either voluntarily or when called on

教学内容与进度安排

Course Schedule： Introduction and Long-Run Growth​ Core concepts of macroeconomics, data definitions, and dynamic general equilibrium modeling methods​ Stylized facts of economic growth and core logic of the Solow Growth Model​ Evolution of macroeconomic thought and review of basic mathematical tools​ Microfoundations of Macroeconomics​ Intertemporal consumption and saving decisions of households (Two-Period Model, Euler Equation, Permanent Income Hypothesis)​ General equilibrium in endowment economies and production economies (determination of real interest rates, market clearing)​ Firm production, labor demand, investment decisions, and the principle of firm value maximization​ Money demand and monetary neutrality (MIU Framework, Fisher Relationship, Classical Dichotomy)​ First Quiz​ III. Medium-Run and Short-Run Macroeconomic Fluctuations​ Neoclassical Model (medium run): Aggregate supply-demand equilibrium and impacts of exogenous shocks New Keynesian Model (short run): Nominal rigidities and the IS-LM-AD-AS framework​ Policy non-neutrality and dynamic adjustments (output gap, Phillips Curve, optimal monetary policy)​ Classic paper studies: Monetary neutrality, expectation theory, and policy debates​ (Paper presentations will start around Week 8)​ Linkages Between the Financial System and Macroeconomics​ Financial intermediation, bank operation mechanisms, and the money creation process​ Fundamentals of asset pricing, bond market structure, and term structure of interest rates​ Corporate capital structure, stock market pricing, and financial bubbles​ Financial frictions, financial crises, and the Zero Lower Bound constraint​ Application of unconventional monetary policy and fiscal policy​ (Paper presentations will continue; the second quiz will be scheduled before the semester ends)
