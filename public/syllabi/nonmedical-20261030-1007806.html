课程简介（中文）

本课程专为在职工程管理硕士设计，考虑到学员的认知水平和学习特点，以经典英语诗歌为核心教学材料，搭配指导性文章。通过研读诗歌，学员能弥补诗歌学习空白，领略精妙语言技巧，提升英语运用能力与文学审美素养，实现语言能力与人文素养的双重提升，为工程管理能力注入文化活力。

课程简介（英文）

This course is designed for Master's students to enhance their English proficiency, critical thinking, and cultural literacy through an engagement with English and American poetry. We will use poetry not merely as literary artifacts, but as powerful tools for exploring complex ideas, cultural narratives, and the nuances of the English language. Through close reading, active discussion, and reflective writing, students will deepen their understanding of themes such as identity, society, nature, and the human condition, while refining their ability to express sophisticated ideas in English.

教学目标

本课程专为在职工程管理硕士量身打造。考虑到学员兼具工程专业背景与碎片化学习特征，课程以经典英语诗歌为核心教学材料，搭配深度解析文章，助力学员填补诗歌学习空白。在价值引领上，通过诗歌中坚韧、协作等主题传递正向理念；知识传授方面，带领学员研习诗歌语言技巧、文化内涵；能力培养维度，提升英语表达与文学鉴赏能力，打破专业思维定式。最终实现语言能力与人文素养的协同发展，为学员成长为兼具国际视野与文化底蕴的工程管理人才奠定基础。

教学方式

教学以教师讲解为引领，教师系统剖析经典英语诗歌，解读词汇语法、创作背景与文化内涵，并结合工程管理专业特点，挖掘诗歌与创新思维、团队协作等领域的关联。课堂互动采用小组讨论、问答、朗诵分享等形式，激发学员主动性，培养其团队协作与批判性思维能力。课后，教师精选主题相关的诗歌、评论及传记等材料，学员利用碎片化时间阅读，并完成阅读笔记与思考任务。教师通过点评阅读成果，集中讲解共性问题，实现课堂内外联动，强化学习效果。

教学内容与进度安排

Week 1: Course Orientaion Readings: Article: "Freewriting" by Peter Elbow Poem: Robert Frost, "The Road Not Taken" Focus: Introduction to the course. We will practice "freewriting" to overcome writer's block and then apply this to "The Road Not Taken," discussing our own interpretations. Week 2: The Individual vs. The World Readings: Emily Dickinson, "I'm Nobody! Who are you?" Maya Angelou, "Still I Rise" Focus: Comparing and contrasting two powerful declarations of self, one of quiet rebellion (Dickinson) and one of defiant empowerment (Angelou). Week 3: Singing America – The Ideal and the Industrial Readings: Walt Whitman, "I Hear America Singing" Carl Sandburg, "Chicago" Focus: This week, we explore two powerful odes to American identity. We'll contrast Whitman's optimistic, harmonious vision of individual workers with Sandburg's raw, muscular, and proud celebration of a modern, gritty industrial city. Week 4: A Deeper River – Voices of Protest and Heritage Readings: Langston Hughes, "I, Too, Sing America" Langston Hughes, "The Negro Speaks of Rivers" Focus: We analyze how Langston Hughes writes back to the mainstream American narrative. "I, Too" is a direct response to Whitman, while "The Negro Speaks of Rivers" offers a profound alternative history, connecting Black identity to a deep, timeless global heritage. Week 5: The Nation and Its Heroes Readings: Walt Whitman, "O Captain! My Captain!" Emily Dickinson, "Success is counted sweetest" Focus: How does poetry shape our understanding of heroism, success, and loss? We'll examine Whitman's famous elegy for Abraham Lincoln and contrast it with Dickinson’s more cynical and private meditation on the nature of victory. Week 6: Nature as a Mirror for the Soul Readings: William Wordsworth, "I Wandered Lonely as a Cloud" Mary Oliver, "When I am among the trees" Focus: Comparing the Romantic view of nature (a source of spiritual memory) with a contemporary ecological consciousness (a place of grounding and belonging). Week 7: The Wisdom of the Wild Readings: Robert Frost, "Stopping by Woods on a Snowy Evening" Mary Oliver, "The Wild Geese" Focus: Nature as a source of philosophical questions. We will analyze the tension between obligation and desire in Frost's poem and discuss the message of self-forgiveness and belonging in "Wild Geese." Week 8: The Challenge of Modernity Readings: William Butler Yeats, "The Second Coming" William Butler Yeats, "The Lake Isle of Innisfree" Focus: Contrasting two responses to the modern world: one of apocalyptic anxiety ("The Second Coming") and one of escapist longing ("Innisfree"). Week 9: The World of Shakespeare – Love and Life Readings: William Shakespeare, "Sonnet 18: Shall I compare thee to a summer’s day?" William Shakespeare, "Sonnet 29: When, in disgrace with fortune and men's eyes" William Shakespeare, "All the world’s a stage" Focus: A deep dive into the master. We will analyze how Shakespeare uses the sonnet form to explore love's power to grant immortality and offer salvation from despair. We will then examine his famous meditation on the stages of human life. Week 10: The Music of English Readings: Robert Burns, "A Red, Red Rose" & "Auld Lang Syne" Anonymous, "Scarborough Fair" Shakespeare, "O, Mistress Mine, where are you roaming?" Focus: We will listen to musical recordings of these poems, focusing on rhythm, rhyme, repetition, and the emotional power of song lyrics. Week 11: The Ambiguity of Walls Reading: Robert Frost, "Mending Wall" Focus: A deep dive into a single, complex poem. We will debate the famous line: "Good fences make good neighbors." Week 12: Complicated Family Portraits Readings: Robert Hayden, "Those Winter Sundays" Theodore Roethke, "My Papa's Waltz" Focus: Analyzing how poets convey complex feelings—love mixed with fear, regret, and gratitude—without stating them directly. Week 13: Wisdom for Life – Finding Happiness and Meaning Readings: Carl Sandburg, "Happiness" W.H. Davies, "Leisure" Ella Wheeler Wilcox, "Solitude" Focus: We discuss poetry as a source of life lessons. Sandburg’s narrative poem offers a critique of intellectualism and finds joy in simple community, a theme echoed in "Leisure" and "Solitude." Week 14: Love, Loss, and Parenthood Readings: Christina Rossetti, "Remember" Kahlil Gibran, "On Children" Focus: We will contrast Rossetti’s Victorian meditation on love and death with Gibran’s famous modern spiritual advice on the nature of parenthood. Weeks 15-16: Synthesis and Final Projects Activity: These final weeks can be used for student presentations, in-depth workshops on any remaining poems, and a final reflective essay or project where students connect the themes from several poems to their own experiences or field of study.
