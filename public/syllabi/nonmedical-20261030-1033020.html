课程简介（中文）

The course Black Hole Physics provides a comprehensive and rigorous examination of black hole solutions within the framework of general relativity, beginning with the Schwarzschild black hole and extending to charged and rotating black holes. Students will explore the motion of test particles using geodesics and affine parameterization, as well as the role of symmetries and Killing vectors. The course delves into the physics of spherically symmetric collapse, introducing black holes, white holes, and the Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates, and includes a thorough analysis of concepts such as the event horizon, null hypersurfaces, Killing horizons, and surface gravity, culminating in a discussion of the Hawking temperature and the Tolman-Unruh temperature. Topics on charged black holes, including the Reissner-Nordström solution, and rotating black holes, particularly the Kerr solution and its associated phenomena such as the ergosphere and the Penrose process, will be covered. The course also addresses the formal definitions of energy and angular momentum in black hole spacetimes, black hole mechanics, and concludes with the study of Hawking radiation.

教学目标

The objective of the Black Hole Physics course is to provide students with a rigorous and comprehensive understanding of the theoretical and physical properties of black holes within the frameworks of general relativity and quantum field theory. Upon completion, students will be able to systematically analyze the key black hole solutions, including Schwarzschild, Reissner-Nordström, and Kerr, with a focus on their spacetime structures, geodesics, and symmetries. They will develop a thorough understanding of the event horizon, singularities, Killing vectors, and the thermodynamic properties of black holes, such as surface gravity and the Hawking temperature. The course will also address advanced concepts, including spherically symmetric collapse, Kruskal-Szekeres coordinates, Killing horizons, and the ergosphere, as well as energy extraction processes such as the Penrose process and super-radiance. In addition, students will engage with the laws of black hole mechanics, the phenomenon of Hawking radiation, and the associated information paradox. This course aims to equip students with the analytical tools and conceptual foundations necessary for advanced research in black hole physics, general relativity, and quantum gravity.
