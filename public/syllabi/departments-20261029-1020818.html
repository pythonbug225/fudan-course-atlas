课程简介（中文）

本课程旨在对博弈论——即战略决策的研究——的基本概念和工具进行严谨的介绍。以Robert Gibbons的《博弈论基础》（A Primer in Game Theory）为主要教材，学生将探索理性主体在各种情境下如何进行互动。课程涵盖四种主要类型的博弈：完全信息静态博弈、完全信息动态博弈、不完全信息静态博弈以及不完全信息动态博弈。我们将分析纳什均衡、子博弈精炼纳什均衡和贝叶斯纳什均衡等均衡概念，并将其应用于寡头竞争、讨价还价和拍卖等经典经济学问题中。

课程简介（英文）

This course provides a rigorous introduction to the fundamental concepts and tools of game theory, the study of strategic decision-making. Using Robert Gibbons' A Primer in Game Theory as the primary guide, students will explore how rational agents interact in various settings. The course covers four main types of games: static games of complete information, dynamic games of complete information, static games of incomplete information, and dynamic games of incomplete information. We will analyze equilibrium concepts such as Nash Equilibrium, Subgame Perfect Nash Equilibrium, and Bayesian Nash Equilibrium, applying them to classic economic problems like oligopoly competition, bargaining, and auctions.

教学目标

By the end of this course, students should be able to: Formalize Situations: Translate real-world strategic situations (from economics, politics, and daily life) into formal game-theoretic models. Solve Games: Apply appropriate solution concepts (Nash Equilibrium, Backward Induction, Bayesian Nash Equilibrium, Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium) to solve for optimal strategies. Analyze Information: Understand how the timing of moves and the availability of information affect strategic outcomes. Apply Theory: Use game theory to analyze economic phenomena such as Cournot and Bertrand competition, principal-agent problems, and signaling.

教学方式

Lectures: Core concepts and mathematical derivations will be presented in class using the whiteboard and slides. Problem Sets: Regular homework assignments to practice solving games and proving basic properties. Case Studies: Occasional discussion of real-world applications (e.g., OPEC production decisions, spectrum auctions) to illustrate theoretical concepts.

教学内容与进度安排

Part I: Static Games of Complete Information Week 1: Introduction to Game Theory. Normal-form representation. Iterated elimination of strictly dominated strategies. Week 2: Nash Equilibrium: Motivation and Definition. Pure strategy Nash Equilibrium. Week 3: Applications: Cournot Model of Oligopoly, Bertrand Model of Oligopoly, and the Tragedy of the Commons. Week 4: Mixed Strategy Nash Equilibrium. Existence of Nash Equilibrium. Part II: Dynamic Games of Complete Information Week 5: Extensive-form representation. Game trees and information sets. Backwards induction. Week 6: Subgame Perfect Nash Equilibrium (SPNE). The Stackelberg Model of Duopoly. Week 7: Two-Stage Games of Complete but Imperfect Information. Bank Runs. Week 8: Repeated Games: The Prisoner's Dilemma repeated twice vs. infinitely. Folk Theorem intuition. Week 9: Dynamic Games Application: The Rubinstein Bargaining Model. Week 10: Midterm Exam (Covering Parts I & II) Part III: Static Games of Incomplete Information Week 11: Introduction to Incomplete Information. Normal-form representation of Bayesian Games. Types and priors. Week 12: Bayesian Nash Equilibrium (BNE). Application: First-Price Sealed-Bid Auctions. The Revelation Principle (Introduction). Part IV: Dynamic Games of Incomplete Information Week 13: Perfect Bayesian Equilibrium (PBE). Requirement 1-4 and Signaling Games. Week 14: Application: Corporate Finance, Job Market Signaling (Spence Model), and Cheap Talk. Week 15: Refinements of PBE (Intuitive Criterion). Review for Final Exam.
