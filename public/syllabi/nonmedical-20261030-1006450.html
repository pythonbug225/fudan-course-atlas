中国文化史

来源：复旦教务系统教学大纲；正文整理。

课程简介（中文）

本课程旨在描绘中国传统文明的一些不同成就和鲜明特色。 它涵盖了中国从最早的时间到1911年清末的历史。 它考察了从中国历史上的形成时期到公元前221年中国统一以及随后的帝国王朝的主要政治、社会、经济、宗教和文化发展。 该课程是按时间顺序描述和以主题为中心的讨论的结合。 内容广泛，包括中国独特政治制度的起源和演变、儒家思想的演变及其在中国历史中的作用、佛教的到来和发展、中国与外界的关系、帝国后期的经济增长和文学艺术的发展等。

课程简介（英文）

This course is designed to portray some of the diverse achievements and distinctive characteristics of traditional Chinese civilization. It covers the history of China from the earliest time to the end of the Qing dynasty in 1911. It examines the main political, social, economic, religious and cultural developments from the formative period in Chinese history, through the unification of China in 221 BCE and the subsequent imperial dynasties. The course is a combination of chronological depiction and theme-focused discussion. It covers a great variety of topics ranging from the origins and evolution of a distinctive Chinese political system, the evolution of Confucianism and its role in Chinese history, the arrival and growth of Buddhism, the relation between China and the outside world, to the economic growth in late imperial period and developments in art and literature.

教学目标

To be able to identify major historical periods, ideas, people and events in Ancient Chinese history. To be able to construct interpretations of China’s past, identifying origins and causal factors, explaining issues in relation to particular historical contexts. To be able to craft and defend coherent and persuasive arguments both in written and oral forms.

教学方式

Lectures and discussion of historical materials (in English translation)

教学内容与进度安排

Session 1 Course Introduction What is China? (geography, languages, dynasties, etc). China’s contribution to the world civilization. The methods of studying Chinese history: traditional and modern historiography of China. The Origins of Chinese Civilization: Neolithic period. Session 2 The Beginning of Written History: The Xia, Shang and Western Zhou Dynasties. Readings: Ebrey, Chpt. 1; Mair, pp. 8-44. How did the ancient Chinese interpret the pre-history of China? What is the significance of the different interpretations in different periods? How do we know about the three dynasties? What were the main achievements and significant transitions during the 1300 years? What were the main features of this Chinese civilization? Session 3 Essential Political Ideas and the Philosophical Foundation: Eastern Zhou Readings: Ebrey, Chpt. 2; Mair, pp. 45-87, 99-129 (especially the Lyrics of Chu and Xun Zi) What were the main changes in politics, economy and society during this period? What were the main arguments of each school of thoughts? What were the features of Chinese political thinking, compared with other civilizations? Session 4 The Creation of the Bureaucratic Empire: Qin and Beginning of the Han Dynasty Readings: Ebrey, Chpt. 3; Mair, pp. 137-142 (Han Fei Zi), 147-173. Why did Qin succeed in consolidating the empire? What kind of political system was established during the period? What was State Confucianism? Session 5 NO Class, October 1 festival You can finish the readings from Mair on the previous historical periods (till p. 146) and look for the book for your book report Session 6 Flourishing Early Empire and Its Collapse: the Han dynasty Mair, pp. 179-225. Oral presentation on the Primary Materials in Translation in class: Victor H. Mair, Nancy S. Steinhardt, and Paul R. Goldin, Hawai’i Reader in Traditional Chinese Culture. Territorial expansion of Han Empire, its cultural achievements. What can be drawn from the comparison of Han Empire and Roman Empire? The reasons for the collapse of the Han Empire. Session 7 The Age of Division and the Intellectual Change: the period of Three Kingdoms, Western Jin, Northern and Southern Dynasties. Readings: Ebrey, Chpt. 4; Mair, pp. 251-264, 270-287. What were the main dynasties and their cultural characteristics? What were the new trends during the period? The new intellectual currents. Why did Buddhism become so popular in China at that time? Session 8 The Reunification of Sui and the Cosmopolitan Empire of Tang Readings: Ebrey, Chpt. 5; Mair, pp. 295-299 (Selections from the Platform Sutra), 340-367. Why did Sui succeed in unifying the country? Why did Sui soon fall apart? What were the main changes in politics and economy during Tang dynasty? What factors ended Tang? Session 9 Proto-modern? Economical and Technological Revolution in Song Dynasty, Five Dynasties and Northern Song periods Readings: Ebrey, Chpt. 6. Readings: Mair, pp. 380-389, 405-422 (Recollections of the Northern Song Capital), 426-428 (Zhu Xi). Session 10 Southern Song and Nomadic Rule of Northern China: Liao, Jin dynasties and the Mongol Conquest: establishment of the Yuan dynasty Readings: Ebrey, Chpt. 7; Mair, pp. 437-439 (Vernacular Paraphrases), 444-455 (Ni Zan) Session 11 Book review: oral presentation in class. Each student – 5 to 10 minutes Yuan dynasty and downfall of the Mongol rule Session 12 Ming Dynasty: Reestablishing the Chinese Empire Readings: Ebrey, Chpt. 8 (reign of the Ming Taizu); Mair, pp. 456-460, 467-504. Written book reviews are due in class. Session 13 End of the Ming and Manchu invasion of 1644. Readings: Ebrey, Chpt. 8 (end); Mair, pp. 517-524 (Li Yu), 539-542 (Feng Menglong). Session 14 China under the Manchus (till the Qianlong’s reign) Oral presentation on the Primary Materials in Translation, Victor H. Mair, Nancy S. Steinhardt, and Paul R. Goldin, Hawai’i Reader in Traditional Chinese Culture Readings: Ebrey, Chpt. 9 (till the end of the Qianlong’s reign/year 1796), Mair, pp. 567-573 (Yuan Mei), 607-613 (Biographies of Exemplary Women). Session 15 Crisis of the Qing Empire: From McCartney’s Delegation to the Opium Wars. Taiping Rebellion and New Unequal Treaties. Early Modernization and the Decline of the Dynasty. Readings: Ebrey, Chpt. 9 (ending part). Session 16 Writing the final paper; presentation on the topic of final paper in class; questions and answers Session 17 Writing the final paper
