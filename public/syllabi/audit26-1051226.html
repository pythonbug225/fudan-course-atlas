课程简介（中文）

本课程旨在介绍概率论的基础知识，包括古典概型、随机变量、随机变量的性质、常用分布、极限定理等内容，使学生掌握概率论的基础理论与基本方法，为后续课程的学习打好基础。

课程简介（英文）

This course will introduce the fundamental knowledge about probability, including classical probability model, random variables and their properties, common distributions, limit theory, etc. Students are expected to master the fundamental theory and basic methods in probability theory and build a solid foundation for later courses.

教学目标

Through this course, students will master the basic concepts of probability and statistics, understand its basic theories and methods, and thereby gain a preliminary grasp of the basic ideas and methods for dealing with random phenomena. Students will also develop the ability to use probability and statistical thinking to analyze and solve practical problems. At the same time, this course will lay the necessary knowledge and skill foundation for subsequent statistics courses and professional courses.

教学方式

This course is taught by the instructor, supplemented by classroom discussions and analysis of specific issues by students. Students review and summarize the content learned in class through homework assignments and apply the knowledge learned in class to solve problems.

教学内容与进度安排

Week 1: Course orientation, combinatorial foundations, and classical probability models

Session 1: Course introduction; the role of probability in data science; the basic counting principle; permutations; permutations with repeated objects.
Session 2: Combinations; multinomial coefficients; equally likely outcomes; classical probability calculations; inclusion-exclusion; 

Week 2: Random experiments, sample spaces, events, and probability axioms

Session 1: Random experiments, outcomes, sample spaces, events, and relationships and operations among events.
Session 2: Axioms of probability; complements, monotonicity, finite additivity, and other basic consequences; 

Week 3: Conditional probability, Bayes' formula, and independence

Session 1: Conditional probability, multiplication rule, law of total probability, and tree-based calculations.
Session 2: Bayes' formula; independence of events; repeated trials; examples involving diagnosis, classification, and reliability.

Week 4: Discrete random variables

Session 1: Random variables, probability mass functions, cumulative distribution functions, and functions of a discrete random variable.
Session 2: Bernoulli and binomial distributions; indicator variables; 

Week 5: Standard discrete distributions

Session 1: Geometric and negative binomial distributions; waiting-time interpretation and the memoryless property.
Session 2: Hypergeometric and Poisson distributions; binomial-Poisson approximation; modeling sampling without replacement and count data.

Week 6: Continuous random variables and standard continuous distributions

Session 1: Distribution functions, probability density functions, interval probabilities, and transformations of one continuous random variable.
Session 2: Uniform, exponential, gamma, and beta distributions; memorylessness; examples from waiting times and uncertain proportions.

Week 7: The normal distribution and one-variable transformations

Session 1: Normal and standard normal distributions, standardization, quantiles, and probability calculations.
Session 2: Distribution-function and change-of-variable methods for g(X); monotone, non-monotone, and piecewise transformations; inverse-CDF transformations; guided problem solving.

Week 8: Expectation and variance of a single random variable

Session 1: Expected value for discrete and continuous random variables; LOTUS; linearity of expectation; expectation of functions of a random variable.
Session 2: Variance, standard deviation, and basic moments; means and variances of the principal distributions studied in Weeks 4-7;

Week 9: Joint distributions, marginals, and conditional distributions

Session 1: Joint mass functions, joint density functions, joint cumulative distribution functions, and probabilities over regions.
Session 2: Marginal and conditional distributions for discrete and continuous random variables; 

Week 10: Independence and transformations of random vectors

Session 1: Independence of random variables; factorization criteria; functions of independent random variables.
Session 2: Transformations of two random variables, change of variables, and the Jacobian method.

Week 11: Sums, convolution, and order statistics

Session 1: Distribution of sums; discrete and continuous convolution; sums of independent standard distributions.

Session 2: Minimum, maximum, and order statistics; applications to ranking, reliability, and extreme observations.

Week 12: Conditional expectation, conditional variance, and mixture models

Session 1: Conditional expectation; conditioning identity; law of total expectation; examples involving mixtures and latent groups.
Session 2: Conditional variance and the law of total variance; random sums and mixture models; applications and problem-solving practice.

Week 13: Covariance, correlation, and multivariate structure

Session 1: Covariance and correlation; properties; linear combinations of random variables; uncorrelatedness versus independence.
Session 2: Covariance matrices, linear combinations of random vectors, the multivariate normal distribution, and interpretation of feature dependence.

Week 14: Moments, generating functions, and probability inequalities

Session 1: Moments, moment-generating functions, uniqueness and sums of independent random variables.
Session 2: Markov, Chebyshev, Chernoff, and a supplementary introduction to Hoeffding's inequality; relevance to finite-sample error control.

Week 15: Limit theorems

Session 1: Convergence in probability and almost sure convergence; weak and strong laws of large numbers.
Session 2: Central limit theorem, normal approximations, continuity correction, and interpretation for aggregated data.

Week 16: Monte Carlo methods, data-science applications, and course integration

Session 1: Uniform random numbers, inverse-transform and rejection methods, and Monte Carlo estimation, supported by an optional instructor demonstration.
Session 2: Integrated data-science cases; brief non-examinable overview of Markov chains and MCMC; final review. The final examination is scheduled separately.
