课程简介（英文）

Pathophysiology is a branch of medical science that combines the knowledge of physiology and pathology to explain the causes and pathogenesis of diseases. It addresses the occurrence, progression, and outcome of diseases to understand their essence, providing the experimental and theoretical foundation for clinical diagnosis, treatment, and prevention. Therefore, it serves as a bridge between basic medical science and clinical medicine, playing a crucial role in medical education.

教学目标

This course requires students to master the mechanism of every basic fundamental pathological process, the basic pathogenesis of disease in each part. Based on the training guidance for the students majoring in clinical medicine, students will be expected to obtain the following knowledge and skill after taking this course.

1. Basic theory and knowledge

1) Master the underlying mechanisms by which disease occurs and the resultant functional and metabolic alternations within the body.

2) Be familiar with the causes and conditions of disease.

3) Understand the pathophysiological basis for clinical diagnosis, treatment and prevention.

2. General capability and skill

1) Self-learning ability: Points will be explained and stressed in the class. Further reading will be instructed.

2) Skills of problem-solving and comprehensive analytical ability: Questions in actual clinical cases will be discussed.

3. Innovative ability

教学方式

Class teaching and class discussion on case study.

教学内容与进度安排

Week 1   Concept of Pathophysiology (3.5 classes, 160mins)

Introduction of pathophysiology

1)    What is pathophysiology？

2)    Why pathophysiology is important？

3)    What knowledge does pathophysiology cover？

4)    How do we pursue pathophysiological research?

General concept of disease

1)    Concepts of health and disease.

2)    Etiology of disease.

3)    Pathogenesis of disease.

4)    Process and outcome of disease.

 

Week 2 Disorders of Water and Sodium Metabolism (3.5 classes, 160mins)

1)    Volume, components, and distribution of water and sodium in human body.

2)    Dehydration (definition, etiology, pathogenesis, alternation of metabolism and function).

3)    Water excess (definition, etiology, pathogenesis, alternation of metabolism and function).

 

Week 3   Disorders of Potassium Metabolism (3.5 classes, 160mins)

1)    Potassium hemostasis (distribution, function and regulation).

2)    Hypokalemia (definition, etiology, pathogenesis, alternation of metabolism and function).

3)    Hyperkalemia (definition, etiology, pathogenesis, alternation of metabolism and function).

 

Week 4   Acid-Base Balance and Disturbances (3.5 classes, 160mins)

1)    Acid-base chemistry, regulation of pH, and laboratory tests.

2)    Metabolic acidosis and respiratory acidosis (definition, etiology, pathogenesis, compensation, alternation of metabolism and function).

3)    Metabolic alkalosis and respiratory alkalosis (definition, etiology, pathogenesis, compensation, alternation of metabolism and function); mixed acid-base disorders.

 

Week 5   Fever (3.5 classes, 160mins)

1)    General Description, Etiology.

2)    Causes and mechanisms.

3)    Alterations of Metabolism and Function, treatment.

 

Week 6 Stress and Related Disease (3.5 classes, 160mins)

1)    General Descriptions, stressor and neuroendocrine response.

2)    The cellular and humoral response; Alternations of metabolism and function.

3)    Stress and diseases; Basic principle of prevention and treatment for stress disorders.

 

Week 7 Hypoxia (3.5 classes, 160mins)

1)    Parameters of blood oxygen.

2)    Conception, classification, etiology and mechanisms of hypoxia.

3)    Alterations of metabolism and function in the body; And pathophysiological basis of prevention and treatment.

 

Week 8 Shock (3.5 classes, 160mins)

1)    The history of shock, and the cause, etiology and classification of shock.

2)    Stages of shock and their pathological changes.

3)    Alterations of Metabolism and function of shock; The conception of multiple organ dysfunction syndrome, SIRS, CARS and MARS; The pathophysiological basis of shock prevention and treatment.

 

Week 9 Disseminated Intravascular Coagulation (3.5 classes, 160mins)

1)    Overview of hemostatic system; Normal balance of coagulation-anticoagulation.

2)    The conception of DIC, and causes and mechanisms.

3)    Primary clinical presentations of DIC, Stages and types of DIC; Principles of prevention and treatment of DIC; The pathophysiological basis of DIC prevention and treatment; Explain the relationship between SHOCK and DIC.

 

Week 10 Ischemia-Reperfusion Injury (3.5 classes, 160mins)

1)    Introduction of Ischemia-reperfusion Injury (IRI); Etiology and influential factors of IRI; Mechanisms of increase of oxygen free radical during IRI; Alterations induced by oxygen free radical.

2)    Mechanisms of calcium overload during IRI; Alterations induced by calcium overload.

3)    Mechanism of ischemia-reperfusion injury induced by neutrophil activation; Functional and metabolic changes during IRI; Prevention and treatment principle.

 

Week 11 Heart Failure (3.5 classes, 160mins)

1)    Etiology, classification and compensatory and adaptive response of heart failure.

2)    Pathogenesis of heart failure: Systolic dysfunction; Diastolic dysfunction; Asymmetry and asynchronism in ventricular contraction and relaxation.

3)    Alterations of metabolism and function of heart failure; Pathophysiological basis of prevention and treatment.

 

Week 12 Respiratory Failure (3.5 classes, 160mins)

1)    Introduction; Etiology and pathogenesis.

2)    Mechanisms of alveolar hypoventilation.

3)    Mechanisms of disturbance of air exchange; Pathophysiological mechanism of clinical and laboratory manifestations; Examples of respiratory failure.

 

Week 13 Hepatic Failure (3.5 classes, 160mins)

1)    Introduction and Conception; Etiology, Syndromes of Hepatic insufficiency (Metabolic disorders, Water and electrolytes imbalance).

2)    Disorders in production of bile salts and elimination of bilirubin; Impaired kupffer cells function; Hepatic encephalopathy (conception, etiology, classification, Ammonia Intoxication) .

3)    False Neurotransmitters; Amino Acid imbalance; The Gamma-Aminobutyric Acid hypothesis; Precipitating factors of HE; Treatment.

 

Week 14 Renal Failure (3.5 classes, 160mins)

1)    Introduction and Acute Renal Failure (ARF): Quick review of renal physiology, biochemistry and anatomy.

2)    Definition of Renal Failure/insufficiency; ARF (Definition, Etiology, Pathogenesis, Clinical manifestation, Treatment principle).

3)    Chronic Renal Failure (CRF) and Uremia.

 

Week 15 Case Study (3.5 classes, 160mins)
