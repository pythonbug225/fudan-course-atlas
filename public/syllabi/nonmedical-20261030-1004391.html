课程简介（中文）

本课程旨在通过对美国文学的审视，纵览19世纪至今美国文化的风貌及其主要文学巨匠的贡献。课程意图通过研读美国著名作家所著的文学作品，深入探讨美国社会的内涵，以及其文化中蕴含的美感、丰富性、多样性与复杂性。在选材过程中，我们特别关注了作品的主题、风格与语言艺术。通过修习本课程，学生将接触到公认的文学经典之作，同时培养对美国文学的功能性理解与鉴赏能力。

课程简介（英文）

This course is a survey of American culture through the examination of its literature and the major contributors to American literature from the 19th century to the present day United States. The intent is to study American society and the beauty, richness, diversity and complexity of its culture through the readings of literary works written by famous American authors. In making selections of the reading, attention has been given to theme, style and language. By taking this course, students will be exposed to what has been generally agreed on as the best literary works meanwhile acquire a functional literacy in American literature.

教学目标

Students will be able to: Learn to appreciate literature by reading aesthetically Compare and evaluate various works of literature Improve their vocabulary skills primarily in the context of the literature Discern and analyze the rhetorical strategies authors employ and consider applying some of these strategies to enhance their own writing when appropriate.

教学方式

讲授为主，结合小组讨论 The class will be conducted in the form of discussion. Students must complete the readings to participate in the discussion. The instructor is responsible for directing the discussion by motivating students with critical reading questions. As this course will involve the student as a whole person in the classroom, allow for genuine response and develop student autonomy. There are no pre-determined answers to some of the designed tasks. Students will be encouraged to offer their individual interpretation or response instead of looking to the instructor for confirmation of "correct" answers. Discussions will be conducted through group and pair works so that students can negotiate the reconstruction of meaning with the text in an interactive way.

教学内容与进度安排

Week Module Teaching Goals 1 From Colonial Literature to Literary Nationalism An introduction Anne Bradstreet: Contemplation Benjamin Franklin: The Autobiography Hector St. John de Crevecoeur: What is an American? To introduce students to the early stages of American literature, focusing on colonial works and the development of a distinct American literary voice. 2 From Literary Nationalism to Early American Romanticism Washington Irving: The Legend of Sleepy Hollow; Rip Van Winkle To explore the transition from a nationalistic literature to the early romanticism in America, highlighting key works that reflect this shift. 3 Transcendent-alism Ralph Waldo Emerson: Self-Reliance Henry David Thoreau: Civil Disobedience; Walden To examine the works of major Transcendentalist writers, emphasizing their ideas on individualism, nature, and spirituality. 4 National Day Holiday 5 American Dark Romanticism Edgar Allan Poe: The Fall of the House of Usher; Black Cat; The Tell-Tale Heart Nathaniel Hawthorne: The Minister’s Black Veil To analyze the darker aspects of American romanticism through the works of Edgar Allan Poe and Nathaniel Hawthorne, focusing on themes of horror and the supernatural. 6 American Romantic Poetry William Cullen Bryant: Thanatopsis Henry Wadsworth Longfellow: A Psalm of Life Edgar Allan Poe: The Raven Walt Whitman: O Me! O Life!; Song of Myself Emily Dickinson: I Died for Beauty, but was Scarce; Because I Could Not Stop for Death To study the poetry of the American romantic period, understanding the themes of death, life, and the human experience. 7 American Realism Mark Twain: The Celebrated Jumping Frog of Calaveras County Henry James: The Real Thing Kate Chopin: The Story of an Hour Charlotte Perkins Gilman: The Yellow Wallpaper To delve into the works of American realist writers, examining their portrayal of everyday life and social issues. 8 American Naturalism Stephen Crane: The Open Boat Jack London: To Build A Fire To explore the naturalist movement in American literature, focusing on the themes of survival, human nature, and the environment. 9 Modernism I (Poetry) Carl Sandburg: Chicago Ezra Pound: In a Station of the Metro e.e.cummings: Pity this busy monster, manunkind Langston Hughes: Theme for English B To introduce students to modernist poetry, highlighting the innovative techniques and themes that define this period. 10 Modernism II (Fiction) Ernest Hemingway: Hills Like White Elephants Scott Fitzgerald: Winter Dreams William Faulkner: A Rose for Emily To examine modernist fiction, focusing on the narrative techniques and the exploration of complex human relationships. 11 Modern Drama Eugene O’Neil: Long Day’s Journey into Night Edward Albee: Who’s afraid of Virginia Woolf Tennessee Williams: A Streetcar Named Desire To study the works of modern dramatists, analyzing their portrayal of human psychology and social issues. 12 Literature after WWII Flannery O’Connor: A Good Man is Hard to Find Kurt Vonnegut: Harrison Bergeron Donald Barthelme: Engineer-Private Paul Klee Misplaces an Aircraft between Milbertshofen and Cambrai, March 1916 Sylvia Plath: Daddy Allen Ginsberg: Howl; America To explore post-war American literature, focusing on themes of disillusionment, social critique, and the human condition. 13 Multi-ethnic Literature Saul Bellow: A Silver Dish Isaac Singer: Gimpel the Fool Vladimir Nabokov: Symbols and Signs Sherman Alexie: This is What it Means to Say Phoenix Arizona To introduce students to the diverse voices in American literature, emphasizing the contributions of writers from various ethnic backgrounds. 14 Presentations 15 Q & A 16 Final Examination 19: 50-21:00 17 Grading Process 18 Submission of Final Grading
