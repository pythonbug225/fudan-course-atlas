课程简介（中文）

公共经济学运用微观经济学的理论和实证分析工具，研究政府政策对经济行为及社会资源配置的影响。本课程首先探讨市场失灵与收入再分配偏好等基础问题，旨在解答：政府应在何时干预经济？政府可通过哪些方式进行干预？这些干预措施对经济运行会产生怎样的效果？课程主题涵盖财政支出相关问题（如教育、社会保险）以及财政收入相关问题（如税收负担、税收效率、个人所得税等）。

课程简介（英文）

Public economics uses the tools of microeconomics and empirical analysis to study the impact of government policies on economic behavior and the distribution of resources in the economy. The course begins with a review of market failures and preferences for income redistribution to answer questions such as: When should the government intervene in the economy? How might the government intervene? And, what are the effects of those interventions on economic outcomes? Topics include issues related to revenue spending (e.g., education, social insurance) and revenue raising (e.g., tax incidence, tax efficiency, personal income taxes).

教学目标

选修本课程的学生需达成以下目标： 1、理解政府干预市场的目标及其核心概念； 2、掌握家庭与企业决策的微观经济学基础； 3、了解经济学家如何运用经济理论、实验室与实地实验及实证研究成果来设计政策； 4、具备评估公共政策的基础分析能力。

教学方式

本课程将以课堂讲授为主，课堂互动提升学习效果。

教学内容与进度安排

Week 1: Introduction to Public Economics Objective: Students will learn: What is public economics, and what are the key questions that the field addresses? What are the key facts about the size and growth of government and the distribution of taxes and spending? How does the debate over health care reform in the United States reflect the key questions of public finance? Week 2: Analytical Tools of Public Economics Students will learn: How do individuals choose how much to consume or how hard to work? How do firms choose how much to produce? What is the theoretical effect of raising cash welfare benefits on economic efficiency? What are “quasi-experiments,” and how can they be used to obtain behavioral estimates? Week 3: Externalities Students will learn: What is an externality, and why does it cause a market failure? When can the private market solve the problem of externalities? What are possible public-sector solutions to the problem of externalities, and what are the advantages and disadvantages of each? Week 4: Public Goods Students will learn: How do we determine the optimal level of public goods? When is the private sector likely to provide the optimal level of public goods? What are the major issues in public provision of public goods? Week 5: Cost-Benefit Analysis Students will learn: How do we appropriately measure the costs and benefits of public projects? What are the best methods for dealing with difficult-to-measure costs and benefits, such as the value of time and of human life? How do we compare costs and benefits to evaluate the optimality of public projects? Week 6: Government Expenditures Students will learn: How does the provision of public goods at the local level affect their optimality? How should different types of spending be shared between different levels of government? What are the impacts of government grants designed to share spending across levels of government? Week 7: Education Students will learn: Why and how should the government be involved in the provision of education? What is the proper role of competition in education markets? What is the current role of the government in the provision of higher education, and should that be changed? Week 8: Social Insurance Students will learn: What market failures justify the provision of public social insurance? Do individuals always need public social insurance to smooth their consumption? What is the moral hazard cost of social insurance, and why is it economically important? Week 9: Health Insurance Students will learn: How generously should insurance cover illness? How generously should insurance reimburse medical providers? How does the Medicaid program impact patient health? How have cost control efforts fared in the Medicare program? Week 10: Unemployment Insurance and Social Security Students will learn: How does Social Security work, and how does it end up redistributing income across society? What effect does Social Security have on consumption smoothing and early retirement? How might we reform Social Security to address its budget shortfall? How do the unemployment insurance, disability insurance, and workers’ compensation programs fit within the general social insurance framework? Week 11: Income Distribution and Welfare Students will learn: How do we measure the income distribution? What factors determine the moral hazard cost of welfare? How can welfare programs be designed to mitigate the moral hazard cost? Week 12: Personal Income Taxes Students will learn: How do income taxes work? What is the proper base for income taxation? What is the appropriate family unit for taxation? Week 13: Tax Incidence Students will learn: What factors determine who bears the burden of taxation? How does considering markets other than the one in which the tax is imposed affect the burden of taxation? What is the distribution of tax burdens in practice? Week 14: Taxes on Labor Supply and Savings Students will learn: How should taxes affect labor supply in theory, and how do they do so in practice? How should taxes affect savings decisions? How does the impact of taxes vary with different underlying models of the savings decision? How do retirement savings incentives affect savings decisions? Week 15: Tax Efficiency Students will learn: What determines the efficiency consequences of taxation? How should governments set taxes across goods? How should governments set taxes on different income groups? Week 16: Corporate Taxes Students will learn: How does the corporate tax work? How does the corporate tax affect incentives for investment and business location? How does the corporate tax affect a firm’s financial decisions such as debt levels and dividend payments? Week 17: Final Exam Objective: Students should take a two-hour closed-book final exam.
