课程简介（中文）

本课程旨在通过对哲学文本的直接研读，提升学生的学术英语水平。每节课都将包含阅读、写作、口语和听力练习，重点在于学习关键的哲学词汇并将其应用于学术语境中。 课程文本主要聚焦于宗教哲学，但也涵盖西方哲学、中国哲学和科学哲学的相关内容，以确保其对不同背景的学生都有价值。 本课程假定学生具备良好的英语基础，因此着重培养学生理解和用英语表达特定哲学思想的能力。尽管如此，课堂教学过程中仍会根据需要纠正学生的语法、句法和发音错误。

课程简介（英文）

This course aims to improve students academic English through direct study of philosophical texts. Each class will include exercises in reading, writing, speaking, and listening, with a special emphasis on learning key philosophical vocabulary and using it in academic contexts. The texts covered mainly focus on philosophy of religion but also includes significant discussions of Western philosophy, Chinese philosophy, and Philosophy of Science to ensure value for a wide range of students. The course assumes a strong working understanding of English as a starting point and thus focusses on developing students' ability to understand and express specifically philosophical ideas in the language. Nevertheless, grammar, syntax and pronunciation will be corrected where appropriate in the course of classroom teaching.

教学目标

By the end of the course, students should be able to: Understand and use key concepts in English pertaining to some of the most significant scholars in the history of philosophy, such as Plato, Thomas Aqinas, and Immanuel Kant. Competently express important Daoist philosophical terms in English and justify those translations. Understand an undergraduate-level philosophy lecture in English. Write a short academic article in English, using appropriate structures, register, and phrasing. Present a short conference paper and respond to unprepared questions in English.

教学方式

The class will be conducted entirely in English, with reference to Chinese only when discussing Chinese philosophical texts. Each class begins with an outline and explanation of key English-language vocabulary for the text under discussion. This is followed by a short introduction to the writer of the text. The main part of each class comprises direct analysis of the text itself. Students are given short sections of the text summarize orally, first in spoken format only, then in both spoken and written format. These excerpts will show the students how the previously given vocabulary is utilised within the text. After the excerpts have been summarized successfully, students are invited to express their opinions about the text and engage in debate around its philosophical argument. Each class ends with a short writing practice in which students demonstrate that they are now able to use the key vocabulary in their own academic writing. After mid-term, student will deliver a short (approximately 10 minute) prepared presentation on a subject of their choice, followed by an unprepared 10 minute Q&A from their classmates. Final assessment will be in the form of one 3,000 word A&HCI-format essay based on one of the texts discussed over the semester, using entirely English-language sources.
