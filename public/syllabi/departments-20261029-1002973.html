课程简介（中文）

经济活动高度集聚于城市。全球56%人口（44亿）聚集在仅占陆地面积1%的城市中，中国亦有超9亿人生活在城市中。城市是增长引擎，孕育商业、文化与创新，但也带来拥堵、高成本与污染。本课程教授城市经济学理论，以此分析中外城市化进程，探讨城市形成机制、空间经济组织，以及要素市场化改革、基建、环境等核心议题，并通过案例与实证分析，培养学生独立分析能力。

课程简介（英文）

Economic activity is highly concentrated in space, giving rise to cities. Today, approximately 56% of the world’s population—4.4 billion people—live in cities, which occupy just 1% of the planet’s land area. Over 900 million people in China live in urban areas. Cities drive growth, offering vibrant opportunities for commerce, culture, and learning—but density also brings congestion, high living costs, and pollution. This course applies urban economics to analyze urbanization in China and globally. Students will examine why cities form, how economic activity is organized spatially, and key urban issues such as labor, land, and housing market reforms, transportation infrastructure, environmental policies, and neighborhood effects. Combining economic theory and empirical analysis, the course draws on case studies from China, the U.S., and beyond. By the end, students will understand core urban theories and evidence, gaining tools to conduct their own research in urban economics and related fields.

教学目标

Today, approximately 56% of the world’s population—4.4 billion people—live in cities, which occupy just 1% of the planet’s land area. This trend is set to continue, with the urban population expected to more than double by 2050, meaning nearly 7 out of 10 people will reside in urban areas. Cities are engines of economic growth, generating over 80% of global GDP, and when managed effectively, urbanization can drive sustainable development through increased productivity and innovation. Cities offer vibrant opportunities—cultural experiences, commerce, and learning—but they also present challenges. High density leads to congestion, rising living and commuting costs, and heightened exposure to pollution and disease. The primary objective of this course is to equip students with the latest theories and research in urban economics, enabling them to analyze key issues related to urbanization in China and other global urban contexts. By the end of the course, students will have a solid grasp of core urban economic theories and empirical findings on critical urban topics. Additionally, they will develop the analytical tools necessary to conduct their own research in urban economics and related fields.

教学方式

Economic activity is highly unevenly distributed across space, as reflected by the existence of cities. In 2020, about 885 million Chinese people crowded together in the urban part, accounting for one percent of the country’s land. Cities have been engines of growth. Cities are also filled with things to see, buy, taste, and learn. However, density also means congestion, makes living and commuting costly, and concentrates exposure to pollution and disease. This course aims to teach students how to apply the latest thinking and research in urban economics to analyze a series of topics related to urbanization in China and other urban-related issues worldwide. Students will learn how economists think about cities. We will explore why cities exist as they do, the natures, determinants, and impacts of geographic organization of economic activities, and a variety of urban policy issues such as the household registration system (hukou), the reforms in land and housing markets, urban land-use policies, transportation infrastructure investment, city and environment, spatial arrangements of educational resources, neighborhood transitions, and place-based policies. Students will learn about urban economic theory and analysis of empirical data sets in the context of addressing important urban-related questions. Empirical examples will be taken primarily from China, the United States, and some European countries. After taking this course, students will comprehensively understand the standard urban theory and empirical evidence on various important applied urban topics. This course will prepare students with many of the necessary tools to undertake their research in urban economics and related fields. To enroll in this course, students must have completed courses that provide a basic understanding of microeconomics and macroeconomics and a basic knowledge of econometric analysis involving cross-sectional and panel data sets. The primary resources for this course will include the provided lecture notes, slides, and recommended readings. Each student will be required to present one paper under the starred topic in the reading list appended below or of his/her choice with approval from the lecturer. Each presentation must include the paper’s questions of interest, motivation, related literature, research method, data, main results, and primary implications (or insights). A final research proposal will be due at the end of the semester. The proposal must be three pages long and written in a Word file (with double space and a font size 12). Each proposal must clearly present the question of interest, discuss the motivation (why the question is worth studying), and elaborate on related literature and the proposal’s additional contributions. Students are expected to discuss at least three strands of related literature, each including at least five papers with decent qualities (e.g., from prestigious academic journals). The instructor will select eight proposals to discuss in the final week. Bonus will be granted to the writers. Students are required to sit for an open-book final paper exam.
